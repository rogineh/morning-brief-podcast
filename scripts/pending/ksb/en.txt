First, national news. The country's big four banks have confirmed they'll pass on this week's Reserve Bank rate rise in full, with Commonwealth Bank, NAB, Westpac, and ANZ all lifting variable home loan rates by a quarter of a percentage point from the ninth of October, while Macquarie Bank will make its own changes to variable loan and deposit rates from the fifteenth. Several smaller mutual banks moved even faster, with Australian Mutual Bank, Teachers Mutual Bank, and UniBank all confirming increases to their variable savings products take effect from today, giving savers a small silver lining even as borrowers brace for higher repayments. Consumer groups say the timing is particularly tough for households heading into the traditional summer spending season, with many mortgage holders now facing their fourth rate rise for the year and little relief in sight before Christmas. Financial counsellors are urging anyone struggling to keep up with repayments to contact their bank early rather than waiting for missed payments to pile up, noting that most lenders have hardship provisions available for customers doing it tough during the transition to higher rates. Comparison sites are also encouraging borrowers to shop around, pointing out that the gap between the most competitive and least competitive variable rates on the market has widened noticeably this year, meaning some households could still save meaningfully by refinancing even as rates rise across the board.

Now to Queensland. Excitement is building across the state with just two weeks to go until the Rugby League World Cup kicks off, with Queensland set to host three of the tournament's eight venues and matches expected to draw around one hundred and ten thousand visitors from interstate and overseas. Suncorp Stadium in Brisbane will host both the men's and women's grand finals along with a blockbuster clash between Australia and Fiji, while Cbus Super Stadium on the Gold Coast will stage a triple-header featuring Samoa against France, New Zealand against Fiji, and New Zealand against Papua New Guinea. Further north, Townsville's Queensland Country Bank Stadium will host the Kangaroos taking on the Cook Islands, giving fans across the state's south-east and far north corners a chance to see international rugby league on home soil. Tourism operators say the tournament, which runs from the fifteenth of October through to mid-November, is expected to deliver a major boost to accommodation, hospitality, and transport businesses across all three host regions during what's traditionally a quieter stretch of the calendar. Local councils have been coordinating with event organisers for months to make sure public transport, road closures, and crowd management are ready to handle the influx, with volunteers also being recruited to help welcome the thousands of interstate and international visitors expected to pass through host cities over the tournament's five-week run.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. New figures confirm the city's economy has grown twenty-five per cent since 2020 to more than fifty-five billion dollars, cementing its status as Australia's largest non-capital city economy and placing it second nationally behind Sydney for economic complexity, a measure of how diverse and sophisticated a local economy has become. The Gold Coast is now home to more than six hundred and ninety thousand people, supports over three hundred and fifty thousand jobs, and counts more than eighty-three thousand local businesses, with manufacturing now accounting for the majority of the region's international export growth as the economy continues to diversify well beyond its traditional tourism base. Council figures say growth sectors including health, education, advanced manufacturing, technology, and professional services are all playing a bigger role in the local economy than ever before, with the city now growing faster than Greater Brisbane and outpacing the average growth rate of Australia's capital cities since 2020. Local business leaders say the figures reflect years of deliberate investment in infrastructure and skills, positioning the Gold Coast to keep attracting major employers and events well beyond its reputation as a holiday destination. The city also generates close to nineteen billion dollars in exports each year, with manufacturing alone now responsible for the majority of that international trade growth, a shift that economists say shows just how far the local economy has moved beyond its traditional reliance on tourism and construction alone.
