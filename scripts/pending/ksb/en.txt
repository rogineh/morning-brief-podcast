Across Australia, researchers led by a team at Curtin University have reported the discovery of what appear to be preserved red blood cells inside a one-hundred-and-fifteen-million-year-old dinosaur bone, in a finding that challenges long-held scientific assumptions about how long delicate biological material can survive the fossilisation process. The fossil belongs to a small theropod dinosaur that lived in what's now north-eastern Brazil, and the research team identified heme, the iron-containing molecule responsible for carrying oxygen in blood, along with other hemoglobin-derived compounds and preserved collagen. Scientists believe a mineral called hausmannite, potentially combined with microbial mats present at the time the animal died, may have helped keep these delicate cellular structures intact for such an extraordinary length of time. The discovery pushes back the oldest confirmed evidence of this kind of preserved dinosaur blood material by several tens of millions of years compared to previous finds, which had only reached back around eighty million years at most. Researchers studying the fossil say the findings, published in a leading scientific journal, could prompt palaeontologists to search more deliberately for similar molecular evidence in other ancient fossils that might previously have been overlooked using less sensitive detection methods. Earlier claims of preserved dinosaur blood components from other fossils had generally been treated with caution by the broader scientific community, given how difficult it is to rule out contamination or misleading signals from the surrounding rock. The research team behind this latest finding says independent verification from other laboratories will be an important next step in confirming just how significant the discovery ultimately proves to be.

In Queensland, construction has officially begun on a major new wind and battery storage project in the state's north, after the one-point-seven-billion-dollar development reached financial close with backing from a group of ten banks. The project, located south-west of Ingham, will eventually combine more than four hundred megawatts of wind generation capacity across sixty-eight turbines with a substantial grid-forming battery system, making it one of the largest wind energy developments to reach this stage of development along the entire east coast. The site has already secured long-term offtake agreements with two energy retailers, and developers expect the project to deliver more than two hundred million dollars in broader economic benefits to the surrounding region, along with as many as five hundred direct and indirect jobs during the construction phase. A major construction contractor has been engaged to deliver the build, describing it as the largest Queensland project the company has undertaken to date, with full operations targeted within the next several years. The project forms part of a broader pipeline of large-scale renewable energy developments progressing across regional Queensland, as the state continues working to expand its electricity generation capacity to meet growing demand.

And on the Gold Coast, two local residents have been recognised with Queensland Australian of the Year nominations for their community contributions, including decades of volunteering and dedicated work supporting young people affected by domestic and family violence. A seventy-six-year-old Gold Coast woman has been nominated in the Senior category after more than fifty years of continuous volunteering work across the local community, a remarkable record of sustained civic contribution spanning more than half a century. In the Young category, a nineteen-year-old Gold Coast woman has been named a finalist for founding a not-for-profit initiative specifically focused on improving support services for young people impacted by domestic and family violence in their own homes. Both women were first recognised at the city's own Australian of the Year awards earlier this year, before being put forward for consideration at the state level alongside nominees from right across Queensland. The nominations highlight the significant, often under-recognised community work being carried out by Gold Coast residents of vastly different generations, with the state-level finalists set to be formally announced in Brisbane later this month.
