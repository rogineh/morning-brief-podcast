Turning to national news, Australian citrus growers have secured improved access to the South Korean market, after authorities there finalised revised import requirements that remove a longstanding administrative hurdle for exporters. Under the previous arrangement, Korean quarantine officers were required to travel to Australia to carry out pre-clearance inspections before fruit could be exported, a process that added significant cost and complexity, particularly for growers located further from major ports. With that requirement now lifted for registered farms and packing facilities, industry representatives say a wider range of Australian citrus producers will be able to access the lucrative Korean market without the logistical burden that previously made exporting there impractical for many smaller operations. Australian citrus exports to Korea have grown substantially in recent years, and growers say removing this bottleneck should accelerate that trend further, particularly for lemons, which have seen some of the strongest export growth of any citrus variety heading to Korea. Trade officials say the change reflects years of behind-the-scenes negotiation between Australian and Korean agricultural authorities, and that similar streamlining efforts remain ongoing across a range of other agricultural export markets. Grower groups have welcomed the news, saying every reduction in export friction matters for an industry that depends heavily on access to diverse overseas markets to remain commercially viable.

In Queensland, construction on the new Bundaberg Hospital has moved into its main building phase, with piling works now underway at the site following the recent signing of an improved contract with the builder responsible for delivering the project. The one-point-two-billion-dollar hospital, being delivered under the state's broader hospital rescue plan, will add more than two hundred new beds and treatment areas once complete, representing a substantial increase in capacity for a region that's seen significant population growth in recent years. The project has moved through a staged delivery process, with early site works completed before the contractor locked in a guaranteed price for the main construction phase, a model authorities say is designed to avoid the kind of cost blowouts and delays that have plagued other major hospital projects in the past. Health officials say the new facility will significantly ease pressure on the existing Bundaberg Hospital, which has long struggled to keep pace with demand from a growing and ageing regional population across the Wide Bay area. Local residents and healthcare workers who've watched the project progress through its early stages say seeing piling work finally get underway marks a tangible, visible sign that the long-promised new hospital is genuinely moving forward rather than remaining stuck in the planning and design phase.

The redevelopment included substantial upgrades to the venue's dining and bar areas, aimed at bringing the hotel's offering in line with the more modern, design-focused venues that have increasingly become the norm across the Gold Coast's hospitality scene in recent years. Hotel operators say the investment reflects continued confidence in Burleigh Heads as a dining and entertainment destination, with the suburb having built a strong reputation in recent years for its mix of beachside atmosphere and high-quality food and drink offerings. Local business groups say venue upgrades like this one help keep the broader Burleigh precinct competitive as a destination, particularly as nearby suburbs also continue investing in new and refreshed hospitality venues to capture visitor and local spending alike. Regular patrons who've followed the renovation say they're looking forward to seeing how the refreshed venue performs over the upcoming summer season, traditionally one of the busiest periods of the year for hospitality venues across the Gold Coast.
