Turning to national news, the federal government has stepped back from an earlier plan to significantly overhaul the working holiday visa program, following sustained criticism from regional businesses and farmers who warned the changes would make it even harder to find seasonal workers. The original proposal would have sharply cut the number of backpackers eligible to extend their stay for a second year, but after pushback from regional tourism and agricultural groups, the government has settled on a more modest set of changes instead. A cap on second-year working holiday visas remains in place, with eligible applicants entering a ballot system rather than simply applying on a first-come, first-served basis. The government is also lifting the amount of specified regional work backpackers must complete to qualify for that second-year visa, moving from three months to four months of eligible work, a change industry groups say reflects just how reliant regional economies have become on this pool of temporary workers. Groups representing regional tourism operators say they're relieved the government pulled back from its original, more drastic plan, though some still argue that any reduction in working holiday numbers amounts to a cut regional businesses can ill afford heading into the busy summer season. The debate highlights a broader tension in migration policy between managing overall visitor and worker numbers at a national level and ensuring regional and rural employers, who often struggle most to fill seasonal roles, aren't disproportionately affected by changes designed with the wider system in mind.

In Queensland, the state's Electoral Commission has confirmed a mayoral by-election will be held for Redland City Council on Saturday the twelfth of December, following the resignation of the council's previous mayor earlier this year. The council has been operating without an elected mayor for an extended stretch in the meantime, with a deputy or acting mayor handling civic duties in the interim. Running a city-wide by-election of this kind is a costly exercise, with estimates putting the cost to ratepayers somewhere in the vicinity of six hundred and fifty to eight hundred and fifty thousand dollars once all the logistics of staffing polling booths and distributing postal votes are factored in. Local residents and candidates are now gearing up for a compressed campaign period ahead of the December vote, with several prospective candidates expected to put their names forward in the coming weeks. Council watchers say the result will be closely followed, given how significant a stable, permanently elected mayor is to providing clear direction for the council's priorities heading into next year's broader budget and planning cycles.

The Gold Coast's mayor thanked Mister Baker for his leadership of one of the country's largest local governments through a period of substantial change for the city, including major growth in population and a heavy program of infrastructure delivery tied to the lead-up to the twenty thirty-two Olympic and Paralympic Games. Council insiders say the search for a new chief executive is likely to attract strong interest nationally, given the scale and profile of the role at a time when the city is managing some of its biggest and most complex projects in its history.
