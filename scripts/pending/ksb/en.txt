Good day, and welcome to your Morning Brief for Saturday, the 3rd of October, 2026. Here's what's happening today across Australia, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

First, national news. Applications remain open until the sixth of October for the latest round of the federal government's Remote Airstrip Upgrade Program, which is offering twenty-five million dollars in competitive grants for safety and accessibility works at remote and very remote airstrips across the country. The funding round comes as the Australian Airports Association renews its push for a permanent, ongoing funding mechanism for regional and remote airports rather than relying on periodic grant rounds, pointing to a recent survey showing nearly three-quarters of the regional airports surveyed reported a financial loss over the last financial year. More than one hundred and forty million dollars has already been committed through eleven previous rounds of the program, but industry figures argue that many of these airports are effectively being propped up by local councils and ratepayers because the air links they provide remain too important for isolated communities to lose. Advocates say a longer-term funding commitment would give councils and operators more certainty to plan major infrastructure works, rather than scrambling to meet the requirements of each new grant round as it opens and closes. Many of the communities relying on these airstrips have no other reliable transport link, particularly during the wet season when roads can become impassable, making continued federal investment a matter of basic access to healthcare, schooling, and supplies rather than simply a convenience for occasional travellers.

Now to Queensland. The Australian Camp Oven Festival is underway this weekend in the small Darling Downs town of Millmerran, drawing visitors from across the state and beyond for two days of cast-iron cooking, campfire smoke, and old-fashioned country hospitality. The festival, which runs through Sunday, has become something of an institution for lovers of traditional bush cooking, with competitors taking their damper and camp oven creations extremely seriously as they vie for bragging rights in front of judges and curious onlookers. Organisers say the long weekend timing makes Millmerran an easy regional escape for visitors looking to combine a relaxed country atmosphere with a genuinely unique slice of Australian food culture, and with pleasant spring weather forecast across the region this weekend, turnout is expected to be strong. Local businesses in the town say the festival provides a welcome injection of visitor spending at a time of year when many regional centres are looking for ways to draw people away from the bigger coastal destinations. Volunteers from the local community have spent weeks preparing the showgrounds for the influx of visitors, with camping sites, food stalls, and live entertainment all part of the weekend program alongside the main cooking competitions.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. A local high school is turning heads for an innovative approach to both food security and classroom engagement, with Pacific Pines State High School using an on-site greenhouse to grow lettuce, herbs, and vegetables while teaching maths, science, and technology in the process. The school is one of nine across south-east Queensland taking part in the not-for-profit Food Ladder's greenhouse program, which now operates sixty-eight greenhouses in schools around the country, with students learning everything from water temperature and growing conditions to the technology that keeps the greenhouse environment stable. Food Ladder's chief executive says produce grown on site can be taken home by students or used in cooking classes, with geography and business classes also getting involved in exploring how the produce might eventually be grown and sold commercially. Applications are now open for another five south-east Queensland schools to join the program, with early results suggesting the hands-on, practical approach is helping lift both student attendance and engagement well beyond what a standard classroom lesson can achieve. Teachers involved in the program say students who otherwise struggle to engage with traditional lessons often thrive in the greenhouse setting, where lessons on biology, chemistry, and basic engineering become immediately tangible through the simple act of caring for a living plant.
