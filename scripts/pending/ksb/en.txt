Good day, and welcome to your Morning Brief for Tuesday, the 29th of September, 2026. Here's what's happening today across Australia, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

First, national news. Treasurer Jim Chalmers has revealed the federal budget's final position for the last financial year came in six billion dollars better than expected, with the underlying cash deficit landing at twenty-two point three billion dollars rather than the twenty-eight point three billion forecast back in May. The improvement was driven largely by stronger-than-expected superannuation and investor income, alongside slightly lower government spending, rather than any boost from commodity prices or wage tax receipts, which actually came in under earlier projections. Despite the brighter budget numbers, the Treasurer struck a cautious tone, warning of difficult months ahead as the Reserve Bank prepares to hand down its own interest rate decision this afternoon, with financial markets and all four major banks now expecting a quarter of a percentage point increase that would take the cash rate to its highest level since 2011. Chalmers said the government remained focused on easing cost-of-living pressure where it could, even as higher borrowing costs loom for millions of mortgage holders across the country in the weeks ahead. Opposition figures have argued the improved bottom line reflects little more than a lucky break on investment returns rather than genuine budget discipline, while independent economists note that the structural pressures on the budget, including rising health and aged care costs, remain largely unchanged despite today's more favourable headline number.

Now to Queensland. The state government's Hospital Rescue Plan is continuing to take shape, with Premier David Crisafulli confirming a major expansion of Coomera Hospital that will deliver two hundred additional beds to one of the fastest-growing parts of south-east Queensland. The upgrade forms part of a broader statewide push to deliver more than two thousand six hundred extra hospital beds, three brand-new hospitals, and major expansions at ten existing facilities across the state, in what officials are calling the largest hospital infrastructure investment in Queensland's history. Health authorities say the Coomera project in particular reflects the sheer scale of population growth in the northern Gold Coast corridor, where existing services have struggled to keep pace with demand from new housing estates springing up across the region. Local residents have welcomed the announcement, with many saying additional beds and services closer to home will ease pressure on nearby hospitals that have increasingly had to absorb overflow from the rapidly expanding suburbs nearby. Health officials have not yet released a firm construction timeline for the Coomera works, but say detailed planning is already underway, with more specifics on staging and expected completion dates promised in the coming months as the broader statewide rescue plan continues to roll out.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. Transport remains a hot topic on the Gold Coast after the state government confirmed new funding through the Gold Coast Transport Plan to make up for the cancellation of stage four of the light rail, a thirteen-kilometre tram extension from Burleigh Heads to Coolangatta that had blown out to as much as seven point six billion dollars in cost estimates. In its place, commuters will get an extension of the light rail from Harbour Town in Biggera Waters through to the Gold Coast University Hospital, along with a new high-frequency bus service dubbed the GC Surfer, running every ten minutes between Robina and Coolangatta via Varsity Lakes, Burleigh Heads, and the airport. Transport planners say the combination is designed to give residents and visitors fast, reliable options along the coastal strip without the enormous price tag and years of disruption a full light rail extension would have required. Local business groups say certainty on the transport question, after years of back and forth over stage four, will make it easier to plan for future growth in the southern Gold Coast, even if some had hoped the original tram line would eventually go ahead. Construction on the Harbour Town to hospital light rail extension is expected to be staged over several years, with the state government saying more detail on timing and construction impacts will be released once final design and procurement work is further advanced.
