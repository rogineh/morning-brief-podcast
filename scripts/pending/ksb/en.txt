A new federal election funding system is set to take effect from the first of January 2027, bringing in lower donation disclosure thresholds and new caps on political gifts ahead of the next federal election. Under the changes, the threshold for publicly disclosing a donation drops sharply from seventeen thousand three hundred dollars down to just five thousand dollars, with donations made during an actual election period required to be reported within twenty four hours rather than the lengthy delays that have applied in the past. New annual gift caps will also apply, starting at fifty thousand dollars per recipient from any single donor, alongside broader state and territory caps and an overall cap across the country, all aimed at limiting how much influence any one wealthy donor or organisation can exert over a single party or candidate. Public election funding is also set to increase under the new system, with the amount paid per vote rising to help offset parties' reduced reliance on large private donations. The Australian Electoral Commission has described getting the complex new reporting and compliance systems ready in time as no small achievement, after an earlier planned start date had to be pushed back due to the scale of the technical work involved. Some commentators have welcomed the changes as a genuine step toward greater transparency, while others argue the caps remain generous enough that well-resourced donors and parties will still find ways to maximise their influence within the new rules.

In Queensland, local members of parliament have moved to reassure workers at the Stanwell Power Station near Rockhampton that their jobs remain secure under the state's long-term energy transition roadmap. The plant is set to continue operating as a coal-fired facility for some years yet before eventually transforming into what the government has branded a clean energy hub by the early 2030s, with existing energy workers covered by a legislated job security guarantee throughout that transition period. A major battery storage project already under construction at the site has recently been expanded, with state government investment increasing to help double the size of the planned battery and create dozens of additional full-time construction roles in the process. Stanwell's current round of apprenticeships is also continuing to bring in future mechanical fitters, electricians, boilermakers, and other trades, alongside administrative and supply chain roles, as the workforce gradually adapts to the site's longer-term direction. Local representatives say the message to the community is one of stability and genuine opportunity through the transition, rather than abrupt job losses, even as the broader shift away from traditional coal-fired generation continues to reshape the regional economy over coming years.

And on the Gold Coast, residents are being urged to stay alert after another round of severe thunderstorm warnings swept through the region, with authorities flagging the risk of damaging winds, large hail, and heavy rainfall across Logan, the Scenic Rim, and the Gold Coast itself. Earlier warnings issued for the area were briefly cancelled as the worst of one system passed through, only for forecasters to flag the possibility of further dangerous storms developing later the same day, keeping emergency services on alert for a prolonged stretch. The unsettled pattern follows what's already shaping up as one of the wettest Octobers the region has seen in recent years, with saturated ground and already swollen waterways meaning even relatively modest additional rainfall now carries a higher risk of localised flash flooding than it otherwise might. Authorities are reminding residents to secure loose outdoor items ahead of any fresh severe weather, avoid sheltering under trees during thunderstorm activity, and keep a close eye on official Bureau of Meteorology warnings as the unsettled conditions continue to move through the area in bursts. Emergency crews say they remain ready to respond quickly if further storm damage or flooding call-outs eventuate over the coming days.
