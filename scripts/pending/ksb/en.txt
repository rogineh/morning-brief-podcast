In national news, fresh scrutiny has emerged over almost seven hundred thousand dollars in public money being used to send delegates to the major United Nations climate conference now underway in the Turkish city of Antalya. Climate Change and Energy Minister Chris Bowen's department confirmed that thirty-four successful applicants, drawn from a pool of more than one hundred and sixty hopefuls, will share the funding to travel to the conference between today and the twentieth of November. The travel support program was designed to help diverse and underrepresented groups take part, with its stated goals including boosting the voices of First Nations Australians and young people at the talks, and recipients reportedly include farming and industry bodies alongside community organisations. The spending has drawn criticism from opposition figures, who have characterised it as a questionable use of taxpayer funds at a time of cost-of-living pressure, with one backbencher describing the outlay as money spent on "junkets" rather than genuine policy outcomes. The criticism follows separate revelations at a Senate estimates hearing this week that an earlier preparatory climate event hosted in Fiji ballooned dramatically over its original budget, adding further pressure on the minister's department to justify its spending as the Antalya talks stretch on for almost two weeks. The department has defended the travel support program as a modest and worthwhile investment compared with the overall scale of the conference, arguing that giving smaller community and industry groups a seat at the table produces better long-term policy outcomes than leaving international negotiations solely to large government delegations.

In Queensland, excitement is building ahead of next week's Australian of the Year awards ceremony, with this year's crop of state nominees now locked in. Wildlife campaigner and television personality Robert Irwin headlines the list of candidates for the main Australian of the Year category, alongside child safety advocates Bruce and Denise Morcombe, along with fellow nominees Beny Bol and Carolyn Robinson, each recognised for their own contributions to the community. Sprinting sensation Gout Gout, who has drawn national attention for his performances on the track this year, is among those shortlisted in the Young Australian of the Year category for the state. The formal ceremony recognising Queensland's selected recipients across all award categories is scheduled to take place in Brisbane on the sixteenth of November, with those chosen then progressing to represent the state at the national awards in Canberra early next year. Organisers say the breadth of nominees this year, spanning wildlife conservation, child safety campaigning, and elite sport, reflects the diversity of contributions being made across Queensland communities. Nominations across the senior category have also drawn attention this year, with three women recognised for decades of volunteer and advocacy work in their local communities, underscoring that the awards remain focused on grassroots contribution as much as on public profile.

And on the Gold Coast, the lawn bowls competition at the Pan Pacific Masters Games has been in full swing for days now, with play continuing at Broadbeach Bowls Club as part of the ten-day, multi-sport festival drawing more than sixteen thousand competitors to the city. The bowls program has been running daily since last Friday, with matches typically starting at nine in the morning and continuing through until five o'clock, before organisers extend the hours on the final two days of competition later this week to accommodate finals play. Like the dozens of other sports being staged across the city this week, the bowls competition forms part of the broader masters games structure, which allows competitors typically aged thirty and above to take part in genuinely organised, officiated sport divided into age-based categories. Local club officials say the steady stream of visiting bowlers has been a welcome boost for Broadbeach throughout the week, with many competitors making a point of exploring nearby cafes and shops between matches, several of whom have travelled from interstate specifically to take part in the age-graded competition. With competition continuing citywide through to a closing ceremony scheduled for the fourteenth of this month, organisers say strong turnout at venues like Broadbeach Bowls Club underscores just how much the games have grown in scale this year.
