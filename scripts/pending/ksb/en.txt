Workers covered by the new agreements will vote on a deal that includes an eight and a half per cent pay rise spread over three years, with a further top-up tied to inflation, and the network is expected to return to a full, normal timetable through a staged rollout beginning next month, with services fully restored by the end of November. Commuters who've dealt with months of unpredictable services will be watching closely to see whether the staged return holds to schedule, with transport authorities saying they're confident the worst of the disruption is now behind the network. Separately, the state's peak transport bodies say freight movements have also been affected by the dispute, and are hopeful that a return to normal rail operations will help ease bottlenecks that built up in supply chains over the winter months. Union representatives have described the outcome as a fair result for workers who kept essential services running through a difficult stretch, while acknowledging the disruption caused real frustration for everyday passengers who had little say in the underlying negotiations.

Council says the investment reflects growing demand from a fast-expanding population and is designed to make sure local sporting clubs, schools, and community groups have access to modern, well-maintained facilities in the years ahead, with several projects also positioned to double as training and support venues in the lead-up to the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Local sporting groups have welcomed the announcement, saying the upgrades are long overdue given how much participation in community sport has grown across the city over the past several years. Council representatives say further details on the rollout timeline for individual projects will be released in the coming months, with some of the smaller community facility upgrades expected to begin as early as this summer.
