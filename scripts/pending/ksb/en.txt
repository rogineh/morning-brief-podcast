First, national news. Major changes to the National Disability Insurance Scheme take effect from tomorrow, with funding for some supports being reset as part of the government's broader push to secure the scheme's long-term future. The changes will affect budget allocations within participant plans, particularly around supports for social, economic, and community participation, as well as funding for improved daily living skills, following legislation passed by federal parliament in August. The reforms are being rolled out gradually over the coming years to give participants, families, and service providers time to adjust, but disability advocates say the changes to how funding is structured and used could still cause confusion for many people during the transition period. The government has defended the overhaul as necessary to keep the scheme sustainable for future generations, arguing that without changes to eligibility, plan management, and provider operations, the scheme's growing cost would become increasingly difficult to fund. Advocacy groups have welcomed the extended timeline for implementation but say ongoing consultation with the disability community will be essential to make sure the changes achieve their intended savings without reducing the quality of support people actually receive. Families with existing plans have been encouraged to speak with their planners well ahead of any review date to understand exactly how the new arrangements might affect their individual funding, with the government promising additional support resources will be made available as the rollout continues.

Now to Queensland. Police are appealing for public assistance after a spate of attempted break-ins across Everton Park in Brisbane's north overnight, with investigators saying a property on Fallon Street was broken into in the early hours of the morning, with several items and a grey Toyota Hilux stolen from the address. The stolen vehicle was then allegedly used by two unknown offenders in a series of further attempted break-ins across the surrounding area, taking in streets including Flockton Street, Trouts Road, Stafford Road, South Pine Road, and Old Northern Road. Detectives are asking anyone who was in the area between midnight and two in the morning, or who may have dashcam or security footage from the time, to come forward, and are urging residents across the broader Everton Park area to check their properties for any signs of attempted entry. Anyone with information has been asked to contact Policelink or report anonymously through Crime Stoppers, with police stressing that even small details could help piece together the movements of those responsible. Local residents have been rattled by the spate of incidents, with several taking to community social media pages overnight to share footage and warn neighbours to be vigilant, while police say patrols in the area have already been stepped up as the investigation continues.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. The city has welcomed its newest young civic leaders, with Olivia Voight from Saint Stephen's College in Upper Coomera named the thirty-fourth Junior Mayor of the Gold Coast, and Sejal Verma from King's Christian College in Pimpama announced as Junior Deputy Mayor. The pair will lead the city's Junior Council, a program bringing together one hundred and fifty students from years ten and eleven across forty Gold Coast high schools to give young people a genuine voice in local government and a hands-on introduction to civic leadership. Program organisers say the initiative has become an important pipeline for developing future community leaders, with past participants going on to careers in law, policy, and public service after their time on the Junior Council. Both incoming leaders said they were looking forward to representing the views of young people on issues affecting the city, with council officials adding that the program continues to grow in popularity each year as more schools nominate students keen to get involved in shaping their community's future. Over the coming twelve months, the Junior Council will meet regularly to workshop ideas on issues ranging from youth mental health to environmental sustainability, with recommendations from the group set to be presented directly to the city's elected councillors for consideration.
