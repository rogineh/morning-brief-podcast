In national news, new protections for aged care residents have come into effect this month under the Aged Care Act, extending safeguards to people who were already living in care before the new framework commenced last November, with the changes designed to make sure existing residents aren't left worse off as the broader system transitions to updated rules. The move comes alongside fresh federal funding announcements, including new capital subsidy payments for residential aged care providers aimed at supporting the construction of additional beds, part of a package first flagged in this year's federal budget to address an ongoing shortage of residential places across the country. Industry analysts have pointed out that growth in the accommodation supplement paid to providers has lagged well behind both construction costs and house prices over the past decade, with one recent sector report noting the supplement has risen by little more than a quarter over that period, even as building costs and property values have climbed far more steeply. That mismatch has fed into broader concerns about the pace of new bed supply, with only a modest number of net new residential places added to the system over the past five years despite a growing older population. The government maintains its reforms are intended to put the sector on a more sustainable footing over time, even as providers continue to warn that funding settings haven't yet caught up with the actual cost of delivering care, particularly in regional and remote parts of the country where building a single new facility can run into the tens of millions of dollars.

In Queensland, exporters from across the state are gearing up for next week's Australian Export Awards in Canberra, after a strong showing at the Premier of Queensland's Export Awards held in Brisbane last month. Cook Medical took out the top honour as Queensland's overall Exporter of the Year, with category wins also going to Green Skin Avocados in agribusiness, Gameloft Brisbane in creative and lifestyle design, Emesent in digital and smart technologies, and a Central Queensland Wagyu supply chain built around King River recognised as the state's top regional exporter. Those winners now go on to represent Queensland at the national awards, competing against category winners from every other state and territory for recognition as Australia's best exporters across industries ranging from agriculture and manufacturing through to digital services. Organisers say this year's Queensland field was particularly strong across agribusiness and advanced manufacturing, reflecting a broader push by the state to diversify its export base beyond traditional resources and into higher-value manufactured goods and digital products. The Queensland winners will be hoping to add a national trophy to their state honours when the Canberra ceremony is held later this coming week, capping off what's been described as a standout year for the state's export sector.

And on the Gold Coast, work is continuing on the airport's long-term master plan, with construction crews progressing through various stages of a broader transformation approved under a twenty-year development framework for the site. The plan, given federal sign-off last year, sets out a path toward a significantly expanded Gold Coast Airport in time for the 2032 Brisbane Olympics, with approved works including a new public transport plaza, additional hotel accommodation on site, and an expansion of Southern Cross University's campus presence at the airport precinct. The airport has already completed several major stages of redevelopment in recent years, including an international terminal expansion and a subsequent domestic departure lounge upgrade, and officials say the latest phase of works builds directly on that momentum. Airport management has described the broader master plan as a generational opportunity to reposition Gold Coast Airport as a genuine gateway for the growing region, with passenger numbers through the terminal continuing to climb as the Gold Coast's population and visitor economy both keep expanding. Local business groups have welcomed the ongoing investment, saying a modernised airport will be essential infrastructure for the city as it prepares to host Olympic events and welcome international visitors in the years ahead.
