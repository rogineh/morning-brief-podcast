Starting with national news, coffee drinkers are feeling a pinch at the counter, with a new price tracker finding that around one in six cafes surveyed have lifted the price of a small coffee since the ban on card surcharges took effect. Among those that did raise prices, the typical increase was about fifty cents, which works out to close to ten per cent on a five-dollar coffee, well above the small surcharge most customers used to pay. Cafe owners say the change hasn't removed the bank fees they're charged for card payments, it's simply stopped them listing those fees separately, so many are folding the cost back into their prices instead. For what it's worth, most cafes surveyed didn't change their prices at all, so the increases appear to be concentrated among a smaller group of operators feeling the squeeze most acutely.

Elsewhere, authorities are continuing a national crackdown on illegal tobacco shops, with Victorian police closing sixty-one stores linked to organised crime over the past month alone. Officers have seized roughly two million cigarettes, tens of thousands of vapes, and more than half a million dollars in cash as part of the operation, aided by new powers that let authorities issue rapid closure notices to any store caught selling illicit tobacco. Similar operations have also been running in South Australia and New South Wales in recent months, as states lean more heavily on tougher licensing and closure powers to disrupt the illicit trade.

Turning now to Queensland, the crackdown has had a dramatic flow-on effect here too. A ten-day blitz on illegal tobacco retailers across the state forced the closure of around a hundred and forty-eight stores, and figures released afterward show legal tobacco sales in Queensland jumped by fifty-five per cent in the wake of the operation. Authorities say the numbers suggest a significant share of the market had shifted to the black market before the crackdown, and they're treating the sales rebound as an early sign the enforcement push is working, though they acknowledge ongoing operations will be needed to stop illegal operators simply reopening elsewhere.

Closer to home, Logan City Council has voted to halve its infrastructure charge on new one-bedroom granny flats, cutting the cost of building one by around thirteen thousand five hundred dollars. The change, due to take effect in November, passed by a vote of eight councillors to three, with supporters arguing it will boost the supply of affordable rental housing in the region. Critics, including warnings raised by the mayor during debate, cautioned that cheaper backyard dwellings could end up being used to cram more renters into smaller blocks of land, with some councillors describing the risk in blunt terms as the potential for backyard slums.

New figures from Tourism Research Australia show the Gold Coast welcomed a record fourteen point seven million visitors in the year to March, cementing the city's position as one of the country's most popular domestic and international destinations. International visitor numbers were a particular highlight, with operators pointing to improved air connections as a key driver behind the growth.

The line-up for this year's festival features some of the biggest names in Australian country music, and organisers say they're expecting another strong turnout as the event continues to grow in popularity each year, drawing visitors from across the country to enjoy live music right on the sand.
