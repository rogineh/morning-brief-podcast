In national news, fresh figures show the national bulk billing rate climbing to just over eighty-four per cent in the most recent quarter, up nearly five percentage points on the same time last year, as the effects of a major Medicare incentive scheme continue flowing through the system roughly a year after it took effect. The scheme, which tripled bulk billing incentive payments for a wide range of consultations, has been credited with lifting the share of fully bulk-billing general practices from around one in five at the start of last year to more than two in five now. Despite that improvement, a separate independent analysis has found that out-of-pocket costs for patients who aren't bulk billed have actually risen in the vast majority of federal electorates over the same period, suggesting the system may be splitting increasingly clearly into those who can access free consultations and those facing higher gap payments elsewhere. Regional variation remains stark, with some parts of the country recording universal bulk billing rates above ninety per cent while others sit closer to half that level, a gap health groups say reflects longstanding differences in GP availability and local competition for patients. The federal government has set a target of lifting the national bulk billing rate to ninety per cent by the end of the decade, and today's figures will likely be cited by both supporters and critics of the scheme as evidence for their respective positions on how well it's actually working.

In Queensland, the state's nominees for next year's Australian of the Year awards were unveiled yesterday in Brisbane, with wildlife conservationist and television personality Robert Irwin among those put forward in the main category. Also nominated were Bruce and Denise Morcombe, the Sunshine Coast couple whose decades of advocacy following the disappearance of their son Daniel helped reshape child safety awareness across the country, and community leader Beny Bol, who arrived in Australia on a humanitarian visa and has since become a prominent voice for multicultural youth engagement. In the Young Australian of the Year category, Queensland's nomination went to sprinter Gout Gout, whose rapid rise on the track over the past year has made him one of the most closely watched young athletes in the country. Nominees in the Senior Australian of the Year category included Carmel Crouch and Joan O'Keefe, both recognised for decades of community service and advocacy work across different parts of the state. All of the Queensland nominees now progress to the national finalist stage, where they'll compete against nominees from every other state and territory, with the overall national winners to be announced in Canberra at the end of January. Organisers say this year's Queensland field reflects an unusually broad mix of sporting achievement, community advocacy, and multicultural leadership, underlining the diversity of contributions being recognised across the state.

And on the Gold Coast, construction continues progressing on the new Coomera Hospital, a roughly two-point-two-billion-dollar project that will eventually deliver around six hundred beds to one of the fastest-growing corridors of the city. After a period of delay, work on the project resumed with a major contractor locked in to deliver the build, and crews have since been working through the substructure stages for the hospital's main buildings as the project moves toward its above-ground construction phases. The facility is intended to ease pressure on the existing Gold Coast University Hospital, which has faced growing demand in recent years as the northern Gold Coast and neighbouring parts of the city continue to be among the fastest-growing population areas in the state. Health authorities say a hospital of this scale will be essential infrastructure for a part of the city that's expected to keep growing rapidly over the coming decade, with housing development continuing at pace across the surrounding Coomera and Pimpama areas. While a firm opening date hasn't yet been locked in, officials say the project remains a priority piece of health infrastructure for the broader region, with updates on construction milestones expected to continue being released as the build progresses through its coming stages.
