Federal MPs and senators are expected to be offered portable duress alarms as part of a broader security upgrade, after the Prime Minister told his party's caucus the government would speed up an existing review into politicians' safety following last week's serious attack on independent MP Helen Haines. At least nine members of parliament will have security upgrades to their electorate offices accelerated, with additional security measures also being stepped up in the public areas of Parliament House itself. The Australian Federal Police Commissioner has noted that while duress alarms are a genuinely useful tool, a direct call to emergency services would still trigger the fastest possible police response in a serious incident. The government's broader review is being fast-tracked, with safety and security recommendations now a particular area of focus following the incident, though some politicians have separately warned that physical security measures alone won't be enough to address the volume of threats and abuse directed at people in public life, particularly women. Overseas precedents suggest alarms alone aren't a complete solution either, with similar systems issued to politicians in other countries occasionally found to have technical faults including unreliable batteries and location tracking. Officials say regular testing and maintenance of any new devices will be essential to avoid Australia running into the same reliability problems experienced elsewhere.

In Queensland, long-serving federal MP Michelle Landry has announced she'll retire from politics at the next election, opening the way for Nationals leader Matt Canavan to potentially contest her seat of Capricornia from the lower house instead of the Senate. Landry, who has held the central Queensland seat since 2013, said the decision to step down was entirely her own, citing the heavy travel demands of the role after more than a decade representing the electorate. Canavan currently sits in the Senate but holds a relatively precarious position on his party's ticket for the next election, making a move to the safer lower house seat an appealing option given he already lives locally in Yeppoon. The shift isn't entirely uncontested, with One Nation's James Ashby confirming he's considering his own run for the seat, and that party reportedly looking to establish a local office in the area ahead of the contest. Landry's retirement adds Capricornia to a growing list of seats expected to see genuinely competitive three-way contests at the next election, reflecting broader shifts in how regional Queensland voters have been splitting their support across the major and minor parties in recent years. Local political observers say Landry's departure also removes one of the more experienced regional voices from federal parliament, with whoever ultimately wins the seat facing a steep learning curve regardless of which party they represent.

And on the Gold Coast, the city council says it will formally lobby the state government to review the penalties local councils can issue against developers responsible for polluting waterways, after a string of incidents involving discoloured groundwater discharged into canals at Palm Beach. A council investigation found groundwater pumped from two separate construction sites on Sixth Avenue breached water quality standards, with the contractor involved ultimately issued three separate fines totalling more than fifty thousand dollars. One local councillor has argued the current penalty framework simply isn't tough enough to properly deter this kind of pollution, noting that the full environmental impact of the discharges is still being investigated by council officers. It's not the first time waterway contamination linked to construction activity has drawn scrutiny on the Gold Coast, with a separate canal discolouration incident earlier this year resulting in a smaller fine against a different building company for similar dewatering-related pollution. Residents in the affected canal-front areas say they still haven't received a full, clear explanation of exactly what caused the discolouration in their waterways, adding to local frustration as the council pushes ahead with its case for tougher state-level penalties. The council says it will provide further updates to affected residents once its own environmental assessment of the Palm Beach incidents is complete.
