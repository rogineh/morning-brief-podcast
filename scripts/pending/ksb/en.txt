Independent federal MP Helen Haines remains in a critical condition in a Melbourne hospital, more than two days after she was seriously injured in an alleged targeted attack outside her electorate office in Wangaratta. Her husband, Phil, has said her recovery will be a long one and has thanked hospital staff and medical teams for their care, while bouquets and messages of support have continued to pile up outside her office in Victoria's north-east. The Prime Minister has described the incident as a real concern, and police leaders have said the attack has highlighted broader worries about threats and violence directed at elected officials, with a safety briefing for state politicians held in response earlier this week. The man charged over the alleged attack has already appeared in court facing several charges and remains in custody as the case proceeds. Haines's staff and colleagues from across the political spectrum have offered messages of support, and the family has asked that, rather than sending flowers, people consider donating to causes in her name. Parliamentary colleagues on all sides have paused usual political hostilities in their public comments this week, with several describing Haines as one of the more widely respected independents in the current parliament regardless of where other MPs sit on policy.

In Queensland, south-east parts of the state are beginning to dry out today after a burst of intense rain triggered widespread flash flooding and emergency call-outs across greater Brisbane and surrounding areas yesterday. The State Emergency Service responded to more than two hundred and seventy call-outs within just a few hours, with leaking roofs the most common issue reported within Brisbane itself, while swiftwater rescue crews responded to several more serious incidents, including campers winched to safety after being cut off by rising water in the Scenic Rim. More than a dozen schools across the south-east remained closed as floodwaters receded, and authorities are urging drivers to stay alert for further flash flooding risk on local roads this morning, even as the heaviest of the rain moves away from the region. Brisbane Airport confirmed flights continued operating without disruption throughout the event, and emergency services have thanked the community for generally heeding warnings to avoid unnecessary travel during the worst of yesterday's conditions. Authorities say the clean-up in hard-hit pockets of the region is likely to continue over the coming days as waterlogged roads and properties dry out. Insurers have also urged residents affected by water damage to document the damage with photos before starting any clean-up work, saying early documentation makes the claims process considerably smoother once assessors are able to visit affected properties.

And on the Gold Coast, a major new retail centre is set to be built on a two and a half hectare site next to Dreamworld, after property group Spotlight bought the block for thirty million dollars from its previous owner. The purchase forms part of a broader, state-approved masterplan for the Dreamworld precinct, which allows for staged development across the site covering tourism, accommodation, retail, commercial, and residential uses over the coming years. The buyer's director said the new retail centre is intended to serve both local residents and the large number of visitors who already pass through the area each year on their way to the theme park and surrounding attractions. The sale adds to a string of recent development activity along that stretch of the Gold Coast, as landholders look to capitalise on steady visitor numbers and population growth in the surrounding suburbs. Local business groups have welcomed the investment, saying a well-designed retail centre in that location could create jobs and give residents more everyday shopping options without needing to travel further into the main tourist strip. No firm construction timeline has been released yet, with the buyer saying detailed design and approval work still needs to be finalised before any building work can begin on site.
