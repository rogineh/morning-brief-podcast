Now to Queensland. The destructive Varroa destructor mite, a parasite that has devastated honey bee populations elsewhere in the country, has been detected in Far North Queensland, in what's believed to be the most northern detection of the pest recorded anywhere in Australia. Authorities are moving to assess how far it may have already spread, given the mite's track record of crippling both commercial beekeeping and the broader pollination that much of the state's agriculture depends on, with biosecurity teams now surveying properties across the region to try to contain any further spread. Meanwhile, a coronial inquest into the 1998 disappearance of Joanne Butterfield has heard emotional testimony from her daughter, Skye Lemon, who was sixteen when her mother vanished after leaving a home on foot near Port Douglas, telling people beforehand that she intended to quit her job, leave town, and hitchhike south. Lemon told the inquest she noticed a marked change in the lead detective's line of questioning after a national media appeal in 2021, when he revealed he'd learned what she had told her mother in their final conversation, though she said she never signed the statement that came from a later formal interview with police.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. Detectives from Taskforce Maxima have charged fifteen people with more than fifty offences following a major operation targeting the alleged recruitment of gang members across the city and the wider southeast region, part of an ongoing crackdown by Queensland Police on organised criminal networks that has already led to a string of similar arrests elsewhere in the region this year. And Gold Coast City Council is pressing ahead with a trial to temporarily close part of the Surfers Paradise esplanade to traffic, reimagining the strip as a pedestrian boulevard for strolling, gathering, and relaxing rather than a thoroughfare for cars. Council says no car parking will be lost during the trial, with traffic monitored on surrounding roads to gauge the impact before any permanent change is considered, as the city looks for ways to ease pressure on its road network ahead of significant population growth expected over the next two decades.
