First, national news. Victoria's corruption watchdog has charged a former senior employee of the National Gallery of Victoria over an alleged scheme in which he used a work account to purchase millions of dollars' worth of mobile phones before selling them for personal gain. Investigators allege the scheme ran for around six years and amassed roughly eight million dollars' worth of devices, with the former employee now facing eight charges including seven counts of theft and one count of misconduct in public office, and due to appear in court next month. The gallery says it has since introduced new financial controls and oversight measures to prevent a repeat of the alleged fraud, and the case is expected to prompt renewed scrutiny of procurement practices at other major public cultural institutions around the country as they review their own internal safeguards. The alleged scheme reportedly went undetected for years despite the scale of the spending involved, raising questions about how closely large arts and cultural institutions monitor day-to-day purchasing decisions made by trusted long-serving staff.

Now to Queensland. The state government has confirmed early works will begin within weeks on a major transformation of the Brisbane Showgrounds, following the conclusion of this year's Ekka, as part of a broader legacy infrastructure push tied to the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Officials say the redevelopment is designed to modernise the historic showgrounds site while preserving its role as home to the annual agricultural show, with planners balancing heritage considerations against the practical demands of a venue that will need to serve both everyday community use and major international events in years to come. The timeline released today gives local businesses and regular showground users a clearer picture of how construction will be staged over the coming years, with authorities promising updates as each phase gets under way and assurances that next year's Ekka will proceed largely as normal despite works beginning nearby. Organisers of the show say they've worked closely with planners throughout the design process to make sure the site's most treasured traditions and facilities are protected, even as large sections of the grounds are progressively rebuilt over the coming years.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. The BLEACH Festival is returning to the city with its biggest program yet, shifting for the first time to a spring date running from the first to the eleventh of October across venues including Kurrawa Park, Emerald Lakes, and HOTA. Under a new artistic director, this year's festival brings together close to fifty free and ticketed events and more than two hundred and ninety artists from the Gold Coast, across Australia, and overseas, headlined by a free opening concert and the return of New Zealand favourites SIX60 to the Gold Coast stage. One of the standout works will see a performer suspended inside a large block of melting ice at Surfers Paradise Beach from dawn on opening day, gradually revealed over roughly seven and a half hours as the ice slowly disappears, in what organisers describe as a striking piece of endurance art unlike anything the festival has attempted before. Local tourism operators say the shift to a spring slot is expected to give the event a fresh boost, with early ticket interest reportedly strong ahead of the program's full public rollout. Nearby cafes, bars, and accommodation providers say they're already gearing up for the busiest stretch of the shoulder season, with many extending trading hours across the eleven days to make the most of the crowds the festival typically draws to the beachfront precincts.
