Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has travelled to the Pacific for pre-COP31 climate talks running from today through until Thursday, with leaders set to spend time in Tuvalu before signing a declaration in Fiji ahead of next month's full United Nations climate summit. Australia's climate credentials are facing renewed scrutiny at the gathering after the federal government moved closer to approving a major expansion of the Hunter Valley coal mine in New South Wales, just days before the talks began, with the New South Wales Independent Planning Commission having already given the project its go-ahead. Pacific leaders and civil society groups have reacted with frustration at the timing, with two advocacy groups taking out a full-page newspaper advertisement in Fiji apologising to Pacific island nations and describing the coal approval as a betrayal. Australia's fossil fuel exports remain a sensitive point in the relationship, with the emissions associated with the country's coal and gas exports alone more than doubling the emissions produced by the entire domestic economy, a point Pacific nations have repeatedly raised as they push wealthier countries to move faster on reducing fossil fuel reliance. Albanese's government has defended its broader record on climate action, pointing to its renewable energy targets and emissions reduction commitments, but the timing of the coal approval has made it harder for Australian representatives to deflect criticism at a forum specifically focused on the existential threat rising seas pose to low-lying Pacific nations.

Now to Queensland. The state government has fast-tracked a thirty-five-year lease extension for bp's fuel storage facility at Bulwer Island in the Port of Brisbane, unlocking an investment of up to one hundred million dollars to refurbish five idle storage tanks and add fifty-four million litres of additional fuel storage capacity by early 2029. The upgrade forms part of the state's broader fuel security strategy, which is focused on boosting local storage and supply resilience so businesses, transport operators, and critical industries are less exposed to disruptions in the global fuel supply chain. Under the terms of the new lease, bp will also have the option to refurbish a further five tanks down the track, potentially unlocking an additional forty-nine million litres of capacity, or even constructing entirely new storage facilities at the site if demand continues to grow. Officials say the extended lease, which gives bp certainty of tenure out to 2061, reflects the scale of long-term planning needed to keep the state's fuel supply chain resilient as Queensland's population and economy continue to expand, with the initial upgrade alone expected to boost capacity at the site by around twenty per cent once complete.

Anyone with information is being asked to contact Policelink directly or submit a report anonymously through Crime Stoppers, with investigators stressing that no detail is considered too minor to pass on while the search for Andrea continues.
