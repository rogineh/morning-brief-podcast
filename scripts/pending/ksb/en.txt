Turning to national news, the latest labour force figures show the unemployment rate climbed to four point nine per cent in October, its highest level since late 2021, in a result that came in well above what most economists had been expecting. The number of unemployed Australians rose by close to eighteen thousand people over the month, while overall employment actually fell by around forty-four thousand, pointing to a labour market that is cooling more quickly than many forecasters had anticipated. Young Australians have felt the shift most acutely, with the youth unemployment rate ticking up to just over eleven per cent, continuing a trend of young job seekers struggling more than the broader workforce to find consistent work. Economists say the weaker results give the Reserve Bank plenty to consider ahead of its early December meeting, with some now suggesting the softening jobs market strengthens the case for holding interest rates steady rather than pushing through another increase. Business groups say the figures are a reminder that higher borrowing costs and slower consumer spending are starting to show up directly in hiring decisions across a range of industries, not just in isolated pockets of the economy. Treasury officials have acknowledged the labour market is easing from its tightest levels in years, though they maintain the broader economy remains in reasonable shape heading into the new year.

In Queensland, the state government has released a new ten-year plan setting out its long-term approach to supporting volunteering across Queensland, covering the period through to 2036. The plan was developed following extensive consultation with volunteers, community organisations, and stakeholders across the state, and is built around three main priorities: building the capacity of volunteer organisations, removing practical barriers that can discourage people from getting involved, and better recognising and valuing the contribution volunteers already make. The government has attached a modest but targeted funding commitment to support the plan's early implementation, aimed at helping community groups put some of its priorities into practice over the coming year. Volunteering organisations have broadly welcomed the long-term framing of the plan, saying a decade-long outlook gives the sector more certainty than the shorter funding and policy cycles it has often had to work within in the past. Officials say the timing is deliberate, with the state wanting a strong and well-supported volunteer base in place well ahead of hosting the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games, when demand for trained volunteers across the state is expected to surge. Community groups have been given until early December to provide final feedback on aspects of the plan before its implementation details are locked in.

And on the Gold Coast, the Suns have continued reshaping their playing list through the AFL trade and free agency period, headlined by the arrival of veteran Collingwood premiership player Scott Pendlebury on the opening day of trading. The club also added Hawthorn pair Aidan Schubert and Sam Butler in a separate deal, continuing a recruitment push aimed at bringing more experience and support into a playing group the club believes is closer to contending than its recent ladder positions suggest. Those additions came alongside some difficult list decisions, with the club delisting former number one draft pick Jamarra Ugle-Hagan ahead of the official list lodgement deadline, though the two sides have since agreed in principle to a one-year deal that would see him return to the club once formalities are completed. The club also delisted tall forward Elliott Himmelberg, with football department staff indicating they intend to pursue bringing him back through the upcoming draft instead. Suns officials say the moves reflect a deliberate strategy of using every avenue available in the trade and draft period to reshape the list, rather than relying on a single big-name signing to change the club's fortunes. Members and fans across the Gold Coast say there is a renewed sense of optimism heading into next season, with the club's recruitment activity this off-season among the busiest in its history.
