Turning to national news, newly released Medicare figures show the national bulk-billing rate has climbed to eighty-three point six per cent, up from seventy-seven point six per cent a year earlier, in what health officials are calling the biggest quarterly jump since the early days of the pandemic. The rise follows the government's decision to triple bulk-billing incentive payments for the most commonly claimed GP consultations, alongside a newer program that pays practices directly for moving to fully bulk-billing models. More than a thousand general practices that previously charged mixed fees have now signed up to bulk bill every patient, according to the latest data, as the government pushes toward its stated target of lifting the national rate to ninety per cent by 2030. Patient groups have welcomed the improvement, saying it reflects a genuine easing of out-of-pocket costs for many households at a time when cost-of-living pressures remain front of mind. Health economists caution that the improvement, while real, has been concentrated in short, straightforward consultations, and argue that funding settings still need further work to properly reflect the needs of patients with more complex or chronic conditions. The government says it remains committed to building on the current trajectory, with further detail on the next phase of its Medicare strategy expected in the months ahead.

In Queensland, the state government has sent its largest-ever trade delegation to the China International Import Expo in Shanghai, with more than one hundred and fifteen representatives from Queensland businesses taking part in this year's event. Of that group, around twenty companies are attending the expo for the first time, reflecting what organisers describe as growing interest among smaller and mid-sized Queensland exporters in building a presence in the Chinese market. The delegation builds on last year's mission, which organisers say generated a record one hundred and thirty million dollars in export deals for Queensland businesses across sectors including agriculture, resources, and consumer goods. The state's trade minister, who is leading this year's delegation, says the mission reflects a deliberate strategy of using major international expos to open doors for exporters that might otherwise struggle to get a foothold in such a large and competitive market on their own. Industry groups representing Queensland exporters say events like the expo remain one of the most effective ways for smaller operators to secure face-to-face meetings with major Chinese buyers and distributors in a single trip. The government says it expects to provide an update on the specific deals and partnerships struck during this year's mission once the delegation returns home and the agreements are finalised.

And on the Gold Coast, the state has formally opened the next stage in expanding the city's light rail network, calling for expressions of interest from construction firms for a new northern extension linking the Gold Coast University Hospital to Harbour Town. The project will run along Olsen Avenue and Oxley Drive, delivering at least three new stations at Musgrave Avenue, Labrador, and Biggera Waters, along with a new public transport interchange at Harbour Town and upgrades to the existing light rail depot at Southport. The extension is designed to connect a growing northern corridor of the city to the broader G:link network, which now stretches from Helensvale through to Burleigh Heads following the recent opening of the Stage Three extension. Transport planners say the new line will also require additional light rail vehicles and a depot upgrade to handle the expanded network, with further details on cost and construction timing expected once the procurement process is further advanced. Local business groups along the proposed route have welcomed the announcement, saying a confirmed light rail connection typically brings a noticeable lift in nearby property and retail activity even before construction begins. Residents in the corridor say they are hopeful the project moves more smoothly through delivery than some of the city's other recent transport planning processes, which have at times faced lengthy delays and changes of direction.
