Turning to national news, families continue adjusting to an expanded paid parental leave scheme that now offers a full six months of government-funded leave to be shared between parents, up from a considerably shorter entitlement in years past. The expanded scheme, which took effect in the middle of this year, also now includes superannuation contributions paid directly on top of the leave payments themselves, addressing a long-standing criticism that new parents who took extended time off work were effectively being penalised with a permanent gap in their retirement savings. Advocacy groups focused on gender equity in the workplace say the superannuation change in particular should help narrow the retirement savings gap between men and women over time, given women have traditionally taken the bulk of extended parental leave and therefore borne the brunt of lost compulsory super contributions during those periods. Employer groups have broadly supported the scheme's expansion, though some smaller businesses say adjusting rosters and workflows around longer periods of staff leave remains an ongoing practical challenge, particularly in industries where finding temporary replacement staff can be difficult. Family advocacy organisations say the shift toward a genuinely shared six-month leave entitlement, rather than one overwhelmingly taken by mothers, is slowly starting to change workplace culture and expectations around caregiving responsibilities, even if deeply ingrained patterns will likely take considerably longer to shift in full.

In Queensland, the state's first dedicated critical minerals processing facility has officially opened in Townsville, giving mining companies a shared, purpose-built site to test and refine processing methods for minerals considered essential to batteries and other emerging technologies. The hundred-and-forty-six-million-dollar facility allows smaller mining and processing companies to trial and validate their production techniques at a meaningful scale without each having to build and fund an entire standalone processing plant of their own. A vanadium production company has already signed on as the facility's first foundation customer, planning to use the site to demonstrate and validate its own processing methods before committing to larger-scale commercial production elsewhere. Industry groups say having this kind of shared testing infrastructure available domestically removes a significant barrier that's previously forced many Australian critical minerals companies to send samples overseas for processing trials, adding cost and delay to projects at a crucial early stage. State officials say the facility's location in Townsville reflects North Queensland's growing importance as a hub for the critical minerals sector, with the broader region home to a significant share of the raw mineral deposits that make these processing technologies commercially viable in the first place.

And on the Gold Coast, the city's council has continued progressing its commitment to protecting the region's natural environment, having established its first council-managed conservation area earlier this year to protect a significant wildlife corridor spanning more than a hundred and twenty hectares. The designation locks in long-term protection for the area, shielding it from future development pressure as the city's population and housing needs continue to grow rapidly across the broader region. Environmental groups have welcomed the move, saying formally protected conservation areas like this one play a crucial role in maintaining habitat connectivity for native wildlife, particularly as surrounding bushland elsewhere on the Gold Coast continues to be cleared for new housing and infrastructure projects. Council officials say the conservation area forms part of a broader strategy to balance the city's continued growth with genuine environmental protection, rather than treating conservation as an afterthought once development decisions have already been made. Local residents and community groups say they're hopeful the designation sets a precedent for further conservation commitments as the council continues working through its broader planning and growth strategies in the years ahead.
