Good morning, and welcome to your Morning Brief for Wednesday, the 11th of November, 2026. Here's what's happening today across Australia, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

In national news, a new ban on disability service providers offering kickbacks and inducements is set to take effect later this month, as the next phase of the government's broader package of changes to the National Disability Insurance Scheme continues to roll out. The ban, scheduled to commence on the twenty-seventh of November, forms part of a wider reform agenda the government has described as securing the scheme for future generations, following years of concern about rising costs and instances of providers exploiting participants or the system itself. Other changes within the same package are being phased in on a staggered basis, including adjustments to how community participation funding is assessed, which will generally apply as individual participants have their plans reviewed or renewed rather than all at once. Advocacy groups have broadly welcomed efforts to crack down on predatory behaviour within the scheme, while also urging the government to ensure genuine service providers aren't caught up in additional red tape as the new compliance measures come into force. The scheme has grown substantially in both participant numbers and overall cost since its establishment, prompting successive governments to pursue reforms aimed at putting it on a more sustainable long-term footing without undermining the support it provides.

In Queensland, nine state schools have been honoured at this year's Showcase Awards, held at Parliament House in Brisbane to recognise excellence in teaching and learning from across the state's public education system. Spring Mountain State School, representing the metropolitan south region, and Abergowrie State School in north Queensland were among the schools recognised in this year's early years category, with winners selected from a field of nominees spanning every region of the state. Each of the honoured schools will share in a bursary pool worth a hundred and twenty thousand dollars, which they can use either to extend their own successful programs or to help other schools across Queensland adopt similar practices. The awards have run for more than two decades now, consistently highlighting examples of innovative teaching, strong community partnerships, and measurable improvements in student outcomes at individual school level. Education officials say the program plays an important role in giving high-performing schools, including many in regional and remote areas, the same recognition and visibility more commonly associated with larger metropolitan institutions. This year's event fell during a period the department has used to spotlight the broader teaching profession, with officials noting that strong public education outcomes depend heavily on recognising and retaining talented teachers and school leaders across the system.

And on the Gold Coast, major construction work is ramping up on the new Coomera Hospital, a project officials say will eventually reshape healthcare access across the rapidly growing northern corridor of the city. The hospital's cost has risen to around two point two five billion dollars since it was first proposed, with builder Multiplex now overseeing work that has moved into its main construction phase during the second half of this year following a period of delay. Once complete, the facility is expected to provide more than six hundred beds across two separate stages of development, making it one of the largest single health infrastructure investments in the region's history. The project's original opening timeline has been pushed back substantially, with the first beds now not expected to become operational until around 2031, reflecting both the scale of construction involved and broader cost pressures affecting major infrastructure projects nationally. Local health advocates say the investment remains critical regardless of the extended timeline, pointing to rapid population growth in the surrounding Coomera and northern Gold Coast corridor as placing increasing strain on existing hospital capacity in the meantime. Residents in the area will likely need to continue relying on existing facilities for several more years yet before the new hospital eventually opens its doors.
