In national news, thousands more Australians will have access to a fully funded university place from next year, after legislation establishing the new Australian Tertiary Education Commission formally passed federal parliament and the first round of expanded funding allocations began flowing through to individual providers. From the start of 2027, a further sixteen thousand fully funded Commonwealth-supported places will be added to the system, part of a broader commitment to expand the overall pool by around two hundred and thirty thousand places over the coming decade. The new commission will take over responsibility for setting and distributing the total number of funded places available each year, working directly with individual universities and other providers rather than leaving allocation decisions solely in the hands of central government departments. Separately, the government has also published indicative figures for international student numbers in 2027, setting an overall national planning level of almost three hundred thousand new overseas student commencements, with the bulk of those places allocated to public universities and a smaller pool set aside for other providers. Education sector representatives have broadly welcomed the additional domestic funding, while continuing to call for further certainty around research funding more broadly, arguing that teaching capacity alone doesn't address longstanding pressure on university research budgets. Student groups have also welcomed the additional funded places, saying the expansion should help ease competition for popular courses that have in recent years left some eligible school leavers missing out on their preferred area of study altogether.

In Queensland, a sweeping planning overhaul for the Bowen Hills area of inner Brisbane could ultimately fast-track as many as twenty-seven thousand new homes, in a move the state government has directly linked to capturing long-term economic benefits from hosting the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games. The proposed changes would allow buildings of up to fifty storeys in parts of the precinct, a dramatic increase on what's currently permitted, while also removing some existing requirements around affordable housing contributions and green building standards that currently apply to development in the area. Officials say the area's proximity to the planned Victoria Park Olympic precinct, along with planning changes already accelerating housing delivery at the nearby Northshore Hamilton site, makes Bowen Hills a logical focus for this kind of concentrated, higher-density development. Public submissions on the proposed changes closed last month, and a final decision on exactly how the new planning scheme will look is still being worked through by relevant authorities. Supporters argue the changes are necessary to meaningfully address the city's housing shortage, while some planning advocates have raised concerns about the trade-off involved in removing existing affordable housing and sustainability requirements in pursuit of faster overall delivery. Local residents in the surrounding suburbs have been encouraged to review the detailed draft documents before any final scheme is formally adopted, with authorities promising further opportunities for community input as the project progresses.

And on the Gold Coast, tonight marks the official closing ceremony of this year's Pan Pacific Masters Games, bringing the curtain down on ten days of competition that drew more than sixteen thousand athletes from over forty countries to the city. The closing night celebration is being held at the Gold Coast Convention and Exhibition Centre in Broadbeach, with Australian dance act The Potbelleez headlining the entertainment lineup as competitors, officials, and local supporters gather to mark the end of this year's event. The Games spanned more than forty different sports across numerous venues throughout the city, with competition continuing right through to today for several disciplines even as other sports wrapped up their programs earlier in the week. Organisers say the sheer scale and diversity of this year's event, spanning everything from swimming and athletics to lawn bowls and ocean swimming, helped spread visitor spending broadly across hotels, restaurants, and attractions citywide throughout the entire ten-day period. With tonight's ceremony bringing this edition to a close, attention will now turn to assessing the Games' full economic impact on the city, following strong participation numbers reported across individual sports throughout the fortnight.
