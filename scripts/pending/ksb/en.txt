Across Australia, tributes have flowed from across the motorsport world following the death of Alan Jones, Australia's most recent Formula One world champion, who passed away yesterday at the age of eighty following a brief illness. Jones became the first driver to win a world title for the Williams team back in 1980, clinching the championship driving a Ford Cosworth-powered car in the second-last round of that season, and he remains the last Australian to win the sport's premier drivers' title to this day. Over the course of his career, Jones claimed twelve grand prix victories, establishing a reputation as a fast, uncompromising racer who became one of the defining figures of his era in Formula One. The Williams team, which Jones drove for throughout his championship-winning years, said everyone connected with the organisation was deeply saddened to learn of his passing, while Formula One's own chief executive described him as a true great of the sport. The promoters of the Australian Grand Prix also paid tribute, noting that Jones holds a genuinely special place in the broader history of Australian motorsport. Jones had celebrated his eightieth birthday only days before his death, and he's survived by his wife and six children.

In Queensland, Brisbane residents are being invited to have their say on a draft twenty-year vision for the future of Mount Coot-tha, one of the city's most popular natural attractions, with public feedback open until the end of this month. The draft plan covers protecting existing bushland and wildlife habitat on the mountain, improving the network of walking trails, refreshing several of its most popular visitor destinations, and considering genuinely new options for the future of the mountain's long-running quarry site. Among the ideas being floated is transforming that quarry area into dedicated parkland once current operations eventually wind down there over the coming decade, alongside broader proposals for upgraded visitor facilities and expanded nature-based tourism experiences across the wider precinct. Officials involved in developing the draft have been careful to stress it represents a long-term planning vision rather than a confirmed construction schedule, with authorities specifically ruling out turning the mountain into anything resembling a theme park. The draft has already drawn substantial public interest, having been shaped by thousands of individual pieces of community feedback gathered during earlier consultation, with a final version of the vision expected to be settled by the middle of next year once this latest round of submissions closes.

And on the Gold Coast, football fans are gearing up for a significant weekend as the Suns prepare to host Hawthorn in an AFLW elimination final, with both clubs chasing their first-ever finals victory in what shapes as a genuinely historic occasion for each side. The Gold Coast women's team enjoyed a dramatic turnaround this season, climbing from the competition's wooden spoon just last year to finish fifth on the ladder with a nine-win, three-loss record heading into this weekend's sudden-death clash. Key midfielder Anne Hatchard, who joined the Suns from Adelaide and quickly became a standout contributor in her first season at the club, says the team still has plenty of improvement left in it even after securing a finals berth for the first time since 2023. Sunday's match is scheduled for the Suns' home ground a little after three o'clock, with both Gold Coast and Hawthorn entering the contest still searching for their maiden finals win after each falling short in previous finals appearances across the competition's history. Local football fans are expected to turn out in strong numbers for what both clubs have described as a genuinely significant step in their respective development as finals-calibre sides within the still relatively young national women's competition.
