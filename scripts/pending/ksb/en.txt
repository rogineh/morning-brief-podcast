Public health group Quit says illegal tobacco advertisements are continuing to slip through the cracks on social media, with the federal government's enforcement efforts barely scratching the surface of the problem. Investigators reviewing the issue found multiple examples of illegal tobacco ads running on Facebook that weren't removed after being reported by users, only coming down once journalists made direct enquiries with the platform. The Therapeutic Goods Administration has issued one point five million dollars in fines against tobacco advertisers so far this year, but Quit describes that figure as just the tip of the iceberg given how widespread the ads remain, and is calling for retailers and social media companies alike to face real consequences for allowing the promotions to stay up. Separately, researchers have also raised concerns about nicotine pouch advertising, finding that despite such advertising being illegal in Australia, almost every group surveyed reported being exposed to it, with social media once again the most common source and TikTok the most frequently cited platform. Health advocates say the findings highlight just how far platform enforcement still lags behind the law, leaving young Australians regularly exposed to products they shouldn't be seeing promoted at all. Quit says it wants to see mandatory, faster takedown obligations placed on the platforms themselves rather than relying on users and journalists to flag individual ads one at a time, arguing that the current system puts the burden of enforcement on the public instead of the companies profiting from the advertising in the first place.

Organisers say the event has become an important fixture on the water sports calendar for the region, giving Cairns a chance to showcase its wake park facilities to a national and international audience while also drawing visitors to the city during what's traditionally regarded as a quieter stretch of the tourist season. Local tourism operators say events like this one help diversify the far north's appeal beyond its more famous reef and rainforest attractions, bringing a dedicated niche audience of water sports enthusiasts to the region each year.
