Across Australia, a new Medicare-funded health check for three-year-old children officially launched earlier this week, reintroducing a developmental screening item that was actually abolished roughly a decade ago under a previous government. Under the new arrangement, every three-year-old eligible for Medicare can access one lifetime health assessment with a doctor, covering a broad review of medical history, behaviour, social relationships, toileting progress, lifestyle habits, and sleep patterns, all compared against typical developmental milestones for that age group. General practitioners conducting the check can refer families on to additional support services if any concerns are identified during the assessment, helping catch potential developmental issues earlier than might otherwise happen before children start school. The check has also been recognised as meeting existing health check requirements tied to family tax benefit payments, meaning parents completing the assessment can simultaneously satisfy an administrative requirement they'd otherwise need to address separately. Health advocates have broadly welcomed the reintroduction of the check, noting that early childhood development specialists had long argued for its return given the value of catching developmental concerns while intervention options remain most effective. Parents are being encouraged to book the assessment through their usual general practice rather than waiting for a specific invitation, with practices around the country now working through how best to fit the new check into their existing appointment schedules.

In Queensland, a parliamentary committee is due to hand down its report tomorrow on contentious proposed changes to the state's mining objections process, legislation that's drawn sharp criticism from legal experts and conservation groups since it was first introduced to parliament in September. The proposed changes would limit who's legally able to formally object to new mining lease applications, restricting standing to directly affected landholders, local governments, and parties located within a hundred and twenty-five kilometres of a proposed mine's boundary. The legislation would also end the current system of automatically referring formal objections to the Land Court for independent review, instead making that referral discretionary and subject to a minister's or relevant authority's own judgment about whether a hearing is warranted. The state's resources minister has defended the proposed changes as necessary to prevent what he's described as vexatious objections from needlessly delaying legitimate resource projects, arguing the reforms would streamline the approvals process without actually weakening environmental protections. Legal and environmental critics strongly disagree, warning the changes would meaningfully reduce independent scrutiny of mining decisions and effectively silence many Queenslanders and conservation groups who might otherwise have a legitimate stake in how a particular project proceeds.

And on the Gold Coast, organisers are making final preparations for the Pan Pacific Masters Games, a major ten-day multi-sport event for masters-level athletes that begins tomorrow and is expected to draw more than sixteen thousand competitors and supporters to the city. The biennial event brings together athletes typically aged thirty and above competing across dozens of different sports, ranging from traditional athletics and swimming disciplines through to various team sports, with age-based categories allowing competitors well into their seventies and eighties to continue competing at an organised, officiated level. Local tourism and hospitality operators are expecting a significant economic boost from the ten-day event, with visiting competitors and their support networks filling hotels, restaurants, and local attractions across what's traditionally a slightly quieter period between the end of the school holidays and the start of the Christmas tourist season. The games have become a notable fixture on the city's sporting and events calendar since their establishment, prized locally both for the direct economic activity they generate and for showcasing the Gold Coast's extensive sporting infrastructure to a visiting international audience. Organisers say venues across the city have been finalising logistics in recent days, with opening ceremonies and the first full day of competition both scheduled to get underway tomorrow.
