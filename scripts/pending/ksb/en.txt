Turning to national news, the federal government's expansion of the Commonwealth Prac Payment is continuing to roll out, with the scheme set to extend eligibility to ten additional frontline health disciplines from the middle of next year, including clinical psychology, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and speech pathology. The payment currently offers eligible students around three hundred and thirty-eight dollars a week while they complete mandatory, often unpaid, placements as part of their university studies, support that's designed to help cover basic living costs during what can otherwise be a financially gruelling stretch of study. The government says the expansion will support roughly ninety-five thousand students each year once fully implemented, with total investment in the program running into the hundreds of millions of dollars over the next several years. Universities and peak bodies representing allied health students have broadly welcomed the move, arguing that unpaid placement requirements have long been a significant barrier to completing these degrees, particularly for students without substantial family financial support or those juggling part-time work on top of full-time study. Education groups say the change addresses a long-standing fairness issue in how Australia trains its future health workforce, given these placements are compulsory for graduation yet have traditionally come at direct financial cost to the student rather than any kind of paid employment.

In Queensland, the newly expanded Hervey Bay Hospital has officially opened its doors, marking the completion of one of the state's flagship hospital redevelopment projects under the government's broader plan to rebuild capacity across the public health system. The expansion adds significant new capacity to a hospital that has long served a rapidly growing regional population across the Fraser Coast, with local health advocates saying the additional beds and upgraded facilities should meaningfully ease pressure that's built up over recent years as the local population has expanded faster than the hospital's capacity could keep pace with. Health Minister officials who attended the opening said the project represents exactly the kind of regional investment the government's hospital rebuilding plan was designed to deliver, prioritising communities that have historically had to travel significant distances for more complex care. Local residents and healthcare workers who've watched the project take shape over recent years say the completion marks a genuine milestone for the region, even as they note that growing demand means today's expanded capacity will still need to be carefully managed and potentially built upon again in years to come. The opening adds to a string of similar hospital completions delivered under the same statewide rebuilding program in recent months.

And on the Gold Coast, locally based road safety company Acusensus has continued its rapid expansion into the United States market this year, adding several new state government contracts for its mobile phone detection and speed enforcement camera systems across jurisdictions including Connecticut, Kentucky, and North Carolina. The company, which builds automated cameras capable of detecting illegal phone use and speeding from moving traffic, has built its reputation domestically through contracts with state road safety authorities here in Australia before expanding more aggressively overseas over the past couple of years. Executives say the US market represents an enormous growth opportunity given the scale of the road toll there and growing political appetite among state transport departments to crack down on distracted and dangerous driving using proven detection systems rather than developing one from scratch. The company's continued contract wins have been closely watched by local investors, with its share price responding positively each time a new state agency signs on, reflecting growing confidence that the business has found a genuinely exportable product with strong recurring revenue potential. Local business groups say Acusensus's success represents exactly the kind of homegrown success story the Gold Coast's growing innovation sector hopes to produce more of in the years ahead.
