Across Australia, education ministers from the Commonwealth, states, and territories are meeting today to consider a New South Wales proposal to return NAPLAN writing tests to pen and paper for students in years five, seven, and nine, eight years after the national literacy and numeracy test first moved online. Education experts have backed the push, arguing that handwriting plays an important role in how children learn and retain information, a factor some researchers believe has been under-appreciated since testing shifted to computers. Under the current format, only year three students complete their writing assessment on paper, while all older students sit the same test on a computer screen instead. New South Wales wants any change implemented from 2028, giving schools and the testing body time to prepare, with the state's education minister saying she's genuinely open to seeing what a return to paper testing might reveal about student performance. Experts consulted on the proposal say any shift back to paper would need to include proper accommodations for students with learning difficulties and additional needs, while one researcher noted the change could also have flow-on effects for how typing and digital literacy are taught more broadly in primary and secondary classrooms.

In Queensland, the Bureau of Meteorology is warning of a fresh round of severe storms moving across the state's south-east today, bringing the possibility of large hail and damaging winds to areas including Brisbane and the Sunshine Coast, while a serious heatwave is simultaneously building over the state's Gulf Country in the far north-west. Forecasters say temperatures in parts of the Gulf region could climb as high as forty degrees over the coming days, a stark contrast to the storm-prone conditions expected further south and east at the same time. The warning follows a run of unsettled spring weather across the south-east, including a particularly dangerous storm cell late last month that dumped hail up to seven centimetres in diameter across parts of the region and caused significant localised damage. Authorities are urging residents in storm-affected areas to secure loose outdoor items, avoid sheltering under trees during thunderstorm activity, and stay updated through official Bureau of Meteorology channels as the system moves through, while those in the state's far north-west are being reminded to take standard heat-safety precautions given the extreme temperatures forecast there. The contrasting conditions across different parts of the state are a reminder of just how geographically varied Queensland's weather can become heading into the build-up to the wet season.

And on the Gold Coast, the Suns have confirmed they'll delist key forward Jamarra Ugle-Hagan and fellow forward Elliott Himmelberg, but say they intend to select both players again through next month's national draft in a now-common list management strategy used by several AFL clubs. Ugle-Hagan, a former number-one draft pick who joined the Suns during last year's trade period, is among several notable names caught up in this year's wave of delistings across the competition, with half a dozen other clubs also planning to redraft players they've formally let go. Himmelberg, meanwhile, has spent much of the past year recovering from a serious knee injury, and is reportedly on track to return to AFL football for the first time in well over a year once pre-season training resumes under his renewed Suns contract. The unusual practice of delisting and then immediately redrafting the same player allows clubs to manage their salary cap and list size more flexibly heading into the new season, without actually losing the services of players they still value. Suns fans will be watching next month's draft closely to see exactly where in the order both players end up being selected, even though the outcome for the club itself is effectively already settled.
