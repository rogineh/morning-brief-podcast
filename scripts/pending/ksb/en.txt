Fresh figures show headline inflation jumped to four per cent in August, up from three point five per cent in July, driven largely by rising housing costs and higher prices at the petrol pump. The jump comes just days after the Reserve Bank lifted the cash rate for the fourth time this year, taking it to four point six per cent, and will likely reinforce the central bank's view that price pressures remain stronger than it would like heading into summer. Treasurer Jim Chalmers has promised further action on both inflation and productivity, though economists say the government faces a difficult balancing act between supporting households doing it tough and avoiding measures that could add even more fuel to price growth. Separately, the federal government has written to a number of vocational education providers after noticing a sharp spike in new student enrolments in the weeks since it was announced that a particular course would be shuttered from the fifth of October, with officials now seeking an explanation for the sudden surge before the cut-off date arrives. Consumer advocates say the episode highlights how quickly students can be left scrambling when course closures are announced with limited notice, and are calling for clearer transition arrangements to be built into future funding decisions so enrolled students aren't caught out partway through their studies.

Now to Queensland. The state government has introduced nation-leading reforms to Parliament that will make it mandatory for clinicians to refer high-risk mental health patients to police where there's an assessed danger they could commit violence involving a weapon, part of a broader package responding to a coronial inquest into a tragic incident in the state's west several years ago. The reforms will also strengthen firearm prohibition orders, giving police more power to stop dangerous offenders from accessing weapons, alongside improved intelligence-sharing between Queensland Police and Commonwealth national security agencies. Officials say the changes fall short of mandating checks for all firearms licence applicants and won't extend to the private health system, but represent a significant tightening of how the state identifies and manages people assessed as an escalating risk to community safety. The government says the reforms reflect lessons learned from the inquest and are designed to give frontline clinicians and police clearer pathways to intervene before a crisis occurs. Mental health groups have broadly welcomed the intent behind the changes but say adequate training and support for clinicians making these assessments will be essential, cautioning that poorly resourced implementation could undermine trust between patients and the health professionals treating them.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. Plans are progressing for the proposed two billion dollar Gold Coast Arena at Carey Park in Southport, with a consortium bringing together Live Nation Entertainment and Plenary Group, known as AmplifyGC, selected earlier this year to design, build, finance, and operate the venue. The twelve-thousand-plus seat arena is being planned as both a Brisbane twenty thirty-two Olympic and Paralympic Games venue and a long-term home for major touring artists, sporting events, and large-scale entertainment, with construction targeted to begin in 2027 and an opening planned for 2030. Economic modelling commissioned by the city suggests the project could generate up to two billion dollars in construction output, support more than six thousand five hundred jobs, and inject around seven hundred million dollars into the regional economy. Council officials say fast-tracked planning and approval pathways available under Olympic-related legislation should help keep the project on schedule, positioning the Gold Coast with a world-class indoor venue befitting its growing reputation as a major events destination well beyond the Games themselves. Local business groups say landing a venue of this scale would finally give the city the capacity to host major touring acts and events that currently bypass the Gold Coast in favour of Brisbane or Sydney, keeping more entertainment spending and visitor nights within the local economy once the arena eventually opens its doors.
