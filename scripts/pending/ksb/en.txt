Across Australia, defence officials have told a parliamentary committee that the country is weighing up an Iron Dome-style interceptor system to help protect military bases from missile and drone attack, with a goal of having a ground-based shield in place by the early 2030s. Among the options being considered are a system used by the United States Marine Corps, the well-established Patriot missile system, and a land-based version of the Aegis naval defence system, with a competitive selection process expected to run alongside the Talisman Sabre military exercise in 2027. A senior Air Force officer told the committee that the Defence Force expects to bring formal recommendations to government next year, after officials cited a sharp rise in drone incursions over defence bases, from nineteen recorded incidents in 2022 to more than two hundred last year. Australia doesn't supply weapons to Israel, where the original Iron Dome system was developed, and officials stressed that any purchase would involve variants of the technology produced or licensed through American defence contractors rather than direct acquisition from Israeli suppliers. The push comes against a backdrop of growing global concern about the vulnerability of military installations to cheap, mass-produced drones, a threat that's prompted several allied nations to accelerate their own investment in layered air defence systems over the past couple of years.

In Queensland, a new specialist hospital unit at Logan has opened its doors to help patients living with delirium or dementia make a safer transition out of hospital and back to their families or aged care homes. The Acute Cognitive Unit provides specialised care for patients experiencing complex behaviours associated with dementia, with particular emphasis on preventing and managing delirium, a sudden and often distressing state of confusion that can develop quickly in older hospital patients, especially after surgery, infection, or a sudden change in environment. Staff at the unit work as part of a broader hospital-wide push bringing together nursing, medical, and allied health teams dedicated to planning and delivering evidence-based dementia and delirium care, building on an existing model at the hospital that assigns dedicated nursing rounds to patients identified as being at higher risk. Health authorities say early intervention and the right environment can make a significant difference to outcomes for older patients experiencing cognitive decline during a hospital stay, including shorter overall admissions and a reduced likelihood of being readmitted soon after discharge. The new unit reflects a broader focus within the state's health system on improving care pathways for an ageing population, as hospitals across the state continue to see rising demand from older patients presenting with complex and overlapping health needs.

And on the Gold Coast, the mayor says the council will consider appealing a Planning and Environment Court decision that overturned approval for a fourteen-storey, hundred-and-thirty-million-dollar tower at Kirra, after the judge found the proposed building would visually dominate the surrounding low-rise neighbourhood. In her judgment, the judge described the tower's design as creating a jarring, canyon-like effect for nearby residents, comparing its mismatch with the surrounding streetscape to a person wearing ill-fitting clothing, and found the council had been wrong to approve a building that exceeded existing height limits for the area. The ruling followed a legal challenge brought by local residents against the council's original approval of the project just over a year ago, continuing a long-running pattern of disputes over high-rise development along parts of the southern Gold Coast coastline. The mayor acknowledged that mounting an appeal could cost the council as much as a million dollars in legal fees, but says councillors want to properly weigh up their options given the implications the ruling could have for other height-limit disputes already working their way through the courts. The development's backer has said it's reviewing the court's decision and hasn't yet confirmed whether it will pursue its own separate appeal or redesign the project to bring it into line with the judge's concerns.
