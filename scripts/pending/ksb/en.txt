The Australian Human Rights Commission has released a new report finding that pregnancy discrimination remains widespread in Australian workplaces, warning it continues to be a major barrier to gender equality and workforce participation more than a decade after an earlier landmark review first exposed the problem. The report makes eighteen recommendations, including changes to the Sex Discrimination Act to explicitly ban indirect discrimination based on family responsibilities, and new limits on redundancies made during or immediately after a worker's parental leave. The Commission says formal complaints about pregnancy discrimination have climbed noticeably over the past several years, which it says likely reflects both a genuine rise in cases and greater willingness among workers to come forward. Advocates have welcomed the recommendations as a necessary update to protections that haven't kept pace with how common pregnancy and parental leave discrimination remains, while some employer groups have cautioned that any legislative changes should be carefully targeted so they don't create new compliance uncertainty for small businesses. The government has not yet responded formally to the report's recommendations, though the Sex Discrimination Commissioner has urged swift action given how long the underlying problem has persisted. Workplace lawyers say the findings are likely to prompt a fresh wave of inquiries from pregnant workers and new parents about their rights, regardless of whether the specific legislative changes end up being adopted.

In Queensland, debate continues over the state government's expanded Adult Crime, Adult Time laws, two years after the policy was first introduced as a central plank of its approach to youth crime. The government points to falling numbers of youth crime victims as evidence the tougher approach is working, and has recently added further offences to the list covered by the laws, including aggravated assault causing bodily harm, along with new police powers to ban repeat offenders from certain city centres. An independent criminologist has pushed back on the government's figures, arguing the comparison being used understates the true picture and that the overall crime rate remains at a twenty-year high. Queensland's Human Rights Commissioner has separately urged the government to consult more widely and weigh the evidence carefully before expanding laws that override standard human rights protections for young offenders. The debate has also played out in parliament, where a separate rehabilitation-focused sentencing bill passed despite opposition, with the government framing it as a complement to its tougher measures and the opposition arguing resources would be better directed elsewhere. Community groups working with at-risk young people say whatever the final mix of policies looks like, consistent long-term funding matters more than any single piece of legislation passed in any one parliamentary term.

And on the Gold Coast, excitement is building ahead of this weekend's Gold Coast 500, the opening round of the Supercars Finals Series, running from Friday the 23rd through Sunday the 25th on the Surfers Paradise street circuit. Local favourite Cam Waters heads into the weekend looking for redemption after a disappointing result at the Bathurst 1000, and will be hoping to add to what's already a strong record at the event, having won the Saturday race in each of the past two years. Waters starts the weekend well placed in the pitlane order for his Tickford Racing team, a position some in the sport say could work in his favour by keeping him clear of any potential mid-pack congestion involving rival teams. Organisers are expecting large crowds across the three-day event, with road closures already in place around the Surfers Paradise precinct and local businesses bracing for a busy weekend of race-goers filling nearby cafes, bars, and accommodation. For many residents, the Gold Coast 500 remains one of the standout events on the city's annual calendar, drawing visitors from across the country to the glittering street circuit along the beachfront, with practice and qualifying on Friday, the first Finals Series race on Saturday, and the weekend wrapping up with the final race on Sunday.
