Regional airline Rex has secured its future under new ownership, with the federal government confirming its purchase by a US aviation company alongside a financing agreement designed to keep the carrier's regional routes running without interruption. The deal brings some certainty to a string of smaller communities that had faced losing their only scheduled air service during Rex's prolonged financial difficulties, with the new ownership structure aimed at stabilising operations while the airline works through its broader turnaround. Regional mayors and business groups have welcomed the resolution, saying reliable air links are essential for everything from medical appointments to business travel in towns where driving to the nearest major airport can take several hours. The government says it will continue monitoring the carrier's finances closely to ensure the regional network remains viable over the longer term, rather than simply being saved in the short term only to face the same pressures again down the track. Aviation analysts say the case highlights a broader challenge facing regional air services across the country, where thin passenger numbers on many routes make it difficult for any single operator to run a genuinely profitable network without some form of government support or subsidy underpinning at least part of it.

In Queensland, new police figures show crime victim numbers have fallen by nearly ten per cent over the past two years, with some of the steepest declines recorded in high-harm offence categories including unlawful entry and robbery. Police say the improvement reflects a sustained push toward intelligence-led policing and targeted operations against repeat and organised offenders, rather than any single short-term crackdown. The figures mark a notable turnaround after several years in which recorded offences had been climbing, and officials say they're cautiously optimistic the trend can be sustained if current policing strategies and resourcing levels are maintained. Community safety advocates have welcomed the decline but caution that a two year improvement, however encouraging, shouldn't be read as a sign the underlying drivers of crime in some communities have been fully addressed. Police say a specific operation targeting repeat and organised offenders has been particularly effective, and they're now looking at how to apply similar methods to other offence categories that haven't seen the same level of improvement so far.

Meanwhile on the Gold Coast, the airport is pushing hard to lock in new direct international routes, building on a strong year that saw international passenger growth of around twenty five per cent. Airport executives say the goal now is to establish genuine hub connections into Asia as well as longer haul links to the United Kingdom and Europe, rather than relying solely on existing short and medium haul routes to New Zealand and the Pacific. The push follows the airport's two hundred and sixty million dollar terminal expansion, which doubled its footprint and added capacity for substantially more aircraft movements ahead of what's expected to be a major surge in demand in the lead-up to the 2032 Olympics. Tourism and business groups say securing more direct long haul capacity would be a significant win for the broader region, reducing the need for travellers to transit through Brisbane or Sydney and making the Gold Coast a more attractive gateway in its own right for international visitors and investment alike. Negotiations with prospective airline partners are understood to be ongoing, and airport executives say they're hopeful of confirming at least one new long haul agreement within the next year or so, though they caution that securing international route rights and aircraft availability from partner airlines can take considerably longer than simply having the terminal capacity ready to support new services.
