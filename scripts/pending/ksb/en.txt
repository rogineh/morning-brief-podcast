First, national news. A striking new four-metre-high sculpture of beloved children's author Andy Griffiths has been unveiled, created using three-dimensional printing technology and designed to capture what admirers describe as the writer's enduring "inner kid," the same restless, mischievous spirit that's powered his hugely popular books for young readers over more than two decades. The artwork has already drawn crowds keen to see the larger-than-life tribute in person, with fans and fellow authors alike praising it as a fitting nod to a career built on making generations of children fall in love with reading through silliness, adventure, and a healthy disregard for grown-up rules. Griffiths himself has spoken warmly about the project, saying he's touched that his work has resonated deeply enough with readers to inspire something so ambitious, and organisers say they hope the sculpture will become a popular photo stop for families for years to come. The tribute has particular resonance for parents who grew up reading his books themselves and are now passing the same stories on to their own children, a generational handover that fans say speaks to just how deeply his stories have embedded themselves in Australian childhoods.

Now to Queensland. A newly combined push dubbed the Queensland Heartland bid is emerging as a serious contender for the NRL's twentieth licence, joining together the historically rugby league-mad Toowoomba and Darling Downs region with Rockhampton and the wider Central Queensland area in a single proposal designed to tap into some of the code's deepest grassroots support. The bid will compete against rival proposals from Ipswich and New Zealand, with the NRL's chief executive expected to reveal which region ultimately secures the new licence by Christmas, and any successful franchise not expected to take the field until 2029. Backers of the Heartland bid say combining two proud but separate rugby league heartlands into one proposal gives it a scale and reach that neither region could offer alone, pointing to passionate local participation numbers and a deep pool of home-grown talent as reasons the NRL should back a genuinely regional Queensland club. Community leaders across both regions have welcomed the joint approach, saying a combined bid avoids the risk of splitting local support and resources across two separate, smaller proposals that might otherwise have struggled to compete against better-resourced rivals.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. All eyes are on Cbus Super Stadium today, where the home-town Titans face the Brisbane Broncos in the NRLW preliminary final, with a place in next weekend's grand final on the line for the winner. The Titans enter off the back of a record-breaking regular season, finishing second on the ladder with ten wins from eleven games, and will be chasing a first-ever grand final appearance for the club after falling short as runners-up back in 2023. Brisbane, the reigning premiers, will provide a stern test as they look to keep their title defence alive, setting up what promises to be a fiercely contested Queensland derby in front of what's expected to be a big home crowd. Local fans have been snapping up tickets throughout the week, with the club encouraging supporters to arrive early given the scale of interest in a match that could deliver the Gold Coast its first appearance in an NRLW grand final. Local businesses around the stadium precinct say they're expecting a busy afternoon regardless of the result, with the match capping off what's already been a hugely successful season for the club both on and off the field in terms of crowd numbers and community engagement.
