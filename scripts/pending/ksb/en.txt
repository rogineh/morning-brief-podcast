In national news, work is continuing on the federal government's broader overhaul of veteran support services, with construction now under way on a new Veterans' and Families' Hub in Geelong, adding to a growing national network of similar centres designed to bring support services together under one roof. The hub forms part of a wider reform program that's been rolling out since a dedicated Veteran and Family Wellbeing Agency began operating earlier this year, created specifically to help current and former Australian Defence Force members and their families navigate what's historically been a complex and fragmented support system. The broader reforms stem largely from recommendations handed down by the Royal Commission into Defence and Veteran Suicide, with this year's federal budget allocating further funding specifically aimed at implementing those recommendations across the veteran support network. Ex-service organisations have broadly welcomed the additional investment, while some have cautioned that associated savings measures elsewhere in the system could partially offset the benefits if they're not managed carefully alongside the new spending. Officials say the hub model, which brings together healthcare, employment support, and social connection services at a single location, has already shown promising results at existing sites around the country, with more hubs planned to open in other regional and metropolitan areas over the coming years.

In Queensland, the state government has moved ahead with a new ten-year freight strategy aimed at preparing the state's roads, rail, and port networks for a projected surge in freight movement over the coming decade. The plan sets out around seventy individual actions to be delivered over the next five years, including a new incentive scheme designed to shift a greater share of freight onto rail rather than road, along with discounted fuel access charges for operators using the state's north-west rail line. Officials say the state's overall freight task is projected to grow by roughly twenty per cent by 2032, driven by population growth, expanding resource and agricultural exports, and the broader economic activity expected to accompany preparations for the Olympic and Paralympic Games. Industry groups within the freight and logistics sector have broadly welcomed the strategy, saying better coordination between road, rail, and port infrastructure has long been needed to avoid bottlenecks as demand on the network continues climbing. Transport officials say the plan will be reviewed periodically over its lifespan to make sure it keeps pace with how freight patterns actually evolve, rather than locking in a single fixed approach for the full ten years regardless of how conditions change.

And on the Gold Coast, Bond University graduate Molly Swanson has been named Queensland's Rhodes Scholar for the year, securing one of the world's most prestigious postgraduate scholarships and earning the chance to continue her studies at Oxford University in the United Kingdom. Swanson's academic work has focused on how emerging digital technologies intersect with human rights protections, an area of growing importance as governments and institutions around the world grapple with how best to regulate powerful new tools while safeguarding individual freedoms. The scholarship, which covers the full cost of study at Oxford along with a living allowance, is awarded to just a handful of students across the country each year, making Swanson's selection a significant achievement for both her and the university that nurtured her academic career. Bond University said it was immensely proud to see one of its own graduates recognised at this level, noting the achievement reflects well on the quality of research and mentorship available at the Gold Coast institution. Swanson is expected to begin her studies at Oxford next year, joining a long line of Rhodes Scholars from Australia who've gone on to prominent careers across law, public policy, academia, and international affairs.
