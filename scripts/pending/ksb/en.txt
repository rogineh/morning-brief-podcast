Across Australia, security screeners at Sydney Airport are walking off the job today in two separate windows, from five to eight in the morning and again from four-thirty to seven-thirty this evening, in what's being described as the third round of industrial action at the airport's checkpoints following earlier stoppages in late September and in mid-October. The workers, employed by screening contractor Certis, voted down the company's latest pay offer, with the union representing them saying the dispute centres on the company's push to cut minimum rostered hours. Screening checkpoints are expected to stay open throughout both stoppage windows but to run at reduced capacity, meaning travellers should expect longer queues rather than outright cancellations, and airlines including Qantas, Jetstar, Virgin Australia, and Rex are all allowing passengers flying out of Sydney today to shift their travel to another date without a change fee. Qantas says its terminals will open earlier than usual, from three in the morning, to help manage the expected queues. Travellers heading to Sydney Airport today are being urged to arrive well ahead of their flights and to check directly with their airline for the latest advice before leaving home.

In Queensland, Premier David Crisafulli has used an interview marking two years since his government took office to firmly rule out any future arrangement with One Nation, even as he acknowledged his government still has more work to do on cost-of-living pressures. The Premier reaffirmed that cutting youth crime remains his top priority heading into the back half of his term, pointing to it as the issue that defined his election win and continues to shape his government's agenda. On cost of living specifically, the government has moved away from the broad, universal energy rebates offered under the previous Labor administration, instead shifting toward smaller, more targeted relief measures aimed at specific groups of households, a change some commentators say leaves the government more exposed on an issue voters continue to rank as a top concern. Political analysts quoted in the coverage suggested cost of living could prove a genuine challenge for the government as it heads toward the next state election, particularly given the continued rise in support for One Nation in regional and outer-suburban seats. The Premier's office has previously faced separate questions this year over how a ministerial conduct matter involving two of his colleagues was handled, though today's interview focused mainly on the government's broader record and priorities two years in.

And on the Gold Coast, a key council committee has voted to cap the chief executive's spending authority at one hundred million dollars for major decisions such as land purchases, until councillors formally endorse any deal above that threshold. The move from the Governance, Administration and Finance Committee is being described as a stopgap measure rather than a permanent budget change, coming as the council searches for a permanent replacement for its previous chief executive, who resigned abruptly a few months ago. That departure followed earlier criticism from some councillors over the use of ratepayer funds for overseas travel, which had been described at the time as disproportionately high compared with other councils. The committee's chair said the new cap strikes a balance between keeping proper financial oversight in place and still allowing council staff enough flexibility to do their jobs without unnecessary delays on routine decisions. The proposal still needs to clear a final vote at the full council meeting next month before it can take effect. The spending cap comes against the backdrop of this year's adopted city budget, a two-point-seven billion dollar plan that included an average rates increase of four-point-seven per cent, nearly double the increase ratepayers saw the year before.
