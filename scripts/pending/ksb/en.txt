Across Australia, the Therapeutic Goods Administration has approved a daily tablet version of the weight-loss medication Wegovy, marking the first time a drug from this particular class of medicines has been cleared for use in Australia in oral form rather than as an injection. The approval covers four different strengths of the tablet, and the medicine has been cleared for the same range of uses already approved for the existing injectable version of Wegovy, meaning patients will still need a prescription from their doctor to access it. The manufacturer has indicated it's preparing for an Australian launch later this year, with some reports suggesting the tablet could become available to patients before Christmas, though other industry observers expect broader availability may not arrive until sometime in 2027. Pricing for the Australian market hasn't yet been finalised or publicly released, and the medicine still needs to clear some remaining manufacturing and packaging checks before supply can begin reaching pharmacies. For patients who've found the existing injectable formulation difficult or inconvenient to use, a daily tablet option could significantly expand access to this type of weight-management medication, assuming the eventual price point proves broadly affordable once it does reach the market. Health experts have welcomed the approval as a meaningful step forward for patients who experience needle anxiety or who've struggled to maintain the regular injection schedule the existing formulation requires, while cautioning that the tablet will still need to be taken consistently and under proper medical supervision to achieve the same results seen in clinical trials.

In Queensland, the state's massive Borumba pumped hydro energy storage project remains on hold after authorities confirmed last month that procurement for key exploratory tunnelling and drilling work has been suspended while the government reviews the project's overall business case. The eighteen-point-four-billion-dollar scheme, located near Gympie in the state's south-east, has faced mounting cost pressure in recent months, with the government citing concerns partly linked to high-profile cost blowouts affecting a similar large-scale pumped hydro project interstate. Despite the suspension of the tunnelling and civil works procurement specifically, the body overseeing the project says broader exploratory activities, including geotechnical data collection and ongoing economic evaluation work, are continuing in parallel while the wider review of the business case plays out. Earlier stages of preparatory work had already progressed significantly, including geophysical investigations that began earlier this year and an interim contract covering early enabling works like access tracks and tunnel portal preparation. A revised business case for the project is now expected sometime during the next financial year, pushing back an earlier timeline that had targeted a decision around the middle of this year, leaving the project's ultimate future still genuinely uncertain for now.

And on the Gold Coast, new figures show a modest but growing number of local businesses are successfully diverting food waste away from landfill through a council-run composting program, even as participation in the scheme so far remains relatively limited. Just twenty-three businesses across the city have formally enrolled in the program to date, but those businesses collectively diverted more than six hundred tonnes of food waste from landfill over the past year, representing an increase of roughly fifty per cent compared with the previous twelve-month period. A separate, smaller trial specifically targeting food waste collection from apartment buildings and other multi-unit developments has diverted a further three tonnes of material over its first six months of operation. Council figures also show broader household green waste recovery climbed by almost four thousand tonnes over the past financial year, a result officials have partly credited to improved household education reducing contamination rates in general green waste collections. However, the program faces a genuine funding question heading forward, with state government financial support for the broader food waste initiative set to expire next year, raising questions about how the council might sustain or expand current diversion rates without that ongoing assistance.
