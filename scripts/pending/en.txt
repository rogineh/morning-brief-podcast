In technology, a group of governments and leading AI companies signed onto a new multilateral framework last week aimed at strengthening oversight of the most advanced artificial intelligence systems, in a ceremony hosted by the French president at the Élysée Palace. The so-called Paris Call for AI Safety and Security brings together more than a dozen countries alongside roughly ten major AI developers, including several of the biggest names in the industry, who've committed to disclosing known risks in their systems and submitting to non-binding external audits. Governments involved in the pact have also agreed to establish confidential channels through which employees at AI companies can raise concerns about systemic risks without fear of retaliation, building on similar whistleblower protections floated in earlier international AI safety efforts. The declaration builds directly on a string of previous agreements stretching back several years, including the Hiroshima AI process and the Seoul frontier AI safety commitments, each of which has progressively tried to establish shared international norms around the development of increasingly capable AI systems. Crucially, the new call remains entirely voluntary, with no enforcement mechanism, deadlines, or penalties attached to any of its commitments, meaning its ultimate impact will depend heavily on whether signatory companies and governments follow through in practice. Supporters say even a non-binding framework like this one helps establish a baseline of shared expectations across an industry that's continued to move faster than most national regulatory systems have been able to keep pace with.

Turning to national news, Qantas has confirmed plans to cut roughly eight hundred and fifty corporate roles over the coming months, with much of the affected back-office work being shifted to outsourcing partner Accenture and performed predominantly offshore in India. Chief executive Vanessa Hudson informed staff of the decision directly, with the airline saying the changes amount to around three per cent of its Australian workforce and are expected to begin taking effect from the middle of next year. The move has drawn sharp criticism from the Australian Services Union, which says it had previously been assured there were no plans to send Australian jobs offshore, and which has vowed to fight the restructure through whatever means are available to it. Qantas shares actually rose on the news, with investors apparently viewing the cost reduction favourably even as the decision drew political and union criticism, extending a run of gains that's added significantly to the airline's market value this year. The announcement adds to a broader pattern across the corporate sector in recent months, with other major employers having already flagged similar moves to shift back-office functions overseas in search of cost savings, a trend that unions say is increasingly squeezing white-collar roles in sectors well beyond the traditional blue-collar jobs more commonly associated with offshoring pressures.

In Queensland, the Kangaroos capped off the Rugby League World Cup in style last night, defeating New Zealand thirty-two points to twelve in front of a packed Suncorp Stadium to claim an extraordinary fourth consecutive men's World Cup title. Liam Martin set the tone early, scoring the fastest try in the tournament's history just minutes after kickoff, before winger Jack Bostock added further damage with another try of his own, continuing a prolific run of form that saw him finish the tournament as one of its leading try-scorers. Cameron Munster's accurate goal-kicking helped extend Australia's lead through the middle stages of the match, with the Kangaroos ultimately proving too strong for a spirited New Zealand side across the full eighty minutes. The win extends Australia's unbeaten World Cup streak to twenty-three consecutive matches, a run stretching back to their last defeat in a World Cup final, which came against these same opponents at this same venue all the way back in 2008. Brisbane's hosting of the tournament's grand final weekend capped a hugely successful campaign for Queensland, with sell-out crowds and strong television audiences underlining rugby league's enduring pull across the state, even as attention now turns toward preparations for the next international rugby league calendar.

And on the Gold Coast, the proposed new multi-purpose arena planned for Carey Park at Southport continues advancing through its development process, with the AmplifyGC consortium now working through the formal tender phase after being confirmed earlier this year as the sole shortlisted group for the project. The consortium, which brings together expertise in venue financing, construction, and event programming, is working toward finalising a full project agreement, which is expected to be reached in early 2027, setting up a three-year construction window from there. If that timeline holds, the finished venue would open in 2030, giving organisers a couple of years to stage events there before it serves as one of the city's venues for the Brisbane 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games. Planners say the arena, which is set to hold at least twelve thousand spectators, has been designed from the outset to host everything from major concerts and entertainment through to Olympic-standard sporting competition, reflecting the Gold Coast's growing ambitions as a host city for major events well beyond the Olympics themselves. Local business groups have welcomed the project's steady progress, saying a venue of this scale will meaningfully expand the city's capacity to attract large-scale touring acts and international sporting fixtures that have previously bypassed the Gold Coast in favour of larger capital city venues.
