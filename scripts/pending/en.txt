Turning to national news, official figures released yesterday show Australia's unemployment rate jumped to four point nine per cent in October, its highest level since October 2021, after the economy unexpectedly shed around forty-three thousand jobs during the month. The result caught economists off guard, who'd broadly been expecting a modest gain of roughly twenty-five thousand positions rather than an outright decline, with the bulk of the losses concentrated in part-time employment specifically. The weaker-than-expected data immediately reignited debate over the Reserve Bank's current policy settings, with some economists now arguing the central bank may need to reconsider its recent emphasis on persistent inflation risk in light of clear signs the labour market is cooling more sharply than previously assumed. Youth unemployment also ticked higher, reaching eleven point three per cent, adding to concerns that younger workers are being disproportionately affected as overall hiring activity slows across the economy. Financial markets reacted quickly to the data, with traders increasing their expectations for the central bank to eventually ease interest rates, even as policymakers continue weighing that prospect against inflation that remains above their preferred target range.

In Queensland, early works are expected to begin before the end of the year on the new National Aquatic Centre, one of the centrepiece venues being built ahead of the 2032 Brisbane Olympic and Paralympic Games. The venue's water-inspired design, unveiled earlier this year by the architectural team behind the project, is intended to create both an elite competition facility capable of hosting swimming, diving, water polo, and artistic swimming during the Games, and a long-term community asset once the Olympic program has concluded. Main construction on the venue is scheduled to get under way in 2027, with the facility forming one of seventeen new or upgraded venues being delivered under the broader multi-billion-dollar Games infrastructure program currently under way across the state. Authorities overseeing the Games venues program say every milestone across the broader portfolio was achieved within its approved timeframe and scope over the past financial year, a result they've pointed to as evidence the enormous construction task remains firmly on track this early in the process. With several major venues now moving from design into active construction phases, the coming year is expected to bring a noticeable ramp-up in visible building activity across multiple Olympic project sites simultaneously.

And on the Gold Coast, developer Graya has secured approval for a one-hundred-and-sixty-million dollar beachfront apartment project called Coral on Palm Beach's Jefferson Lane, marking the company's debut venture directly on the beachfront after previously building inland and riverside projects elsewhere on the coast. The nine-storey development will rise on a consolidated site spanning roughly thirty-six metres of beach frontage, after the developer bought out a neighbouring property and redesigned the scheme to make the most of the expanded block within a tight five-week turnaround. Apartments in the building are expected to be priced well into the multi-million-dollar range, reflecting the increasingly premium nature of beachfront development along this stretch of the southern Gold Coast in recent years. Construction is already under way, with completion targeted for December 2028, adding to a wave of boutique, architecturally distinctive developments that have increasingly defined Palm Beach's evolving skyline. Local property analysts say the project's approval, and the broader appetite for premium beachfront product it reflects, underscores just how much buyer demand for exclusive coastal addresses on the Gold Coast has continued strengthening even as broader housing affordability pressures weigh on other segments of the property market.
