Meta is preparing to launch Hatch, a new consumer AI agent platform designed to handle everyday tasks on behalf of users by tapping into services like DoorDash, Etsy, Reddit, Yelp, and Outlook through a customisable dashboard that can function as a fitness tracker, trip planner, or general-purpose assistant. Now to national news here in Australia. Brisbane Lions coach Chris Fagan has stepped down just days after leading the club to a historic third consecutive premiership, telling players and staff he wants to return to Melbourne to be closer to his family after a decade in the job. Fagan, who leaves after a club-record two hundred and forty-three games in charge, described telling the playing group as one of the hardest things he's ever had to do, but said the all-consuming nature of an AFL head coaching role meant now was the right time to step away while the club is on top. His departure sets up what's expected to be one of the most hotly contested coaching vacancies in years, with the Lions widely regarded as genuine premiership contenders again next season. Separately, authorities are warning that south-east Australia is bracing for potential flooding and severe storms over the next two days, a dramatic turnaround after the region recorded its second-driest September on record. Forecasters say a stream of tropical moisture feeding into a low-pressure system over the southern Murray Basin could trigger flash flooding and damaging wind gusts in areas that, until recently, had been desperate for rain, with authorities urging residents in flood-prone areas to monitor conditions closely as the system intensifies. The sudden shift from drought-like conditions to flood risk within the space of a month has caught some farmers off guard, with agricultural groups noting that paddocks hardened by weeks of dry weather may struggle to absorb heavy rainfall quickly, raising the risk of dangerous run-off even in areas that aren't typically considered flood-prone.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. The Toowoomba Carnival of Flowers is winding down its final week, with Australia's longest-running floral festival marking its seventy-seventh year with more than forty million petals splashed across the city's parks and gardens. The festival's final stretch includes the Weekend Table dining series running through until Sunday, featuring a dozen curated dining experiences in scenic spots around the city, along with a second running of the popular Paw Parade for dog owners on Sunday. Major floral displays remain open daily and free to explore in Queens Park Botanic Gardens and Laurel Bank Park, giving visitors a last chance to take in the spectacle before the carnival wraps up on Monday after more than eighty events held across the festival's multi-week run this year. Local tourism operators say the carnival continues to be one of the biggest annual drawcards for the Darling Downs region, bringing visitors from across the state and interstate to see Toowoomba transformed into a sea of colour each spring.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. Organisers have revealed the full program for Gold Coast Open House, with the free weekend event returning on the tenth and eleventh of October to offer rare public access to buildings and spaces normally off-limits to visitors. This year's program includes a behind-the-scenes look at the Hinze Dam and the Gold Coast Desalination Plant at Tugun, which can supply drinking water to three hundred thousand homes a day, as well as a tour of the Gold Coast's Disaster and Emergency Management Centre, built to withstand a category five cyclone. Architecture fans will also be able to explore Bond University's striking Abedian School of Architecture and Griffith University's Perry Cross Spinal Research Institute, while several distinctive private homes around the city, including properties at Ashmore, Currumbin Valley, and Southport, will open their doors for the weekend as well. Organisers say the program is designed to showcase the breadth of design and infrastructure shaping the Gold Coast today, from cutting-edge research facilities to quietly innovative family homes tucked away in the hinterland.
