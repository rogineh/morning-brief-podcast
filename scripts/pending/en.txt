The country's housing market has lost significant momentum heading into what's traditionally the busiest selling period of the year, with the latest weekend auction data showing a national clearance rate of just over forty four per cent, down sharply from above sixty seven per cent at the same time last year. Sydney's clearance rate held up relatively better at just under fifty three per cent, while Brisbane's figure came in markedly weaker at under eighteen per cent, reflecting just how uneven the slowdown has been across different capital cities. Property analysts say the usual spring selling season hasn't delivered its typical lift in activity this year, with buyers increasingly cautious ahead of the Reserve Bank's next interest rate decision in November. Real estate agents say many vendors are still adjusting their price expectations to the new reality, and that properties priced in line with genuine current buyer demand are still selling reasonably well, while overly optimistic listings are increasingly sitting on the market for much longer than sellers might be used to. Buyers' agents say the shift has quietly handed more negotiating leverage back to purchasers in several markets, a genuine change after a long stretch in which sellers had largely been able to dictate terms.

Now, the news from here in Queensland.

A two billion dollar joint funding deal between the federal and state governments is moving ahead, aimed at unlocking enabling infrastructure for as many as fifty one thousand new homes across designated growth areas throughout Queensland. The package combines close to four hundred million dollars in direct grants with one point six billion dollars in zero interest concessional loans, specifically targeted at infrastructure like roads, water, and sewerage that growth areas need in place before large-scale housing construction can actually begin. Named growth corridors receiving funding under the deal include Mount Peter near Cairns, Southern Thornlands in the Redlands, and Waraba in Moreton Bay, reflecting a deliberate spread of investment beyond just the state's biggest cities. Housing industry groups have welcomed the funding as addressing one of the more persistent handbrakes on new supply, saying land with planning approval but no enabling infrastructure has been a recurring and frustrating bottleneck in the state's broader housing affordability challenge. Local councils involved in the growth areas say they're now working through the practical sequencing of the works, since infrastructure like trunk water and sewerage generally needs to be in the ground well before individual house blocks can be released for sale to builders and buyers.
