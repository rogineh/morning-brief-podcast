Good morning, and welcome to your Daily Brief for Thursday, the 8th of October, 2026. Here's what you need to know today, starting with technology and AI.

The Council of Europe and Microsoft have signed a new cooperation agreement focused on artificial intelligence, aiming to align the company's AI development practices more closely with the Council's human rights and rule-of-law standards. The agreement covers areas like data protection safeguards and responsible deployment of AI tools within government services, and officials say it reflects a growing push across Europe to make sure major technology companies are working within established legal and ethical frameworks as AI becomes more deeply embedded in public institutions.

Meanwhile, French AI company Mistral has released a public preview of its newest flagship model, Mistral Large 4, which the company describes as natively multimodal with roughly one trillion total parameters, of which around forty-nine billion are active at any one time. The model is designed to process text, images, and other data types within a single unified system, and Mistral says the new architecture allows it to deliver strong performance while keeping computing costs more manageable than fully dense models of a similar scale. The release adds Mistral to a growing list of labs racing to put out next-generation multimodal systems as competition in the frontier AI space continues to intensify.

Turning now to news from across Australia.

Woolworths customers are feeling the impact of a freight fuel levy surcharge that's nearly tripled since the outbreak of conflict involving Iran earlier this year, as rising global oil prices flow through to the cost of getting goods onto supermarket shelves. Retail analysts say the surcharge is a reminder of just how exposed Australia's supply chains remain to international energy shocks, even for a company the size of Woolworths, and they expect similar pressures to show up gradually across other retailers in the months ahead.

Elsewhere, the Victorian Greens have kicked off their state election campaign with a pledge to tackle high grocery prices by establishing a number of state-owned supermarkets across Melbourne and regional Victoria. Under the proposal, the government-run stores would sell essential items at a steep discount compared to the major chains, with smaller discounts on other goods, and the party says it would also look to give the state's essential services regulator more power to cap prices on key household items. The major parties have been largely dismissive of the idea so far, setting up grocery affordability as an early flashpoint in the lead-up to November's state election.

Now, the news from here in Queensland.

A group of the state's mayors has written an open letter to the Prime Minister, urging the federal government to abandon proposed changes to the natural disaster recovery funding arrangements that councils rely on after floods, cyclones, and bushfires. The mayors argue the changes, as currently proposed, would shift too much of the financial burden back onto local governments and could, in some cases, push smaller councils toward insolvency. The federal government has pushed back on that framing, urging state and local counterparts not to, in its words, stoke unnecessary fear, and says it remains open to further consultation before any final changes are locked in.

On a brighter note, the Savannah in the Round festival kicks off today in Cairns and runs through until Sunday, bringing visitors out to the Gulf Savannah region for a celebration of outback culture, music, and local produce. Organisers say the event has grown steadily in popularity in recent years as more travellers look to combine a Far North Queensland holiday with a taste of genuine outback life, and local tourism operators are hoping for another strong turnout this year.

And finally, here's what's happening on the Gold Coast.

The clock is ticking down on the Gold Coast Suns' decision over departing forward Ben King, with the club now facing a deadline of tomorrow afternoon to decide whether to match Hawthorn's offer and keep him at the club. King has kicked more than three hundred goals in his time at the Suns, and while speculation has swirled for months that he wants to join Hawthorn, the club has given no public indication of which way it's leaning as the deadline approaches. Whatever the outcome, the pick Gold Coast receives in compensation if it lets him go is expected to be a significant piece of this year's trade period.

In lighter news, organisers of the Boost Mobile Gold Coast 500 have unveiled the full entertainment lineup for this year's race weekend, running from the twenty-third to the twenty-fifth of October in Surfers Paradise. A new music precinct on Macintosh Island, called Sound Track, will host headline sets from Sneaky Sound System, Bag Raiders, and Havana Brown across the weekend, alongside a run of supporting DJs and live acts between on-track sessions. Organisers say the new precinct is designed to give fans somewhere to keep the party going between races, building on what's already one of the biggest events on the Gold Coast's annual calendar.
