In technology, the British artificial intelligence infrastructure firm now known as Era4, originally launched as Carbon3.ai, continues pushing ahead with its billion-pound plan to build the United Kingdom's first fully renewable-powered data centre network. The company's strategy centres on converting legacy industrial and energy sites, including landfill gas facilities and other renewable generation assets, into data centre capacity rather than building entirely new sites from scratch, an approach it says allows it to move faster while sidestepping some of the grid connection bottlenecks that have slowed other data centre projects across the country. The firm has already committed tens of millions of pounds to its flagship site in Derbyshire, which draws power from a nearby landfill gas and renewable energy facility, and has set its sights on developing a nationwide network of more than thirty similar sites over the coming years. Industry analysts say the approach reflects a broader rethink across the data centre sector, as developers increasingly look for ways to secure reliable renewable power and grid access quickly rather than waiting years for new large-scale connections to be built from the ground up. The company's leadership, which includes a former senior UK government AI policy official, says the strategy is also designed to support what it calls sovereign AI infrastructure, ensuring the country retains greater domestic control over the compute capacity needed to run increasingly important AI systems rather than relying entirely on infrastructure owned and operated offshore.

The Reserve Bank has signalled it's paying particularly close attention to whether a softer jobs market translates into weaker wage growth and, in turn, easier inflation pressure, or whether other global and domestic cost pressures continue to outweigh any relief from a cooling labour market. Business groups say the data presents a mixed picture for employers, with some sectors continuing to report difficulty finding workers even as the overall unemployment rate drifts higher, reflecting ongoing mismatches between where job seekers are located and where vacancies actually exist. Treasury officials say they continue to expect the labour market to moderate gradually rather than sharply in the period ahead, consistent with the broader goal of bringing inflation back within target without triggering a sharp rise in unemployment.

In Queensland, the state government has released a new batch of water availability studies covering Cape York, the Burdekin Basin, and the Pioneer Valley, identifying more than one hundred and thirty thousand megalitres of additional water that could potentially be unlocked for irrigators and other users across the state's north. The bulk of that volume centres on the Burdekin Basin, where authorities say a significant opportunity exists to release water in the Belyando-Suttor catchment, subject to further planning and consultation with local irrigators and other stakeholders. In Cape York, the recommendations point to smaller, more targeted releases across several catchments, alongside efforts to cut red tape for landholders seeking to access water entitlements in that region. The Pioneer Valley findings take a different approach, focusing less on unlocking new water volumes and more on improving how existing water is used, including support for water trading arrangements and opportunities tied to the region's broader economic development plans. Water authorities say the studies form part of a broader, ongoing program aimed at better understanding where additional water supply genuinely exists across the state, with officials cautioning that identifying a potential volume on paper is only the first step before any water can actually be made available to users.
