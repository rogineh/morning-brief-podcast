Opposition figures, however, have seized on projections suggesting wholesale electricity prices could rise substantially in the coming years, arguing that current relief measures are merely delaying rather than solving a deeper affordability problem tied to the pace of the energy transition. The government has pushed back on those projections, saying its broader investment in renewable energy and transmission infrastructure is the right long-term path to bringing prices down, even if the transition involves short-term cost pressures. The exchange is the latest round in an ongoing political contest over energy policy that's likely to remain a significant point of difference between the major parties heading into coming budget and election cycles, with households on both sides of the debate anxious for clearer, more durable answers on where power bills are headed.

In Queensland, the state government has unveiled a new masterplan for the redevelopment of the Prince Charles Hospital in Brisbane's north, promising at least one hundred additional beds, an expanded emergency department, upgraded cardiac catheterisation labs, and a new dedicated mental health facility. The masterplan follows an independent review that was highly critical of an earlier expansion attempt at the hospital, which was found to have blown out in cost by hundreds of millions of dollars while falling well behind schedule under the previous administration. Health officials say the new plan has been designed to avoid the mistakes identified in that review, with a more realistic staged approach intended to deliver genuine capacity increases without the budget blowouts that plagued the earlier failed attempt. Public consultation on the masterplan is now open, with patients, carers, and local residents invited to complete a short survey on the proposed designs before submissions close in early December. The hospital serves a large and growing catchment across Brisbane's northern suburbs, and health advocates say the additional capacity is sorely needed given population growth in the area has consistently outpaced the hospital's ability to expand its services in recent years.

The timing is notable, with the city now entering its traditional peak summer season, when visitor numbers typically climb further as school leavers, holidaymakers, and families flock to the coast over the summer months. Local tourism bodies say they're optimistic the current season can build on last year's record result, pointing to continued growth in direct international flight connections and sustained investment in major attractions and accommodation as reasons for confidence heading into the busiest stretch of the year.
