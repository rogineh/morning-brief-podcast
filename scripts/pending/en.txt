In the United States, businesses are grappling with the fallout from a new set of worker-protection laws signed by California Governor Gavin Newsom at the end of last month, specifically targeting the risks posed by artificial intelligence in the workplace. The new rules bring employment screening, worker monitoring, task scoring, and productivity tracking systems under far greater scrutiny, requiring employers using AI tools to make hiring or performance decisions to be more transparent about how those systems work and give workers clearer avenues to challenge automated decisions. Given California's size and influence, employment lawyers say the laws are likely to shape how AI vendors design their products for the rest of the country, not just for companies operating in that state.

Elsewhere in the tech world, India Mobile Congress opened in New Delhi, a four-day event jointly organised by the country's Department of Telecommunications and its Cellular Operators Association. This year's tenth edition of the conference has put a heavy focus on next-generation sixth-generation mobile networks, AI-native telecommunications infrastructure, satellite communications, and quantum communications, alongside more familiar themes like cybersecurity and robotics. Organisers say the event reflects India's ambition to play a bigger role in setting global standards for the next generation of telecommunications technology, rather than simply adopting standards set elsewhere.

Turning now to news from across Australia.

All eyes are on Mount Panorama this weekend, where Brodie Kostecki is chasing a piece of Supercars history at the Bathurst 1000. The West Australian driver has claimed pole position in each of the last three editions of the race, putting him level with the legendary Peter Brock and Allan Moffat for the most consecutive poles at the Great Race. Should Kostecki top Saturday's top-ten shootout, he'll become the first driver in the Gen3 era to claim four poles in a row, and he goes into the weekend as the outright favourite, having never been beaten in a shootout since the current generation of cars was introduced. His Dick Johnson Racing team will be without his usual race engineer this weekend, after a mountain bike accident, but Kostecki says the team is well prepared regardless.

In other news, tourists hoping to visit the Twelve Apostles along Victoria's Great Ocean Road will soon need to book ahead and pay a fee, as authorities look to manage the enormous crowds the iconic rock formations attract every year. Officials say the new booking system is designed to spread visitor numbers more evenly across the day and reduce overcrowding at the lookout points, while also raising funds that can be put back into maintaining the site's facilities.

As organisers continue working through the finer details of the Brisbane twenty thirty-two Olympic and Paralympic Games, it's not just the host city getting involved. Outback Queensland artisans and performers, some more than a thousand kilometres from Brisbane, are putting their hands up to be part of the Games, taking the state government's promise that the event will be "for all of Queensland" to heart. An opal miner from the state's gem fields says it would be a dream to see Australia's famous boulder opals showcased on the world stage, given the country supplies more than ninety per cent of the world's opal. In the outback town of Winton, the co-founder of a comedy and circus troupe is training a team of whip crackers in the hope they might perform during the opening or closing ceremonies, while a long-running regional toy maker known for its handmade teddy bears has pitched a koala mascot for the Games. It's a reminder that for a Games still six years away, towns far from the Gabba and the new Olympic venues are already finding ways to get a piece of the action.

And finally, here's what's happening on the Gold Coast.

Construction is set to begin on a key piece of the Faster Rail project linking Logan and the Gold Coast, with work starting on replacing the Kuraby level crossing as part of a broader five point seven five billion dollar upgrade jointly funded by the state and federal governments. The project will eventually double the number of tracks between Kuraby and Beenleigh from two to four, remove five level crossings along the twenty-kilometre corridor, and upgrade several stations, all aimed at cutting travel times and easing congestion on one of the busiest rail corridors in the south-east.

And the Gold Coast Suns' AFL trade saga has taken another step, with Hawthorn formally lodging its free agency paperwork to sign forward Ben King. As a restricted free agent, the move gives Gold Coast three days to decide whether to match Hawthorn's offer and keep King at the club, or allow the move to proceed and accept pick number seven as compensation. The decision is shaping up as one of the most closely watched calls of this year's trade period, with the Suns' list management team weighing up King's value against what they could build with an early first-round selection instead.
