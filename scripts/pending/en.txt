In technology, OpenAI has begun rolling out two new models, Astra and Sol, continuing the rapid pace of releases among the major AI companies this year. Astra is positioned as the company's frontier model for its hardest and most demanding projects, while Sol is pitched as a faster, more cost-effective option designed for everyday professional work. Both models are now available to paying ChatGPT subscribers and enterprise customers, as well as to developers building their own applications through the company's programming interface. The company says Astra shows a meaningful improvement over its predecessor on internal evaluations of real-world professional tasks, while using noticeably fewer computing resources to reach those results, and Sol shows a smaller but still solid improvement over the model it replaces. Independent testing has been more mixed, with some evaluators ranking Astra behind rival models from competing AI labs on certain benchmarks, even as it performs strongly on others. Of particular note, the company says Astra is the first model it has released publicly that meets its internal threshold for posing a meaningful risk around assisting with cyberattacks, prompting additional safeguards and monitoring around how the model can be used for security-related tasks. The releases underscore how competitive the AI sector remains heading into next year, with each major lab continuing to push out new models every few weeks as they compete on price, capability, and safety credentials all at once.

Turning to national news, Virgin Australia is pushing to lock in long-term approval for its twice-daily flights between Sydney and Doha, as the airline looks to cement a partnership with Qatar Airways that has reshaped its international network in recent years. The application, lodged with the regulator that allocates international flying rights, would put the combined Virgin and Qatar schedule on the Sydney route close to the frequency Emirates already offers on its own daily service out of Dubai. Virgin had scaled back some of its Doha capacity earlier in the year as conflict in the Middle East led to temporary airspace closures across the region, but the airline says conditions have since stabilised enough to justify locking in the expanded schedule for the longer term. Separately, the airline has confirmed that its Brisbane-to-Doha and Perth-to-Doha services will resume in early December, restoring routes that had been paused during the disruption. Industry analysts say the push reflects how central the Qatar Airways partnership has become to Virgin's broader strategy of offering Australians more competitive long-haul options to Europe and the Middle East, particularly as the two airlines have deepened their codeshare arrangements over the past couple of years. The development comes as both of Australia's major airline groups continue adjusting capacity and loyalty offerings heading into the busy summer travel period.

In Queensland, communities across the state's southeast are continuing clean-up efforts after a severe storm earlier this month that produced more than one hundred and twenty thousand lightning strikes and cut power to close to eighty thousand homes and businesses in a single night. The storm brought down more than four hundred power lines across the region, with the hardest-hit areas including the Scenic Rim, Moreton Bay, Gympie, and parts of greater Brisbane, and energy crews worked around the clock for days afterward to restore supply to affected customers. The event has reinforced warnings from the Bureau of Meteorology that this summer's severe weather season could bring an unusually volatile mix of cyclones, flooding, and extreme heat to the state, with forecasters pointing to warm ocean temperatures and shifting climate patterns as key drivers of the heightened risk. State authorities have renewed their calls for households to finalise emergency plans ahead of the peak of the season, pointing to resources available through the state's Get Ready Queensland program as a starting point for anyone yet to prepare. Emergency services say the scale and intensity of this month's storm, arriving well before the traditional peak of the wet season, is itself a reminder that severe weather risk in Queensland no longer follows as predictable a calendar as it once did. Insurers have also flagged an increase in storm-related claims from the affected regions, adding to pressure on household premiums already facing upward pressure across the state.

And on the Gold Coast, developer Bastion Group has secured approval for a thirty-storey residential tower in the heart of Surfers Paradise, adding another significant project to the city's already busy pipeline of high-rise developments. The two-hundred-million-dollar tower, named Surfer and designed around a concept inspired by a stack of surfboards, will rise on an amalgamated site just metres from Northcliffe Beach and the light rail line, with construction expected to begin in the first quarter of next year. The development will include a gym, swimming pool, private spas, and an owners' lounge, with landscaping designed by a well-known Australian garden personality, and will be managed long-term by the developer's own property management arm once complete. The approval forms part of a broader five-hundred-million-dollar development pipeline the company has underway across the Gold Coast, reflecting continued strong demand for apartments in tightly held coastal precincts where new development sites have become increasingly scarce. Industry figures say Surfers Paradise in particular continues to attract significant development interest despite rising construction costs, with buyers showing sustained appetite for well-located towers close to the beach and the light rail network. Local business groups say each new approval of this scale reinforces confidence in the long-term strength of the Gold Coast property market heading into the new year.
