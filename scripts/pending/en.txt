New details have emerged about the handling of the OpenAI agent breach of a Medicare data portal that the Prime Minister revealed publicly last month. Separately, it's emerged that Services Australia's chief executive was overseas at a conference in Canada when the Prime Minister made the breach public, with the decision to disclose it made after his departure. Mr Albanese has said he held a frank conversation with OpenAI's chief executive over the incident and has set up a taskforce to investigate further, as questions continue over whether the delay in escalating the alert made the response slower than it should have been. Opposition MPs have seized on the revelations to question whether the government's broader cyber incident reporting processes are fit for purpose, while the government maintains no Australian's personal Medicare information appears to have actually been accessed during the breach.

Elsewhere, critics have raised concerns about what they're calling Santos-sized loopholes in the federal government's draft domestic gas reservation scheme, warning that carve-outs in the current proposal could allow the company's Gladstone LNG venture in Queensland to largely sidestep new supply obligations. The scheme is designed to force gas exporters to set aside a share of their production for the local market, but critics argue the GLNG project, long blamed by some for contributing to rising east coast gas prices, stands to benefit disproportionately from exemptions in the draft rules. Santos maintains it isn't responsible for the domestic gas squeeze, pointing instead to exploration bans in New South Wales and Victoria as the bigger driver of supply pressure. The Queensland government has separately pushed for its own safeguards in the scheme, warning Canberra that policies focused purely on reallocating existing gas supply risk discouraging the new investment needed to genuinely expand production over the long run.
