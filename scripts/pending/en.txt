In technology, Microsoft is winding down a major cloud computing deal it struck with Oracle back in 2023, after quietly declining to renew the multi-year arrangement that originally saw Microsoft rent artificial intelligence computing capacity to help power its AI-assisted Bing search engine. According to reporting based on people familiar with the matter, Microsoft has been progressively switching off the rented computing capacity in recent months, with the underlying reason apparently coming down to the fact that Oracle's hardware relied on an older generation of Nvidia chips that no longer suited Microsoft's evolving technical needs. Oracle has pushed back on any suggestion the move reflects weakening demand for cloud computing capacity more broadly, with a company spokesperson insisting demand for its infrastructure remains strong, including continued interest in even its older-generation computing hardware. Despite that reassurance, Oracle's share price fell around four per cent on the news, hitting a fresh fifty-two-week low as investors weighed what the decision might signal about the broader durability of multi-billion-dollar cloud partnerships underpinning the current AI boom. Industry analysts say the move forms part of a much broader reassessment happening across the technology sector right now, as major companies continue reviewing exactly which cloud computing arrangements actually make sense once newer, more capable chips become available elsewhere.

Turning to national news, the federal government is pressing ahead with plans to sell off sixty-seven defence properties across the country, a divestment expected to raise around three billion dollars in gross proceeds as the government looks to redirect defence spending toward other strategic priorities. After accounting for remediation costs and other expenses tied to preparing the sites for sale, the government expects the divestment to ultimately deliver a net benefit of somewhere around one-point-eight billion dollars to public coffers. The bulk of the properties earmarked for sale, roughly forty-eight of the sixty-seven sites, are concentrated across Victoria, New South Wales, Tasmania, and the Australian Capital Territory, with the Defence Minister arguing resources need to shift toward strategically significant locations in the country's north and west instead. The opposition has strongly criticised the plan, describing it as effectively selling off the heart and soul of Australia's defence force, while commentators have also pointed to a degree of political awkwardness given the Prime Minister's own past parliamentary speeches opposing privatisation of some of the very harbourside land now slated for sale. A Senate inquiry examining the broader management of defence estate assets has already received close to two hundred submissions, with sales themselves not expected to proceed quickly given the detailed assessment still required around contamination, heritage restrictions, and personnel relocation at each individual site.

In Queensland, authorities are warning of a severe heatwave continuing to build across much of the state's north, with temperatures forecast to climb into the high thirties and low forties across inland areas and western parts of Cape York as the extreme heat persists into early next week. Towns including Aurukun, Croydon, Kowanyama, and Weipa are among those expected to be hit hardest, with overnight temperatures also remaining unusually high, offering residents little meaningful relief from the heat even after the sun goes down. Separate heatwave warnings have also been issued covering the Northern Goldfields, Upper Flinders, Herbert, and Lower Burdekin regions, reflecting just how widely the current heat event is spreading across the state's northern and inland regions simultaneously. Health authorities are urging residents across all affected areas to stay well hydrated, avoid unnecessary strenuous outdoor activity during the hottest parts of the day, and check in regularly on elderly neighbours, friends, and family members who may be more vulnerable to heat-related illness. While the most severe heat remains concentrated in the state's north for now, authorities say a separate storm risk continues further south and east, meaning Queensland is simultaneously contending with genuinely different, geographically distinct weather hazards playing out across the state at the same time.

Tszyu will face Cuban fighter Erislandy Lara for the belt at Robina Stadium in a fight now scheduled for mid-December, with promoters confirming an undercard featuring several other significant bouts, including a middleweight clash and a world title defence in the junior lightweight division. The announcement continues the Gold Coast's recent emergence as a genuinely significant venue for major Australian boxing promotions, following a string of previous high-profile fight cards staged at the same venue over recent years. Local tourism and hospitality operators are already anticipating a substantial boost from fight week itself, with major boxing cards of this calibre typically drawing significant numbers of interstate and sometimes international visitors to the city for several days surrounding the event. While the fight itself remains more than a month away, excitement is already building locally, with ticket demand expected to be strong given Tszyu's profile and the significance of a genuine world title fight being staged on home soil.
