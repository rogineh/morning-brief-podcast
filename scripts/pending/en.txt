Good morning, and welcome to your Daily Brief for Thursday, the 1st of October, 2026. Here's what's happening today across technology, the nation, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

First, technology and AI. Apple is preparing to expand Siri's AI capabilities to French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish as part of the upcoming iOS twenty-seven point two update, which is currently in testing and expected to land before the end of the month, building on the more conversational, generative AI-powered version of the assistant that first rolled out to English speakers earlier this year. The move is seen as an important step for Apple in closing the gap with rivals in markets outside the English-speaking world, where competitors have generally been quicker to roll out their most capable assistants. Meanwhile, Google's DeepMind division has confirmed its next major model, Gemini four, has now entered what's known as the post-training stage of development, the phase where a model's raw capabilities are fine-tuned and aligned before public release, with the company indicating the model is on track to arrive before the end of the year, though no specific date has been set. Analysts say the timing sets up a busy final quarter for the AI industry, with several major labs expected to jockey for attention with new releases in the lead-up to the holiday period, continuing a year that has already seen an unusually rapid cadence of model launches from companies large and small.

Now to national news here in Australia. A new national emergency warning system called AusAlert has officially gone live today, replacing the ageing SMS-based Emergency Alert system that's been in use for more than a decade. Unlike its predecessor, AusAlert uses cell broadcast technology to send location-based warnings directly to compatible mobile phones without relying on a single mobile tower, making it far more resilient during disasters where parts of the network might be damaged or overloaded with traffic. Australians don't need to register or download anything to receive the alerts, which will sound a distinctive siren-like tone for about ten seconds when a critical or priority warning is issued for their area, and while some states and territories will run the new system alongside the old one for a transition period, the changeover is due to be completed everywhere by next July. Separately, a major reform to disability support for young children took effect today, with the new Thriving Kids program beginning to provide general supports, including a dedicated autism helpline, for children aged eight and under with developmental delay or autism and low to moderate support needs. The four billion dollar program, jointly funded by state, territory, and federal governments, is designed to work alongside the National Disability Insurance Scheme rather than replace it entirely, with more intensive supports such as playgroups and allied health therapies to be phased in gradually over the next two years, and officials stressing that children with high support needs will continue to access the NDIS as they always have.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. It's a big day for anyone hoping to find love on the land, with nominations officially opening today for Queensland Country Life's Bush Bachelor and Bachelorette competition, which returns to the Bell Races on Saturday the second of January for its latest instalment. Any single person aged between eighteen and thirty-five is eligible to enter or be nominated, with five male and five female finalists to be chosen from the pool of applicants before readers vote for the eventual winners, who'll take home two thousand dollars in cash plus prizes, while runners-up receive a smaller cash and prize package. Nominations remain open until the twenty-eighth of October, and organisers say the competition has become something of an institution in rural Queensland social calendars, helping connect singles from properties and towns often hundreds of kilometres apart while raising the profile of the Bell Races itself as a highlight of the regional event calendar each summer.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. The BLEACH Festival has officially thrown open its doors today, kicking off eleven days of arts and culture across the city under new artistic director Felix Preval. The festival opened at dawn with THAW, a monumental outdoor performance staged on the Surfers Paradise foreshore by acclaimed physical theatre company Legs on the Wall, drawing early risers down to the beach to watch performers move against the rising sun in a piece organisers describe as one of the most ambitious opening events in the festival's history. With close to fifty free and ticketed events still to come over the next week and a half, featuring more than two hundred and ninety artists from the Gold Coast, around Australia, and overseas, locals are being encouraged to make the most of the free program in these opening days before ticketed headline acts, including Kiwi favourites SIX60, take over the HOTA precinct later in the festival's run. Festival organisers say the shift to an October date this year is designed to capture the city's spring energy at its peak, and they're encouraging residents and visitors alike to get down to the free events early before the ticketed program ramps up later in the festival's run.
