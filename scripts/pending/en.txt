First, technology and AI. President Trump has named four senior officials to lead a newly created Super Intelligence Force, a body tasked with coordinating the federal government's dealings with consumers, public interest groups, religious organisations, critical infrastructure providers, and the major AI companies themselves. The group will be led by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, Federal Trade Commission chairman Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon research chief and chief technology officer Emil Michael, and Office of Personnel Management director Scott Kupor, reporting directly to the president and his chief of staff. Notably, the body has no statutory authority, no dedicated budget, and no formal place within the existing structure of government, leaving its practical powers somewhat unclear even as it takes on a high-profile coordinating role. Observers have also pointed to a potential conflict of interest in the appointment of Ferguson, who as FTC chairman recently opened an investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic, and the research non-profit METR, even as he's now also tasked with coordinating the government's broader relationship with those same companies. The announcement adds yet another layer to Washington's rapidly evolving approach to AI oversight, coming in the same week that OpenAI pulled its next flagship model over safety concerns and regulators opened a fresh probe into the industry's biggest players. Analysts say the lack of formal legal authority behind the new body means its real influence will likely come down to how closely the AI companies choose to engage with it voluntarily, rather than any power to compel changes in how they develop or deploy their systems.

Now to national news here in Australia. The Sydney Roosters have won an instant classic of an NRL grand final, edging out the Newcastle Knights nineteen to eighteen at Accor Stadium in a match that swung back and forth until the final minutes. Newcastle raced to an early twelve-nil lead courtesy of tries to Francis Manuleleua and a scorching ninety-five-metre run from Kalyn Ponga, only for the Roosters to claw their way back in the second half, levelling the scores before a Mark Nawaqanitawase try put them in front. With the match locked up late, skipper Daly Cherry-Evans slotted a field goal from twenty metres out to push the Roosters seven points clear, and although Newcastle hit back with a try and successful sideline conversion to make it a one-point game in the dying minutes, captain James Tedesco produced two crucial defensive plays under the high ball to seal the win and deny the Knights their first men's premiership in twenty-five years. It caps off a remarkable turnaround for Newcastle, who went from finishing last on the ladder just twelve months ago to within a whisker of a famous premiership triumph.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. Brisbane Lions fans are coming to terms with the departure of dual Brownlow medallist Lachie Neale, who has officially signed with Collingwood on a three-year deal after requesting a trade away from the club, with the Lions set to receive pick thirty-nine in this year's draft as compensation for losing one of the competition's most decorated midfielders. Neale's exit is one of several big-name moves to headline this year's trade period, with former Greater Western Sydney captain Toby Greene also completing a high-profile switch to Geelong after fifteen seasons with the Giants, netting his old club a second-round draft pick in return. Football commentators say Neale's departure leaves a significant gap in Brisbane's engine room after his central role in the club's recent premiership success, though list managers at the Lions say the draft compensation and existing depth in their midfield group should help soften the blow heading into next season. Elsewhere, Queensland Police say they're continuing to seize an average of six illegal e-mobility devices every single day, underlining just how persistent the enforcement effort against non-compliant e-bikes and e-scooters has had to be since tougher rules took effect earlier this year. Officers say most of the seized devices fail basic safety checks around motor cut-out speeds or throttle-only operation, with the ongoing crackdown aimed squarely at reducing the number of serious injuries linked to high-powered, non-compliant devices on footpaths and shared paths across the state.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. Today's King's Birthday public holiday is being marked quietly across the city, with no central parade, ceremony, or fireworks display planned locally, leaving residents to mark the occasion in their own way with a day at the beach, a trip to one of the major theme parks, or simply a relaxed day off. Movie World, Sea World, Dreamworld, Wet'n'Wild, and Currumbin Wildlife Sanctuary are all expected to operate as normal today, giving families one last chance to make the most of the long weekend before schools, offices, and most government services reopen tomorrow. Banks, council customer service centres, and the bulk of Australia Post outlets are closed for the public holiday, so locals with errands to run are being reminded to plan around the closures. With spring weather holding up nicely across the city, beaches and coastal paths are expected to stay busy throughout the day as the Gold Coast wraps up what's been an unusually eventful long weekend of sport, music, and community events.
