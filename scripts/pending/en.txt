First, technology and AI. Google DeepMind has published a new watermarking system called SynthID Bio in the journal Nature, designed to embed invisible, verifiable signatures directly into AI-designed protein sequences and their predicted three-dimensional structures. The system addresses a growing biosecurity gap, since screening software used by DNA synthesis companies has traditionally had no reliable way to tell whether an incoming order describes a naturally occurring protein or one generated by an AI model, a blind spot researchers worry could be exploited to disguise harmful designs such as toxins or altered viral proteins. DeepMind says laboratory testing shows the watermarked designs perform just as well and retain the same natural diversity as unmarked versions, with the signature remaining detectable on the physical, synthesised protein itself rather than only in its digital blueprint, allowing synthesis providers to verify a design's origin using a dedicated key before manufacturing it. The company has been upfront that resistance to deliberate tampering remains an unresolved challenge, but researchers in the biosecurity field have broadly welcomed the approach as a meaningful step toward closing a gap that's become more pressing as AI protein design tools grow more powerful and widely accessible. DeepMind says it is now working with synthesis providers and biosecurity bodies to explore how the watermarking standard might eventually be adopted more broadly across the industry, rather than remaining limited to models the company builds itself, arguing that a gap in coverage anywhere in the supply chain would still leave room for the very risks the technology is designed to catch.
