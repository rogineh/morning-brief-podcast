In technology, OpenAI is reportedly closing in on a new funding round of up to thirty billion dollars at a pre-money valuation of one and a quarter trillion dollars, in a deal led by investors from the United Arab Emirates alongside the investment firm Coatue. If completed at the full size, the round would make OpenAI the most valuable private company in history, and is widely seen as a bridge toward an eventual public listing the company has already confidentially filed for, though no firm date has been set. Reports suggest the one and a quarter trillion dollar figure represents a step down from an earlier target closer to one and a half trillion dollars, after some prospective investors pushed back on the higher price, with the company said to be hoping to lock in commitments from backers within the coming week or two. The company's own chief financial officer has pointed to sometime in 2027 as a more likely window for an actual stock market debut, and OpenAI's chief executive has previously said publicly that right now would be an unwise moment to list, suggesting the company sees this latest private raise as a way to keep building out computing capacity without rushing prematurely into public markets. The sheer scale of the numbers involved continues to fuel debate about whether private investors are pricing in realistic long-term expectations for the AI industry, or whether valuations across the sector have become detached from what any of these companies can plausibly be expected to earn in the near term.

Turning to national news, the United Nations has issued a formal warning to energy giant Chevron after satellite monitoring detected unusually high methane emissions above its Gorgon liquefied natural gas project on Barrow Island, off the coast of Western Australia. The warning came after the UN's methane observatory recorded elevated readings across five consecutive satellite passes between last November and this past August, with the most recent detection flagged in late August triggering the formal notification sent to both the company and government authorities. Chevron has pushed back on the characterisation, saying satellite observations only capture a single moment in time and carry a wide range of uncertainty, while also confirming it's now working with the UN's environment programme to address the concerns raised. Western Australia's environmental regulator says it's aware of the reports and is investigating what compliance or regulatory steps might be required as a result, though the UN's own environment programme says it hasn't yet received any formal response from either Chevron or the federal government addressing the specific readings. It's not the first time Gorgon has drawn this kind of attention, with Chevron having been notified about comparable methane readings at the same site back in July of last year, raising fresh questions about how effectively monitoring and remediation efforts have worked since then.

In Queensland, the Rugby League World Cup has kicked off with opening matches already played, and excitement building ahead of host nation Australia's clash with Fiji at Suncorp Stadium in Brisbane on Sunday evening. The tournament is being staged across a wide spread of venues this year, including fixtures to be played in Queensland beyond Brisbane itself, at the Gold Coast and in Townsville, alongside host cities in several other states and even matches across the Tasman in New Zealand. Organisers say the broad geographic spread is designed to let rugby league heartland communities right across the country experience world cup matches in person, rather than concentrating every fixture in just one or two major stadiums. Local tourism operators in regional and northern parts of the state say they're expecting a solid boost in visitor numbers in host cities as fans travel to follow the various national teams through the tournament's pool stages over the coming weeks. For host venues like Townsville, landing world cup matches is also being framed by local boosters as a chance to showcase their stadiums and hospitality on a genuinely global stage, well beyond the usual domestic rugby league season.

And on the Gold Coast, the Boost Mobile Gold Coast 500 is in full swing this weekend, with organisers expecting close to two hundred thousand visitors to pack into Surfers Paradise across the three day Supercars event. Significant sections of the Gold Coast Highway and surrounding side streets remain closed to general traffic for the duration of the event, and police have been urging local drivers and visitors alike to factor in considerable extra travel time if they need to move through or around the precinct this weekend. The event remains one of the most significant fixtures on the city's annual calendar, drawing motorsport fans from right across the country to the purpose-built street circuit along the beachfront, with flow-on benefits for local hotels, restaurants, and bars that typically see a major surge in trade during race weekend. Local business groups say the economic impact of hosting an event of this size continues to make the disruption worthwhile overall, even as some residents in the immediate vicinity express ongoing frustration over road closures and noise during the lead-up and the race itself.
