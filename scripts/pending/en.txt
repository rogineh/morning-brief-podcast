Now to national news here in Australia. Brisbane have completed one of the greatest comebacks in AFL grand final history, overturning a seventeen-point deficit late in the final quarter to beat Fremantle by seven points, ninety-six to eighty-nine, in front of just over one hundred thousand fans at the MCG, securing an historic third consecutive premiership. The Lions become only the third team this century to complete such a three-peat, joining their own famous early-two-thousands side and Hawthorn's mid-twenty-tens dynasty, with forward Logan Morris kicking the match-turning goals in the dying minutes after Fremantle had appeared destined for a maiden flag. Fremantle fans and players alike were left devastated by the manner of the loss, having led comfortably deep into the final term, while Brisbane's players described the fightback as the product of sheer refusal to accept defeat regardless of the scoreboard. The result cements the current Lions side among the most successful in the club's history, and has already prompted debate among commentators over where this era of Brisbane football ranks against the greatest dynasties the competition has ever produced. Separately, the Australian Army has formally retired its fleet of Tiger armed reconnaissance helicopters after twenty-two years of service, marking the occasion with a farewell flyover of Darwin before the aircraft are replaced by Apache helicopters as part of a broader capability upgrade expected to be completed by the end of the decade. Veterans of the Tiger program marked the occasion with a formal sundown ceremony, reflecting on more than two decades of reconnaissance and support missions as the regiment now begins the process of relocating and retraining on the new aircraft.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. Urgent dredging works are underway at Moore Park Beach north of Bundaberg, where roughly five metres of foreshore has washed away since Tropical Cyclone Alfred struck last year, with a further major flood earlier this year pushing the mouth of Moore Park Creek some two hundred metres north and prompting the regional council to intervene. The dredging operation will close off part of the shifted creek mouth while opening a new channel further south, with the displaced sand used to renourish the eroded beach, though experts caution that dredging alone may only offer a temporary fix given how much more frequent and powerful extreme weather events have become along the Queensland coast. Separately, former Broncos and Origin great Darius Boyd has been confirmed as Queensland's new State of Origin coach, taking over from Billy Slater following this year's series and inheriting a squad looking to reassert its dominance over New South Wales in the years ahead. Boyd, who won multiple premierships and Origin series as a player before moving into coaching and media roles, said he was honoured to take on the job and would be spending the coming months assessing the playing group before selections begin next year.
