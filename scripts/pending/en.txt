First, technology and AI. Anthropic's confidential prospectus ahead of a planned stock market listing has revealed the sheer scale of its ambitions, with the AI lab outlining plans to spend more than five hundred and eighteen billion dollars on cloud computing and infrastructure in the years ahead as it races to keep pace with demand for its Claude models. The filing shows revenue grew more than twelvefold last year to nearly four point six billion dollars, even as the company posted an operating loss of more than eight billion dollars and a net loss of forty-two billion dollars once various financial obligations were accounted for. Compute and infrastructure costs alone came to more than seven billion dollars last year, almost triple what the company spent the year before, underlining just how capital-intensive the race to build ever more powerful AI systems has become. Analysts say a public listing could ultimately value the company at more than two trillion dollars, reflecting a bet that artificial intelligence will reshape the global economy as profoundly as electrification or the internet, though the prospectus also acknowledges plenty of risk, noting that many of the company's largest customers aren't locked into long-term contracts and that a handful of clients account for a disproportionate share of its cash reserves. The disclosure adds to a frenetic week for the AI industry, coming just a day after chip maker AMD confirmed its eight billion dollar purchase of the startup World Labs, underscoring how much capital is now flowing into the sector from every direction.

Now to national news here in Australia. The Greens are bracing for a leadership contest after long-serving leader Larissa Waters stepped down from the role, with New South Wales senator David Shoebridge declining to rule himself out of the running when asked directly by reporters. Shoebridge, a prominent figure on the party's left who has built a public profile through his work on digital rights and government transparency, is considered one of several senators weighing a tilt at the leadership in the weeks ahead, alongside other potential contenders from within the party room. The contest comes at a delicate moment for the Greens, who have spent recent months navigating internal debate over strategy following a mixed set of results at the last election, and party insiders say whoever takes the helm will need to unite competing factions while sharpening the party's message ahead of the next parliamentary term. Separately, federal officials confirmed a review is underway into disability accommodation support as part of a broader push to rein in the rising cost of the National Disability Insurance Scheme, with advocacy groups arguing any changes should encourage providers to expand the range of housing options available rather than simply cutting costs.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. The first piece of the emblem for the twenty thirty-two Brisbane Olympic and Paralympic Games has been unveiled at Cairns Esplanade Lagoon in Far North Queensland, kicking off a multi-day rollout that will travel to regional hubs including Townsville and Rockhampton before the full design is revealed. Retired Olympic swimmer Ian Thorpe and Paralympic swimmer Grant Patterson unveiled the golden, ray-like section of the design, immediately sparking online debate over what the finished emblem might look like, with some observers seeing the outline of a crocodile in the early glimpse and others suggesting the piece is simply too large in scale to read clearly on its own. Organisers say revealing the emblem piece by piece across regional Queensland, rather than all at once in Brisbane, is a deliberate strategy to build excitement and tourism value in parts of the state that might otherwise feel distant from the Games. In other Queensland news, a Central Queensland cattle property has produced the world's best steak, with the Senku Pure Wagyu fillet from Brisbane-based Paradigm Foods taking out the top prize at the World Steak Challenge in Amsterdam, beating four hundred and seventy entries from more than thirty countries in a blind tasting judged by sixty industry experts.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. The countdown is on for the BLEACH Festival, which opens tomorrow under new artistic director Felix Preval with its biggest program yet, bringing together close to fifty free and ticketed events and more than two hundred and ninety artists from the Gold Coast, around Australia, and overseas. The festival kicks off at dawn on the first of October with THAW, a monumental outdoor performance staged on the Surfers Paradise foreshore by acclaimed physical theatre company Legs on the Wall, before a free opening double bill on the second of October featuring Lady Blackbird and Electric Fields. Kiwi favourites SIX60 headline a ticketed show on the third of October alongside Sub-Tribe, with the festival running for eleven days across the HOTA precinct and other locations around the city. Organisers say this year's shift to an October date is designed to make the most of the Gold Coast's spring energy, and with the program spanning live music, contemporary dance, interactive installations, and theatre, festival-goers of all ages are being encouraged to get in early for the free opening events before the ticketed program ramps up later in the week.
