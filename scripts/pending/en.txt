Now to national news here in Australia. A magnitude three point nine earthquake struck Victoria's High Country on Saturday night, with its epicentre recorded near the small town of Ensay in East Gippsland, roughly three hundred and fifty kilometres east of Melbourne, making it the most powerful quake recorded in the region in more than a decade. Geoscience Australia received well over three hundred reports from residents who felt the shaking, with people as far away as Swifts Creek describing their homes rattling, and while there are no reports of significant damage or injury, seismologists have warned that aftershocks remain possible in the coming weeks as the ground settles. Separately, all eyes are on the Reserve Bank of Australia this week, with the board's latest monetary policy decision due tomorrow afternoon following months of speculation that persistent inflation could finally prompt the central bank to lift the cash rate for the first time in some time, a decision that will have immediate flow-on effects for mortgage holders and renters already grappling with elevated living costs. Economists remain split on the outcome, with some pointing to recent retail spending figures and a tight labour market as evidence the economy can absorb a modest rate rise, while others warn that households already stretched by high rents and grocery bills could be pushed further into difficulty by any further tightening. Whatever the board decides, banks are expected to move quickly to pass on the change to variable mortgage rates, meaning many households could see the effect on their repayments within days of tomorrow's announcement.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. The first new ward under the Cairns Hospital expansion has officially opened its doors, delivering twenty-eight new surgical and orthopaedic beds along with four new high-care multi-trauma bays as part of a broader one hundred and eighty-one million dollar upgrade to the facility. The milestone coincides with Cairns Hospital formally achieving accreditation as a trauma service, bringing its trauma, surgical, and orthopaedic teams together under a single coordinated model designed to speed up assessment and treatment for patients with serious and complex injuries across the far north. Health officials say the expansion, which will eventually deliver sixty-four additional beds across two new thirty-two-bed wards once complete, reflects the growing demand being placed on regional health infrastructure as North Queensland's population continues to expand, with the new trauma service standing to benefit patients from across a vast catchment area who previously faced long transfers south for the same level of specialist care. Hospital staff say the accreditation also brings access to additional specialist training and support networks, helping the far north retain skilled clinicians who might otherwise have moved to larger metropolitan centres. Local members have welcomed the milestone as a long-awaited recognition of the pressure regional emergency departments have faced in recent years, and construction crews are continuing work on the remaining wards with further beds expected to come online over the next twelve months.
