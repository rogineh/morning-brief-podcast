Tributes are flowing for much-loved children's author and illustrator Terry Denton, best known for his work on the wildly popular Treehouse series, who has died at the age of seventy-six. Denton's whimsical, densely detailed illustrations became a fixture of Australian primary school libraries and family bookshelves over a career spanning decades, and fellow authors and publishers have been quick to describe him as one of the most significant figures in Australian children's literature of his generation, crediting his work with turning countless reluctant young readers into lifelong book lovers. Separately, Shadow Housing Minister Andrew Bragg is set to float a proposal allowing Australians to draw on their superannuation to help secure a home loan, reigniting a long-running and often contentious debate over whether early access to retirement savings for housing helps buyers get into the market or simply pushes prices higher for everyone. Economists remain split on the idea, with some warning it risks inflating an already stretched property market while others argue it could offer a meaningful leg-up for younger Australians locked out of home ownership under current settings. The opposition is expected to formally unveil further detail on the policy in the coming weeks, with the government so far declining to rule the idea in or out while it awaits the full proposal.
