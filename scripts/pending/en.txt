The study found women and children are disproportionately at risk, largely because their heads and torsos sit closer to the critical impact zone on taller vehicles, and that utes and American-style pickup trucks carry roughly sixty percent higher pedestrian fatality risk than a standard passenger car. Researchers say the findings should feed directly into vehicle safety ratings and urban planning decisions as the average size of cars on Australian roads continues to grow year on year.
