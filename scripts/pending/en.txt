In technology, the FBI has issued a formal warning about an open-source AI tool called Artex, which it says malicious actors have been using to automate almost every stage of a cyberattack at speeds far beyond what human hackers could achieve manually. The bureau's advisory describes a large language model-driven platform capable of handling reconnaissance, exploitation, and data theft with minimal human input, with some documented intrusions reportedly progressing from initial targeting to privileged system access in under three hours. Organisations across a wide range of sectors have already been targeted using the framework, including financial services, government agencies, healthcare providers, and critical infrastructure operators, with attackers reportedly favouring certain freely available AI models to power the tool's decision-making. Although the framework's original developer removed the underlying code once its misuse became apparent, the FBI says it continues to spread through community-maintained forks, local variations, archived copies, and even a paid commercial version that's emerged independently of the original project. Security researchers have identified well over a thousand instances of the tool's control infrastructure still publicly exposed online, and the bureau is urging organisations to review internet-facing systems, particularly older or overlooked ones like partner portals and legacy services, which the framework has proven particularly effective at exploiting.

Turning to national news, the Australian Bureau of Statistics is set to release October's labour force figures later today, with economists watching closely after September's data showed the unemployment rate climbing to four point seven per cent, its highest level in roughly five years. That earlier result surprised many forecasters who'd expected a milder increase, and it's fuelled debate about whether the labour market is now cooling more sharply than the Reserve Bank had been anticipating when it last updated its economic outlook. A further weak reading today would likely intensify calls for the central bank to reconsider its current stance on interest rates, while a stronger-than-expected bounce back would support the view that September's numbers reflected temporary volatility rather than a genuine shift in underlying conditions. Economists say today's figures will carry particular weight given how closely they'll be read alongside other recent indicators pointing to slowing wage growth and softer overall hiring momentum through the back half of this year. Financial markets are expected to react quickly to whatever today's numbers show, with any significant surprise likely to shift pricing on how soon, and how far, borrowing costs might eventually move from their current elevated levels.

A blaze near Hervey Range in the state's north began earlier this month, arriving well before the region's bushfire season typically gets under way, while several shires across the state's south-west, including Balonne and Goondiwindi, remain under formal drought declaration following an unusually dry winter. The Bureau of Meteorology has described the coming months as a potential "triple threat" disaster season, warning that cyclones, flooding, and extreme heatwaves could all affect different parts of the state within the same stretch of months as the broader weather pattern continues shifting. Rural fire authorities say the combination of dry vegetation built up over a parched winter and the strengthening effects of the current weather pattern has created conditions ripe for fast-moving and difficult-to-control fires, particularly in areas that haven't seen significant fire activity in several years. Emergency services are urging residents in bushfire-prone areas to finalise their survival plans now, well before conditions deteriorate further, rather than waiting until a fire is already bearing down on their community.
