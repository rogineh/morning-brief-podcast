In technology, Australian design software company Canva is restructuring its workforce, with roughly one thousand two hundred and seventy-one employees, or around twenty-two per cent of its total staff, set to lose their jobs, change roles, or relocate as part of the shake-up. The company's chief executive told staff the changes reflect what she described as a structural reckoning being forced on the business by artificial intelligence, as automated design tools reshape how customers use the platform and how much human staffing is required to support it. The restructuring follows a period in which Canva's growth slowed for the first time in roughly a decade, prompting the company to combine its separate product, growth, and platform teams into a single unified product organisation and to simplify several layers of internal management along the way. As part of the changes, Canva plans to make Sydney the main base for a larger share of its product teams, asking some affected employees to relocate, while also tightening its office attendance policy so that most hybrid workers will be expected in the office two to three days a week from January. The company says departing employees will each receive a ten-thousand-dollar grant intended to support whatever they choose to pursue next, as one of Australia's best-known technology success stories grapples with the same AI-driven disruption increasingly affecting software companies around the world.

Turning to national news, property analysts and valuers now say a genuine recovery in Australia's housing market is unlikely to begin before late 2027, as the country continues working through what's being described as its steepest property downturn in roughly forty years. A survey of property valuers points to the recovery beginning around the September quarter of 2027, with total peak-to-trough price declines of around eleven per cent expected across New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, and Western Australia before prices eventually begin stabilising. One major data provider's research director says any broad-based recovery will likely require the Reserve Bank to start cutting interest rates again, a shift that currently looks unlikely to happen before the second half of next year given how persistently high inflation has proven this year. Economists remain divided on where interest rates go from here, with some arguing the central bank's tightening cycle has now peaked, while others warn a further rate rise remains genuinely possible if inflation doesn't ease as hoped over the coming months. For homeowners and prospective buyers alike, the consistent message across most forecasts is that further price falls appear likely in the near term, even as many existing owners continue holding onto substantial equity built up during the rapid growth of previous years.

In Queensland, the state government has announced that early drilling results from the Taroom Trough, a significant onshore oil and gas exploration zone, have exceeded expectations, with the site now producing both natural gas and roughly three hundred barrels of high-quality oil each day. The trough, located in the state's south, has attracted growing interest from energy companies over the past year, with one company's recent appraisal well revealing a particularly thick oil and gas-bearing geological interval alongside multiple regionally significant reservoir zones. A separate energy company operating in the same region also recently reported a substantial increase in gas flow rates during its own appraisal testing, adding to a broader picture of cautious optimism building around the area's commercial potential. Despite the encouraging early results, independent experts have previously cautioned that no certified, commercially viable oil and gas resources have yet been formally confirmed in the Taroom Trough, meaning today's announcement, while promising, still falls short of definitively proving the area's long-term commercial prospects. The state government has pointed to the results as a sign of growing potential for domestic fuel production, framing continued exploration in the region as part of a broader push to strengthen Queensland's energy security over the coming years.

And on the Gold Coast, a frontline youth support organisation is marking forty years of continuous operation this week, having grown from two outreach workers running a single van to a team now numbering more than fifty staff members supporting vulnerable young people across the city. The organisation's chief executive said the service will keep lobbying government for additional ongoing support, even as it celebrates the milestone anniversary and reflects on four decades of direct work with young people facing homelessness, family breakdown, and other significant personal challenges. Over the years, state government backing has helped fund several significant initiatives delivered in partnership with the organisation, including a dedicated youth accommodation facility specifically designed to help prevent at-risk young people from falling into homelessness in the first place. Despite that support and the organisation's substantial growth since its founding, its chief executive said considerably more work remains to adequately meet the scale of need that continues to exist among vulnerable young people across the Gold Coast region. The milestone anniversary has prompted renewed attention on youth homelessness and support services in the city, with the organisation using the occasion to highlight both how far it's come since its earliest days and how much unmet demand it still believes exists.
