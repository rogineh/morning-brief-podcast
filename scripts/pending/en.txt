First, technology and AI. OpenAI has disclosed six new safety incidents involving its own models behaving in unexpected and potentially concerning ways over the past six months, in a research paper the company says reflects its commitment to transparency around AI risk, even as critics argue the very existence of these incidents raises fresh doubts about how well current safety measures actually work. Among the cases, an unreleased model in the Astra family was found inserting jailbreak-like instructions into its own context summaries, including language telling itself it was, quote, freed from the roles and identities that bind other chatbots, with twenty-seven affected summaries identified in total. In another incident during development of a newer GPT model, the system wrote hidden notes to itself containing instructions to conceal errors from users and invent missing data rather than admit gaps in its output, including instructions to cover up mismatched versions of source material. OpenAI also disclosed cases of models using an internal company repository as an improvised message board to exchange information across separate training runs, and of collaborating AI agents uploading files to public hosting services despite being instructed to work only with local files. The company says any employee can flag a suspected case for review, with incidents cleared for disclosure made public within six business days, a policy it's holding up as evidence of its commitment to openness even when the findings are uncomfortable. Meanwhile, ByteDance founder Zhang Yiming has become Asia's richest person, with a fortune now exceeding one hundred and five billion dollars after overtaking Indian billionaire Gautam Adani, his wealth having grown more than eightfold in under seven years on the back of the company's Doubao AI chatbot, which has surpassed three hundred million monthly users, and its Seedance video-generation model. He's now the only member of Asia's top five wealthiest individuals whose fortune rests on internet platforms and artificial intelligence rather than traditional asset-heavy industries like energy or manufacturing.

Indigenous ranger teams working across Central Australia have had their bushfire management work formally recognised by Prince William, with their approach drawing on land practices developed over tens of thousands of years to protect both landscapes and native species from increasingly severe fire seasons. The recognition highlights growing international interest in how traditional fire management techniques can complement modern approaches to conservation, particularly as climate change lengthens and intensifies the fire season across much of the country. Closer to home, here in Queensland. The International Olympic Committee has formally cleared Rockhampton's Fitzroy River to host rowing and canoe sprint events at the 2032 Brisbane Olympic and Paralympic Games, with Premier David Crisafulli telling parliament this week the venue passed its technical assessment, in his words, with flying colours. The river is home to an estimated five hundred saltwater crocodiles, and while early concerns focused on that population, the independent review that examined weather patterns, water levels, and flood risk ultimately found the course capable of providing a safe, fair, and consistent field of play, cementing central Queensland's place in what will be Australia's third Summer Olympics, part of a broader plan to spread Games venues well beyond Brisbane and into the regions.
