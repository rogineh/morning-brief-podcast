In technology, OpenAI has released two new models, GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, now available across ChatGPT's paid tiers and through the company's developer API. Astra is being positioned as the flagship option for demanding professional tasks, while Sol offers a cheaper, faster alternative intended for everyday work that doesn't require the absolute cutting edge of capability. The company says Astra shows meaningful improvements over its predecessor while actually using fewer computing resources per task, and that Sol performs remarkably close to Astra on certain coding benchmarks despite running at a fraction of the cost. Independent testing has painted a more mixed picture, with some evaluators placing the new Astra model just behind a rival system from Anthropic on certain measures, even as OpenAI continues emphasising the efficiency gains built into this release. The launch comes roughly two months after the company abandoned an earlier version of Astra over unresolved safety concerns, a decision that delayed this release and underscored the increasingly cautious internal review processes now standard practice at leading AI labs before any major model reaches the public. Both new models have been assessed as meeting the highest tier of cybersecurity risk under the company's own internal safety framework, a classification that triggers additional internal safeguards and monitoring regardless of the model's intended everyday use.

Turning to national news, the federal government has confirmed a funding package worth more than one hundred million dollars aimed at expanding community-led health care models for First Nations mothers, babies, and young children across the country. The funding replaces an earlier, more rigid program with a more flexible model that allows local Aboriginal community-controlled health services to design care pathways suited to their specific communities, rather than following a single standardised template applied uniformly nationwide. Organisations delivering the funded services are spread across multiple states and territories, reflecting a deliberate effort to ensure the benefits reach both urban and remote Indigenous communities rather than concentrating resources in just one or two locations. Health advocates have broadly welcomed the shift toward community-controlled delivery, arguing that locally designed and locally run services have consistently shown better engagement and outcomes compared with services designed centrally and simply rolled out to Indigenous communities from the top down. The investment forms part of a broader, long-running effort to close persistent gaps in health outcomes between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Australians, with maternal and early childhood health specifically identified as an area where early intervention can meaningfully shape outcomes across an entire lifetime.

In Queensland, communities across the state's south-east are continuing to recover from a run of severe storms that swept through the region earlier this week, bringing damaging winds, large hail, and flash flooding to parts of Brisbane and surrounding areas. Energy provider Energex reported significant numbers of fallen trees bringing down powerlines during the worst of the weather, leaving thousands of households temporarily without power as crews worked to restore supply across affected suburbs. The Bureau of Meteorology had issued a series of escalating warnings in the lead-up to the storms, correctly flagging the risk of dangerous conditions even as the exact timing and intensity of individual storm cells remained difficult to pin down precisely until they were already forming. Authorities say this week's severe weather fits a broader pattern forecasters have been warning about for the current storm season, with a strengthening weather pattern and record warm background temperatures combining to create conditions conducive to more frequent and more intense storm activity across the state. Emergency services are urging residents to use the current lull in severe weather to check their own storm preparedness, including securing loose items around properties and reviewing household emergency plans, given forecasters expect further severe weather risk to persist across the region over the coming months.

Local businesses across Surfers Paradise say the week remains one of the most significant events on the Gold Coast's calendar, delivering a substantial boost to accommodation, hospitality, and retail trade at the traditional start of the city's peak summer tourist season. With safety planning for this year's event drawing on lessons refined over many previous editions, authorities say they're confident the week will again pass with the vast majority of participants enjoying the celebrations safely and responsibly.
