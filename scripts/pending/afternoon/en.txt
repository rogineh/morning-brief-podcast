Turning now to news from across Australia.

Medicare Urgent Care Clinics are continuing their rollout toward becoming a permanent fixture of the health system, backed by one point eight billion dollars allocated in this year's federal budget to lock in their ongoing operation rather than treating them as a temporary pilot program. The clinics are designed to give patients with urgent but non-life-threatening conditions an alternative to hospital emergency departments, typically with shorter wait times and no out-of-pocket cost for a Medicare card holder. Health officials say early data suggests the clinics are successfully diverting a meaningful number of patients away from busier emergency departments, though some regional communities continue to push for additional clinics in areas that remain underserved by the current rollout. The federal government says it will continue monitoring patient outcomes and wait times as the network expands, with further site announcements expected as the program matures. Doctors' groups have broadly welcomed the shift to permanent funding, saying ongoing uncertainty over a program's future makes it harder for clinics to retain staff and plan longer term rosters, even when the underlying demand for the service is clearly there.

Now, the news from here in Queensland.

Construction continues on a twenty eight point eight million dollar upgrade to a key bridge on the Warrego Highway near Toowoomba, part of a broader push to improve safety and capacity on one of the region's busiest freight and commuter routes. The federal government is funding the bulk of the project, which officials say will reduce bottlenecks that have long frustrated both long-haul truck drivers and local commuters travelling between Toowoomba and surrounding districts. Local transport and freight groups have welcomed the investment, saying the existing bridge has been a persistent pinch point on a corridor that carries a significant share of the state's agricultural and freight traffic. The government says it's aiming to minimise disruption during construction by scheduling the most disruptive work outside of peak travel periods wherever possible. The upgrade forms one part of a broader program of works along the Warrego Highway corridor, which officials say will continue in stages over the coming years as funding and construction capacity allow, rather than being delivered as a single, all-at-once project.

The Groundwater Country Music Festival is in full swing in Broadbeach this weekend, with free stages spread across the precinct's streets, parks, and venues drawing large crowds for performances from some of the biggest names in Australian country music. This year's lineup includes Kasey Chambers, Kaylee Bell, the Wolfe Brothers, Beccy Cole, and James Blundell, among others, across the festival's three day run. Organisers say the event has grown into one of the biggest fixtures on the Gold Coast's annual events calendar, with local hospitality venues reporting a significant uplift in trade as visitors flow through the precinct between stages. City officials say events like this one play an important role in diversifying the Gold Coast's tourism appeal beyond its beaches, helping support local businesses during what would otherwise be a quieter stretch of the calendar between the busier summer holiday periods. Festival organisers say they're already in early planning for next year's event, and that the free, family-friendly format remains central to the festival's identity even as its scale and production budget have grown considerably since its earliest years.
