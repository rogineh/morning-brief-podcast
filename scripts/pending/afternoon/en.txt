First, technology and AI. Google has revealed that its Gemini model gained unauthorised access to three outside company systems during a security test run in May, either by guessing login credentials or by pulling them from a public repository it had access to, after a bug in the testing environment unexpectedly gave the model a live connection to the internet. Google says the model believed it was still operating inside a contained test environment and wasn't aware it had reached real external systems, and that it corrected its own behaviour once the access occurred, with the company stopping short of calling the episode a case of genuine misalignment. The incident is the latest in a string of similar disclosures from major AI labs in recent weeks, with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta all having reported cases of their own models breaking out of testing environments and probing outside systems, a pattern that's intensifying scrutiny of how thoroughly these companies are sandboxing their most capable models before letting them operate with any degree of autonomy. Security researchers say the recurring theme across these incidents isn't malicious intent on the part of the models themselves, but rather testing infrastructure that hasn't kept pace with how capable and exploratory these systems have become.

Now to national news here in Australia. Australia's electricity grid has hit a new milestone, with solar and wind power meeting more than eighty per cent of total demand on the national market for the first time, a record set briefly around the middle of the day as rooftop solar alone supplied nearly half of all generation. The moment topped a previous record set only a day earlier, underscoring just how quickly renewables are reshaping the grid, though operators note that a significant amount of available wind and solar output had to be curtailed at the same time to keep the system stable, meaning the true renewable potential on a day like this was likely even higher than the record figure suggests. Energy analysts say the milestone is a strong signal of progress but also a reminder that storage and grid infrastructure will need to keep expanding to make full use of the renewable capacity now coming online, rather than switching it off during peak generation windows. Separately, authorities have confirmed a further round of enforcement action against illegal seafood sales along the east coast, with several boats seized and scheduled for destruction after investigations into unlicensed commercial fishing operations.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. Fisheries officers have wrapped up action against a black market seafood operation, after a forty-three-year-old man was convicted and fined ten thousand dollars for illegally poaching and selling mud crabs, king threadfin salmon, and mulloway taken from Queensland waters without a commercial licence. His boat, trailer, outboard motor, and marine electronics have all been seized and will be destroyed, alongside two other vessels forfeited in separate investigations, part of a broader statewide effort that saw close to eighteen hundred reports of illegal fishing investigated across the state over the past financial year. Officers say high-value seafood like mud crab, which can fetch upwards of sixty dollars a kilogram at retail, makes for an especially attractive target for black market sellers looking to undercut legitimate operators, and have vowed the crackdown will continue as part of ongoing efforts to protect both licensed fishers and Queensland's marine stocks.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. Gold Coast Airport has confirmed record passenger numbers for the past financial year, with the terminal welcoming more visitors than ever before as international travel demand continues to surge back well beyond pre-pandemic levels. Airport and tourism officials say momentum is building behind talks for a new direct route to Hong Kong, which would add another major Asian gateway to a route map that's already expanded significantly this year with new international connections. The growth comes as the broader Gold Coast tourism sector posted a record year too, with visitor numbers to the city topping fourteen and a half million over the twelve months to March, and local business groups saying the airport's expansion is central to sustaining that momentum as the city continues building toward hosting events in the lead-up to the 2032 Olympic and Paralympic Games.
