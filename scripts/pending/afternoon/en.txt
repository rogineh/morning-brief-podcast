Now to national news here in Australia. Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke has delivered his long-awaited National Press Club address unveiling the government's overhaul of the migration system, confirming that international students will largely no longer be able to attach partners or children to their visas, though families already in Australia together won't be split up, and exceptions will remain for Pacific and ASEAN nationals and students in certain courses such as PhDs. The package also includes tighter rules to stop people hopping between visa types to extend their stay, tougher action against visa overstayers, and a crackdown on rogue migration agents accused of exploiting the system, with the minister vowing the government's target of bringing net overseas migration down to two hundred and twenty-five thousand by 2028 will be met regardless of how difficult the path there proves to be. Separately, financial markets are increasingly pricing in an interest rate rise from the Reserve Bank at its meeting later this month, after the US Federal Reserve lifted its own benchmark rate overnight to between three point seven five and four percent. Several of the major banks are now forecasting the Reserve Bank will follow suit on the twenty-ninth of September, lifting the cash rate from four point three five to four point six percent, with underlying inflation still running at three point six percent, well above the central bank's target band.

State parliament has passed the government's Circuit Breaker Sentencing bill, paving the way for youth justice camps to be rolled out later this year as part of a broader push to address repeat youth offending, with the government arguing the new facilities will offer a structured, intensive alternative to simply cycling young offenders in and out of detention.
