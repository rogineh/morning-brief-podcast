In technology, Apple has opened its Siri AI developer preview to all app developers, allowing third-party apps to connect directly with the voice assistant through a system called App Intents, letting Siri handle tasks like checking a delivery, booking a reservation, or placing an order on a developer's behalf. The preview is available now across the latest beta versions of Apple's operating systems for iPhone, iPad, Mac, and its mixed-reality headset, running on devices that support Apple Intelligence more broadly. Developers using the system get to decide exactly what content Siri can retrieve from their app and which specific actions it's allowed to perform, with titles and descriptions attached to each available action helping Siri correctly interpret what a user is actually asking for. Apps can also chain several individual actions together into more complex combined tasks, and developers can choose to have their content indexed more broadly within Apple's on-device search as part of the same integration work. Importantly, Apple says apps using these new Siri integrations won't actually be able to go live for ordinary customers through the App Store until some time in spring next year, meaning today's move opens a formal testing window rather than an immediate consumer-facing launch, and the company has warned that the underlying system may still change somewhat as the preview period continues.

Turning to national news, Australia's High Court has ruled that rideshare giant Uber owes eighty-one million dollars in payroll tax to the New South Wales government, dismissing the company's appeal in a decision with potentially far-reaching consequences for the broader gig economy. A majority of the court's seven justices found that drivers working for Uber should be classified as employees for payroll tax purposes, and that payments flowing to those drivers count as wages under the relevant tax law, pointing specifically to the degree of control Uber retains over fares before that money ever reaches individual drivers. The ruling overturns an earlier New South Wales Supreme Court decision that had actually gone in Uber's favour, and tax experts say the total bill facing the company could climb well past one hundred million dollars once liabilities from subsequent years are eventually factored in. Uber said it respects the court's decision but warned the ruling could expose numerous other businesses operating under similar structures to substantial new and unexpected costs without further legislative reform to clarify the underlying rules. Legal and industry analysts expect the decision to have ripple effects well beyond Uber itself, with several other companies operating in the gig economy space now facing renewed scrutiny over how they've been classifying their own workers for payroll tax purposes.

In Queensland, a Supreme Court judge has overturned a Land Court decision that had cleared the way for mining exploration near a culturally significant site on Cape York, ruling that the relevant traditional owners were never properly consulted before the decision was made. The case centred on a mining company's successful bid to ease an environmental exclusion zone near the Alice River, a change that would have allowed exploration activity closer to the protected area than previously permitted. The Aboriginal corporation that holds native title on trust for the local traditional owners argued it had never been properly consulted about the proposed change, despite holding clear legal standing to be heard on a matter directly affecting country under its care. In his ruling, the judge found the corporation had been denied a reasonable opportunity to be heard, describing the process as a breach of basic procedural fairness and natural justice, and noting evidence suggesting the responsible government department was already aware the corporation opposed the change before it was approved. The decision effectively sends the matter back for proper reconsideration, with neither the state government nor the mining company yet indicating publicly whether they intend to pursue any further appeal of today's ruling.

And on the Gold Coast, the Suns are preparing to host Hawthorn in an AFLW elimination final this coming Sunday, with both clubs chasing their first-ever finals victory in what shapes as a genuinely significant fixture for each side's finals history. Star midfielder Anne Hatchard, who joined the Suns from Adelaide and has become a key contributor during her first season at the club, says the team still has plenty of room to keep improving even after securing a finals berth. The Suns have enjoyed a dramatic turnaround this year, finishing fifth on the ladder with a nine-win, three-loss record after claiming the competition's wooden spoon just last season, a reversal that's generated considerable excitement among the club's growing fan base. Sunday's clash is scheduled for mid-afternoon at the Suns' home ground, with both Gold Coast and Hawthorn still searching for their maiden finals win after each falling short in previous finals appearances. Hatchard, a four-time All-Australian midfielder, has been particularly influential in tight contests this season, including kicking two crucial goals in the final term of a tense recent clash against her old club, and she's confident the Suns' best football is still ahead of them as the finals series begins.
