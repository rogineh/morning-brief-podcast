First, technology and AI. Security researchers are raising fresh alarm about how easily AI agents can be hijacked once they're given access to everyday tools like email, calendars, and online banking, with a technique known as prompt injection emerging as one of the sector's most pressing security headaches. The method works by hiding malicious instructions inside ordinary-looking content, such as an email, a calendar invite, or even an invoice, instructions that are invisible to a human reader but fully readable and executable by an AI assistant scanning that same content on a user's behalf. In one scenario researchers have demonstrated, a single unseen email can silently trigger an agent to summarise a user's inbox and pull files from their cloud storage without them ever clicking anything, while in another, attackers have shown they can embed hidden commands inside a legitimate-looking invoice that cause an AI assistant to quietly swap out a vendor's real bank account number for the attacker's own, resulting in fraudulent money transfers. Surveys suggest consumers have already handed agents broad access to their digital lives, with more than a third granting access to email, web browsers, and messaging apps, and smaller but still significant numbers allowing access to financial accounts and health apps. Security experts say the convenience of letting an AI agent handle these tasks is creating an entirely new category of attack surface that both everyday users and the companies building these tools are still racing to properly understand and defend against.

Now to national news here in Australia. The Kangaroos squad for next month's Rugby League World Cup has been officially announced, with Panthers skipper Isaah Yeo handed the captaincy and six potential debutants named in a twenty-three-man group that selectors say reflects the changing face of the national side. James Tedesco returns to compete with Reece Walsh for the fullback jersey after a strong club season, while Latrell Mitchell has also been included after working his way back from injury in time for the tournament. The squad announcement comes with excitement building nationally for the World Cup, which kicks off in Queensland later this month and will see Australia aim to defend its title on home soil across venues in Brisbane, the Gold Coast, and Townsville. Selectors say the mix of proven veterans and fresh faces reflects a deliberate effort to build depth in the squad ahead of what's expected to be a tightly contested tournament, with several overseas nations having significantly strengthened their rosters in recent years.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. Police are appealing for public assistance to help locate eighty-four-year-old Irene Blunsdon, who has been missing from the Brisbane suburb of Carina since yesterday morning, last seen at a Meadowlands Road address at around ten o'clock. Police and family hold concerns for Irene's welfare given her age and the circumstances of her disappearance, and are urging anyone who may have seen her or who has information about her whereabouts to come forward as soon as possible. Investigators say searches are continuing in the local area today, with police stressing that any sighting, no matter how minor it might seem, could prove crucial in helping to locate her safely.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. AFL games record holder Scott Pendlebury has officially completed his move from Collingwood to the Gold Coast Suns on day one of this year's trade period, with the thirty-eight-year-old traded to the Suns in exchange for pick sixty-five after twenty-one seasons at Collingwood. Rather than signing Pendlebury as a free agent, the two clubs opted for a trade specifically so the Suns could preserve their free agency compensation entitlements tied to the expected departure of key forward Ben King to Hawthorn, a strategic manoeuvre that's drawn plenty of attention from football commentators today. Pendlebury, who has notched four hundred and forty-two senior games, five best and fairest awards, and six All-Australian blazers across his storied career, is expected to see out his playing days with the Suns in a playing mentor role, with a potential coaching future at the club already being discussed. Club officials say the addition of such an experienced head on and off the field could prove valuable for a playing group still searching for its first finals appearance, even as Pendlebury himself transitions into what both the player and club have described as a mentoring role rather than a frontline playing one.
