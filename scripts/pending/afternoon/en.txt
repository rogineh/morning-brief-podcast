Good afternoon, and welcome to your Daily Brief for Sunday, the 11th of October, 2026. Here's what you need to know this afternoon, starting with technology and AI.

Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella has called for what he's describing as an "emergency brake" on advanced AI systems, arguing the industry needs a fundamentally new approach to trust and safety as models become more capable and autonomous. In a lengthy post outlining his thinking, Nadella argued that companies should separate AI models from the software that directs their actions, build in independent controls that sit outside the model itself, and maintain tamper-proof, human-readable records of significant actions a model takes. He also said developers should design systems on the assumption that a model could be compromised at any point, rather than simply trusting it by default, a notably cautious message from the head of one of the world's largest AI investors. Nadella argued that the most trustworthy systems won't necessarily be the ones people trust the most by default, but rather the ones specifically designed so that excessive trust is never required in the first place.

Separately, Qualcomm's chief executive says major AI companies are already pushing for smartphones capable of running AI models with as many as one hundred billion parameters by 2028, roughly triple what today's most capable phones can handle on-device. The comments point to a growing push to move more AI processing directly onto consumer devices rather than relying entirely on cloud servers, driven by demands for faster responses, lower costs, and better privacy for sensitive, personal AI tasks. Reaching that kind of scale on a handset will require significant advances in chip efficiency, and manufacturers are racing to develop processors capable of handling that workload without draining a phone's battery in minutes.

Turning now to news from across Australia.

It was a dramatic finish at the Bathurst 1000, with Brodie Kostecki and co-driver Todd Hazelwood snatching an unlikely win on the final lap after race leader Anton De Pasquale ran out of fuel coming down Mountain Straight. The Dick Johnson Racing pair swept past the stricken Team 18 Camaro to take the chequered flag, denying De Pasquale what looked like a certain victory just moments earlier. Will Brown and Scott Pye rounded out the podium in second, continuing what's been one of the most unpredictable editions of the Great Race in years. Kostecki, who'd missed out on a record fourth straight pole position in qualifying, said the win felt almost surreal given how the race had looked all but lost just laps from home, while a shattered De Pasquale admitted the team had misjudged their fuel window by the narrowest of margins.

Elsewhere, the federal government has pushed back the Australian Taxation Office's planned ban on credit card payments for tax bills, delaying the change until the middle of next year after a wave of criticism from small business groups caught off guard by the original timeline. Treasurer Jim Chalmers said the government would cover the cost of the extension, giving businesses more time to adjust, though the opposition has argued the backflip doesn't go far enough, with some MPs calling for the ban to be scrapped altogether rather than simply delayed.
