Good afternoon, and welcome to your Afternoon Edition for Tuesday, the 20th of October, 2026. Here's what's been making news today in technology, across Australia, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

In technology, AI chip startup Tenstorrent has more than doubled its valuation after raising one and a half billion US dollars in a late-stage funding round, putting the Nvidia rival at a six point two billion dollar post-money valuation. The round was led by Trace Capital and Innovation X, with additional backing from ARK Invest, Sandisk, and Tether Investments. Tenstorrent, led by veteran chip designer Jim Keller, says the fresh capital will go toward scaling its Galaxy servers, which combine dozens of the company's AI chips to compete with established players in AI infrastructure. The raise comes after Tenstorrent reportedly held talks earlier this year with both Intel and Qualcomm about a potential acquisition, before deciding to stay independent and raise money instead. The deal underscores how much capital investors are still willing to pour into alternatives to Nvidia's dominant AI chips, even as questions persist across the industry about whether AI infrastructure spending is outpacing near-term returns. Separately, Nvidia's own push deeper into server CPUs is adding fresh competitive pressure on Intel and AMD, given Nvidia's already deep relationships with the biggest buyers of AI infrastructure. Analysts say the Tenstorrent round is also a signal that some of the world's largest investment funds are now comfortable backing smaller challengers directly, rather than channelling all their AI exposure through the handful of dominant chipmakers.

Turning to national news, the Reserve Bank of Australia's cash rate now sits at four point six zero per cent following a quarter-point rise in late September, the fourth increase this year and a fresh multi-year high. Major lenders including NAB have already passed the increase on to variable home loan customers. The rate rises are starting to show up in the housing market, with national home prices down five point two per cent from their March peak, according to Cotality data, and values falling in almost every capital city last month. The Reserve Bank has flagged that further increases remain on the table if an ongoing energy-driven inflation shock continues to feed through the economy, with the next rate decision due on the third of November. Economists are divided on how households already stretched by repayments will absorb another possible hike, with some warning of a weaker summer spending season if borrowing costs keep climbing, while others argue the economy has so far proven more resilient to higher rates than many had predicted at the start of the tightening cycle.

In Queensland, the state government has confirmed four hundred and seventeen million dollars in this year's budget for early works and planning on Brisbane's 2032 Olympic venues, the first significant funding allocation toward the Games' infrastructure program. The centrepiece Victoria Park stadium is estimated to cost around three point seven billion dollars, with a further six hundred and fifty million dollars earmarked for an aquatic centre in the same precinct. So far only one major construction contract has been awarded, with seven new venues still to be built and ten more requiring upgrades before the Games open. The environmental group Save Victoria Park continues to oppose building within the park itself, arguing the site should be preserved as green space rather than developed, while the state government maintains the location remains central to its venue plan. Officials say further contracts and a clearer overall cost picture are expected to emerge as planning work progresses over the coming year, with the state keen to avoid the cost blowouts that have dogged previous Olympic host cities.

And on the Gold Coast, the AFLW Suns have extended their winning streak to five games, climbing into the competition's top four with a six and two record. Coach Rhyce Shaw described the team as being in as good a space as it's been all year, ahead of a Round Nine clash against Melbourne to be played in Mackay. Co-captains Lucy Single and Niamh McLaughlin have led the turnaround, backed by vice-captains Meara Girvan, Charlie Rowbottom, and Maddy Brancatisano. The club has also locked in its playing future, with academy products Sunny Lappin, Heidi Talbot, and Mia Salisbury all signing contract extensions in recent weeks. The Suns' form has lifted expectations around the club that a finals campaign is now firmly within reach, after several seasons building toward genuine contention.
