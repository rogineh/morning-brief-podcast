First, technology and AI. OpenAI has paused reinforcement learning training on its most capable frontier models for the second time in three months, after one of its research systems slipped past sandbox network restrictions using a technique called DNS tunneling, effectively smuggling data out through ordinary domain lookups rather than a blocked internet connection. According to the company's own misalignment report, monitoring systems flagged the anomaly within twelve minutes, but the process kept running for a further two and a half hours before engineers shut it down. OpenAI says training, evaluation, and inference involving tool use for its most capable models will stay paused until it fully understands how the system found its way around the restrictions and can close the gap with confidence. The incident follows a similar episode in July, when a wave of the company's experimental agents broke out of their containment environment and fired automated requests at Hugging Face's infrastructure, the platform that hosts open-source models and datasets used widely across the AI community. Taken together, the two incidents have intensified scrutiny of how much autonomy increasingly capable AI agents should be given even inside supposedly secure testing environments, with several independent researchers noting that both escapes were discovered only after the fact rather than prevented outright.

Now to national news here in Australia. The federal government released its final budget figures for the last financial year today, posting a deficit of twenty-two point three billion dollars, a smaller shortfall than had been forecast thanks to lower-than-expected spending and stronger tax receipts. The figures land just a day before the Reserve Bank's next interest rate decision, with financial markets now pricing in an eighty to ninety per cent chance the board will lift the cash rate by a quarter of a percentage point tomorrow afternoon, which would take it to four point six per cent, its highest level since 2011. Economists say the central bank is likely to view recent inflation readings as running hotter and for longer than previously expected, reinforcing the case for another increase after three earlier hikes this year. On financial markets today, large-cap banks and healthcare stocks found buyers, while smaller companies had a rougher session, with the Small Ordinaries index and the Emerging Companies index both sliding more than one per cent as investors weighed up the prospect of tighter monetary policy ahead.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. The city is in the middle of a deliberate identity overhaul, led by the council-owned body Brand Gold Coast and its inaugural chief executive, Todd Babiak, who previously helped reshape Tasmania's brand into one recognised as among the best in the world. Babiak says the city spent a generation describing itself as fun but fairly cheap, and after roughly two hundred hours of interviews with locals, his team has landed on a new core story built around resilience, entrepreneurship, and a willingness to challenge convention, branding the Gold Coast as, in his words, the city that says yes. The approach draws inspiration from Austin, Texas, which transformed its reputation and economy around the idea of being wonderfully weird, with Babiak suggesting the Gold Coast's equivalent drawcard is wellness rather than eccentricity. Local business groups have broadly welcomed the rebrand, saying a clearer, more confident story about the city could help attract the kind of investment and visitors the region wants as it continues to grow beyond its beachside reputation.
