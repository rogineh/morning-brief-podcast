In technology, global chip and AI stocks suffered one of their roughest trading sessions of the year yesterday, wiping out roughly a trillion dollars in market value in a single day as investors grew increasingly nervous about whether years of massive AI infrastructure spending will ever pay off. Intel had its worst trading day since 1974, with shares plunging more than twenty-eight per cent after the company issued weak guidance for the final quarter of the year, driven largely by soft demand in its personal computer business. Chip-making giant Nvidia also slid more than four per cent, while a closely watched index that tracks the broader semiconductor sector dropped more than seven per cent on the day alone, capping off a month in which that index had already fallen by close to nine per cent. The Nasdaq, heavily weighted toward technology companies, posted its worst single-day performance since June, closing down close to three per cent. Market analysts say the sell-off reflects growing scrutiny of the enormous capital expenditure major technology companies have committed to artificial intelligence data centres and infrastructure, with investors increasingly questioning the timeline for that spending to translate into profit, even as several of these same companies continue reporting strong underlying business growth.

Turning to national news, the federal government has moved to sharply restrict how much public money agencies can spend on branded promotional merchandise, after new figures revealed Commonwealth bodies spent more than three-point-four million dollars on items like tote bags, pens, beanies, lollies, and even condoms over the past financial year. Figures provided to the Senate show the Defence Department was by far the biggest spender, accounting for more than one-point-four million dollars of the total on its own, though a department spokesperson defended the purchases as being limited to specific public health campaigns and recruitment strategies rather than general office giveaways. Under the new restrictions, government agencies will need separate, specific authorisation before purchasing promotional merchandise from taxpayer funds, closing off what had previously been a far more discretionary spending category for individual departments. The move follows growing public and parliamentary scrutiny of government spending more broadly, with similar restrictions already introduced at the state level in Victoria earlier this year targeting branded merchandise bought for internal staff use rather than external public campaigns. Officials say the changes are designed to ensure taxpayer money is being spent on core public services and programs rather than promotional items with limited lasting public benefit.

In Queensland, the Bureau of Meteorology is warning that a broader weather front moving up the eastern seaboard is expected to bring heavy rain and thunderstorms to Brisbane and the state's south-east during tomorrow afternoon and evening, as a cold front colliding with warm, moist air triggers unstable conditions across the region. Forecasters say the system is part of a wider weather pattern already generating storm warnings for several other eastern capital cities further south, with the focus of the most severe activity expected to shift further north into Queensland as the system tracks up the coast over the coming day. While no severe thunderstorm warning has been issued for Brisbane as of this afternoon, residents across the south-east are being urged to keep an eye on Bureau updates heading into tomorrow, given the potential for damaging wind gusts, large hail, and heavy rainfall that can accompany these kinds of unstable weather systems in Queensland at this time of year. The warning comes during what's traditionally a transitional period between the state's dry season and the build-up to the wetter summer months, when sudden severe storms become increasingly common across the south-east corner.

And on the Gold Coast, the city has become part of aviation history after welcoming the first-ever commercial flight to depart from Sydney's brand-new Western Sydney International Airport, with a sold-out Jetstar service touching down on the Gold Coast after taking off from the new terminal earlier in the week. The flight marks the first scheduled service launched from a major new Australian airport in more than fifty years, and strong public interest saw every seat on the historic inaugural service snapped up well ahead of departure. The new western Sydney airport has been progressively ramping up operations over the past week, with cargo flights having begun months earlier in July before passenger services finally launched, and an international service from Auckland touching down just a day after the Gold Coast flight's debut. For Gold Coast tourism operators, the new direct link to Sydney's growing western suburbs is expected to open up an entirely new catchment of potential visitors who previously faced a longer trip through Sydney's existing airport, with several more routes between the two cities expected to be added as the new terminal's operations continue to expand in the months ahead.
