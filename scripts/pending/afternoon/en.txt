In technology, chipmaker AMD has posted record quarterly results, with revenue for the three months to the end of September coming in at thirteen point four billion dollars, up forty-five per cent on the same period last year. The standout performer within those results was the company's data centre division, where revenue very nearly doubled compared with a year earlier, driven largely by continued strong demand for its server processors and the recent launch of its latest generation of AI-focused graphics processing chips. AMD's leadership pointed to that new chip launch specifically as a major contributor to the quarter's momentum, suggesting the company is successfully carving out a larger slice of the booming market for AI computing hardware traditionally dominated by a single major rival. Looking ahead, the company has guided investors to expect revenue of around seventeen billion dollars in the current quarter, which would represent another substantial jump if achieved. One note of caution in an otherwise strong result was a noticeable decline in free cash flow compared with the same quarter last year, with the company attributing that softer cash position to a combination of inventory build-up and continued heavy capital spending as it races to keep pace with surging demand.

Turning to national news, Australians are being urged to stay vigilant against increasingly sophisticated myGov-related scams, after an investigation into fraudulent websites mimicking the government services portal uncovered losses estimated at around two hundred and thirty-five million dollars. The Commonwealth Ombudsman has separately published a review examining how Services Australia responds when fraudsters gain unauthorised access to linked government accounts, concluding that more could be done to protect vulnerable users from this particular style of attack. Services Australia has accepted the Ombudsman's recommendations and says it's progressing a broader security overhaul for the myGov platform, including new verification challenges and a dedicated security dashboard designed to give users clearer visibility over their own account activity. The investigation has already produced at least one successful prosecution, with a Perth-based health professional recently sentenced to a term of imprisonment after being found to have defrauded a federal agency of more than one hundred and forty thousand dollars through a related scheme. Authorities continue to stress that government agencies will never ask citizens to click a link in an unexpected text message or email, urging anyone who receives a suspicious myGov-related communication to report it directly rather than engaging with it.

In Queensland, the network of Medicare Mental Health Centres offering free walk-in support has now expanded to seventeen locations across the state, with the program collectively delivering close to eighty thousand free appointments to Queenslanders seeking mental health care since the rollout began. The centres, which operate without any requirement for a referral or appointment booking in many cases, are designed to provide an accessible first point of contact for people experiencing mental health difficulties who might otherwise struggle to navigate the broader healthcare system. Locations now span from major regional centres through to outer suburban areas, reflecting a deliberate strategy of spreading access well beyond the inner-city clinics that have traditionally dominated mental health service provision. Health officials say demand at existing centres has consistently exceeded initial projections, reinforcing the case for continuing to expand the network into additional communities identified as having limited existing mental health infrastructure. The federal and state governments jointly fund the program, which forms part of a broader national effort to make mental health support more immediately accessible without the cost or waiting-list barriers that can accompany more traditional referral pathways.

And on the Gold Coast, developer Graya has secured council approval for a new one-hundred-and-sixty-million-dollar beachfront apartment project at Palm Beach, continuing a sustained run of major tower approvals across the city's southern suburbs this year. The project, known as Coral, will bring a boutique apartment offering to a stretch of coastline that's increasingly attracting developer interest as more established areas further north become comprehensively built out. Local property analysts say Palm Beach has emerged as one of the more sought-after pockets of the southern Gold Coast in recent years, drawing buyers looking for a slightly quieter beachside lifestyle without being too far removed from the city's busier commercial and entertainment precincts. The approval adds to a lengthy list of major residential developments greenlit across the city in recent months, underscoring continued strong confidence in the Gold Coast property market even as higher financing costs weigh on development activity more broadly across the sector. Industry figures say buyer demand for boutique, lower-density beachfront projects like this one remains particularly resilient compared with larger high-rise developments, as some purchasers continue to prioritise a more intimate building scale over sheer height and unit numbers.
