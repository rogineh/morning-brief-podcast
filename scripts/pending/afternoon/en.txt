Good afternoon, and welcome to your Afternoon Edition for Wednesday, the 21st of October, 2026. Here's what's been making news today in technology, across Australia, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

In technology, Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang took the stage in Berlin today for the company's GTC Europe keynote, with the event expected to focus heavily on sovereign AI infrastructure for the continent, agentic AI systems, and robotics. More than a dozen German companies, including Siemens, Audi, and Mercedes-Benz, are taking part in discussions and demonstrations tied to the event, reflecting a push by major European industrial firms to build AI capability directly into manufacturing and engineering processes rather than treating it as a separate add-on. Huang also sat down with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz to discuss the country's broader AI roadmap, underscoring how central the technology has become to industrial policy conversations at the highest levels of government. Analysts following the event say this year's framing has shifted noticeably from simply selling processing chips toward selling what Nvidia calls complete AI factories, bundling hardware, software, and infrastructure planning into a single offering for governments and large companies. The Berlin event is being watched closely as a signal of how aggressively European industry plans to compete with the United States and China on AI infrastructure build-out over the coming years, even as questions continue elsewhere about how sustainably all of this investment is being financed.

Turning to national news, the man charged over Monday's alleged car attack on independent MP Helen Haines appeared briefly in Wangaratta Magistrates' Court today, with his lawyer not applying for bail. Brendan Nugent, 43, faces charges including dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and a federal offence of harming a Commonwealth public official, with police also laying separate drug-related charges and saying they have not ruled out further charges as the investigation continues. His lawyer told the court he suffers from a rare neurological condition and was not currently medicated at the time of the alleged attack. Nugent is expected to be transferred to Melbourne's Assessment Prison ahead of a committal hearing set down for early February next year, with police required to finalise their brief of evidence by Christmas Eve. Haines, who was seriously injured in the alleged attack outside her electorate office and remains in hospital, has had strong cross-party support since the incident, and Victoria's police chief commissioner says the investigation into what led to it is far from complete. The case has also prompted renewed concern among politicians more broadly about threats and attacks on elected officials, with at least one state MP saying separately that he received a death threat during a recent election campaign.

In Queensland, the state's teachers' union is pushing for tougher criminal penalties for people who assault teachers, with a petition backing the campaign attracting close to seven thousand signatures. The Queensland Teachers' Union wants teachers reclassified as public officers under the criminal code, placing them in the same legal category as police and paramedics and effectively doubling the maximum penalty for serious assault against them from seven years to fourteen. The push follows a union survey of nine thousand members that found seven in ten had experienced a serious incident of aggression, threat, or harm at work this year, with some regional school districts reporting teachers leaving the profession altogether after repeated classroom threats. Critics of the proposal argue that heavier criminal penalties alone won't fix the underlying problem, and that the money involved in expanding police and prison responses would be better spent addressing chronic underfunding in schools themselves. The state government has not yet indicated whether it will take up the union's specific proposal, though workplace violence against teachers has become an increasingly prominent issue in broader public debate over classroom safety this year.

And on the Gold Coast, a Sunshine Coast tattoo artist is behind this year's AFLW Pride Round guernsey for the Gold Coast Suns, designed in collaboration with Suns players Katie Lynch and Lily Mithen. Artist Mady Peters, who works under the name Pokey Pokes, built the design around a football boot, a carabiner, and a cartoon dolphin holding a non-binary flag, with each element chosen to reflect different parts of queer community history and identity within women's football. Peters said the only real brief she was given for this year's design was to make it fun, a lighter approach than some past Pride guernseys that leaned more heavily on formal symbolism. The guernsey has already been worn once this season in a match against Adelaide, and has drawn warm reactions from fans and LGBTQIA+ community groups who've praised the collaboration between the club and a local artist for giving the design genuine community roots rather than a corporate feel. The club says Pride Round continues to be one of the most popular fixtures on its AFLW calendar each year, both for the on-field contest and the broader conversation it opens up around inclusion in sport.
