Good afternoon, and welcome to your Afternoon Edition for Tuesday, the 3rd of November, 2026. Here's what's been making news today in technology, across Australia, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

In technology, chip maker Marvell has agreed to acquire optical networking company Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at around one hundred and fifteen billion dollars, in what's being described as one of the largest technology mergers in history. Under the terms of the agreement, Lumentum shareholders will receive four-and-a-half Marvell shares for every share they currently hold, representing a premium of close to twenty-four per cent over where both companies were trading before deal speculation first emerged. Once completed, Marvell's existing shareholders will own roughly two-thirds of the combined company, with Lumentum shareholders holding the remaining third, and the two businesses are pitching the merger as creating a category leader in the optical and connectivity hardware that underpins modern AI computing infrastructure. The companies say they expect to generate more than a billion dollars a year in cost savings once the merger is fully integrated, though the deal still requires shareholder and regulatory approval and isn't expected to close until sometime in the first half of 2028. Investors reacted unusually negatively to the announcement, with Marvell shares falling around thirteen per cent and Lumentum shares dropping nearly nineteen per cent on the day, as markets weighed concerns about shareholder dilution and the financing risk associated with a transaction of this size.

Turning to national news, the Reserve Bank has lifted interest rates for the fifth time this year, taking the cash rate up by a quarter of a percentage point to four-point-eight-five per cent, its highest level in roughly eighteen years. Today's decision wasn't unanimous, with the central bank's board splitting six votes in favour of the increase to three votes in favour of holding rates steady, an unusually public display of disagreement among policymakers. In its statement explaining the decision, the Reserve Bank pointed to persistently elevated inflation and said further tightening in financial conditions was needed to support getting inflation back within its target range, while also flagging global risks including rising oil prices linked to tensions in the Middle East. The move was widely expected heading into today, with economists at all four of the country's major banks having predicted a rate rise, though the closeness of the board's internal vote suggests there's genuine disagreement within the Reserve Bank itself about how much further tightening is actually warranted. The central bank left the door open to potentially raising rates again in the months ahead, language that will do little to reassure mortgage holders already grappling with the cumulative impact of five rate rises this year layered on top of a housing market already experiencing its steepest downturn in decades.

In Queensland, the Bureau of Meteorology is warning of dangerous supercell thunderstorms developing across parts of central, eastern, and south-east Queensland today, capable of producing giant hailstones, destructive wind gusts, and even isolated tornado activity. The warning follows an overnight storm that dumped hailstones up to ten centimetres in diameter across the state's Fraser Coast region, causing significant damage in affected areas before today's fresh system began developing over inland parts of southern Queensland. A Bureau meteorologist specifically warned that today's supercells could generate wind gusts exceeding one hundred and twenty-five kilometres an hour alongside giant hail and the risk of flash flooding in affected catchments. Authorities are urging residents across the warning zone to move vehicles under cover, avoid sheltering under trees, and stay well away from windows during the most intense phase of any storm cell passing through their area. The warning adds to what's already been an unusually active and damaging start to the storm season across south-east Queensland, with authorities continuing to monitor conditions closely as the system tracks across the region through the remainder of today and into this evening.

And on the Gold Coast, new property data shows local dwelling prices have fallen three-point-six per cent over just the past three months, pushing the median property value across the city down to around one-point-one-six million dollars. The decline mirrors a broader downturn affecting property markets right across the country, as cumulative interest rate rises throughout the year continue weighing heavily on buyer demand and borrowing capacity in markets that had previously seen strong price growth. Property analysts say the Gold Coast's fall over the past quarter has been sharper than the declines recorded in several other major markets over the same period, reflecting just how exposed the city's property values had become after years of rapid price appreciation driven partly by interstate buyers during the pandemic years. Despite the recent pullback, most existing Gold Coast homeowners still retain substantial equity gains built up over that earlier period of rapid growth, even as the current downturn makes entering the market somewhat more achievable for prospective first-home buyers watching prices ease. Local real estate agents say buyer inquiry levels have remained relatively steady despite the price falls, suggesting the market adjustment reflects affordability pressure from higher borrowing costs rather than any broader loss of confidence in the city's long-term property appeal.
