First, technology and AI. Google has confirmed that its research effort to test AI compute in orbit, known as Project Suncatcher, is moving ahead, with a prototype satellite carrying four of the company's tensor processing units set to launch on the first of October aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. The mission, developed in partnership with satellite imaging company Planet, will run on roughly a kilowatt of solar power in low Earth orbit and is designed purely to gather data on how the chips cope with the vibration of launch and the radiation and thermal extremes of space, rather than to demonstrate a working data centre in orbit. Google says the test is an early but genuine step toward exploring whether space-based computing could one day supplement the enormous, power-hungry data centres currently being built on the ground to train and run AI models, with further prototype satellites already planned for launch in 2027 if this first test goes well. Industry observers say the idea remains highly speculative for now, but note that the sheer scale of energy demand from AI is pushing even the most established tech giants to consider unconventional options that would have seemed like science fiction only a few years ago.
