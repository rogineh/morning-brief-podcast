In technology, Swiss robotics company Embodied AI has unveiled a new factory robot called AIR, designed to work directly alongside human staff on production lines without needing the safety cages that typically separate industrial robots from workers. The robot runs on a wheeled base rather than legs, giving it stable mobility around a factory floor, while its two tendon-driven arms allow it to handle delicate tasks like cable assembly and component handling with a level of dexterity the company says approaches human performance. Rather than being pre-programmed for each specific task, AIR starts out under direct human teleoperation, with every session recorded and fed into the company's own learning system until the robot can eventually perform the task independently, typically within about a month of training. The company says early deployment with a consumer electronics manufacturer has shown strong reliability on cable-handling tasks specifically, though independent verification of those performance figures remains limited at this stage. Robotics industry watchers say the approach reflects a broader shift in how manufacturers are thinking about automation, moving away from robots that require extensive upfront programming toward systems that can learn directly from watching human workers perform a task, potentially making automation far more accessible to smaller manufacturers who can't justify the cost of traditional industrial robotics setups.

Turning to national news, the consumer watchdog is renewing its warnings about shopping scams as Australians head into the Black Friday sales period and the broader lead-up to Christmas, with scammers increasingly setting up convincing fake online storefronts designed to look like legitimate retailers. Figures from the national scam reporting service show buying and selling scams have consistently made up the single largest category of financial-loss reports throughout this year, with the proportion only expected to climb further as shopping activity intensifies over the coming weeks. Authorities say common tactics include fake retailer websites offering heavily discounted goods that never arrive, as well as scammers impersonating delivery services or payment providers to trick shoppers into handing over personal or financial details after a purchase. A newer variant authorities have flagged involves fraudulent callback scams that mimic official notifications from well-known payment platforms, designed to lure victims into calling a fake support number where they're then talked into providing sensitive account access. Consumer groups are urging shoppers to stick to well-known retailers where possible, check for secure payment options before entering card details, and treat unusually large discounts with a healthy dose of scepticism heading into what's traditionally the busiest and most scam-prone shopping period of the year.

In Queensland, a major new biomanufacturing facility has opened in south Brisbane, giving local researchers and companies access to specialised manufacturing space needed to move promising medical therapies from the laboratory toward actual clinical trials. The facility, built and operated by the Translational Research Institute with significant backing from the state government, adds roughly seven thousand square metres of additional space across four floors, including cleanrooms built to the pharmaceutical industry's strict manufacturing standards. Global pharmaceutical giant Sanofi has signed on as the facility's first major tenant, a significant vote of confidence from an international player in Queensland's growing life sciences sector and its capacity to support advanced biomanufacturing work locally. Industry figures say facilities of this kind have historically been in short supply across Australia, forcing many promising local research projects to either stall or relocate overseas in order to access the specialised manufacturing capability needed to progress toward human trials. State officials say the investment reflects a broader strategy of building out Queensland's life sciences and advanced manufacturing capabilities, positioning Brisbane as a more serious contender for biotechnology investment and skilled jobs in a sector that's grown rapidly in significance both nationally and globally in recent years.

And on the Gold Coast, the state government has formally opened the expression-of-interest process for the next stage of the city's light rail network, a roughly three-and-a-half-kilometre extension that would connect the Gold Coast University Hospital precinct in Southport through to the Harbour Town shopping and entertainment precinct in Biggera Waters. The proposed route would run along Olsen Avenue and Oxley Drive, adding at least three new stations along the way, along with a new public transport interchange at Harbour Town itself and upgrades to the existing light rail depot in Southport. Interested parties have until mid-December to register their interest, after which the project moves into a more detailed tender process, with the extension intended to be operational in time for the 2032 Brisbane Olympic and Paralympic Games. Local property developers have welcomed the announcement, with several describing the extension as transformative infrastructure for the growing northern end of the Gold Coast, an area that's seen substantial residential development in recent years without matching public transport investment to date. Transport planners say the project forms a key piece of the broader light rail network's long-term expansion, continuing to link major activity centres across the city as population growth along the coastal corridor continues at pace.
