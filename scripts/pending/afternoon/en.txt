In technology, Apple has reclaimed the title of the world's most valuable publicly listed company, overtaking Nvidia after closing Friday with a market valuation of roughly five-point-oh-three trillion dollars compared to Nvidia's four-point-nine-three trillion. Analysts say the shift reflects growing investor nervousness about the sheer scale of capital spending major technology companies have committed to artificial intelligence infrastructure, with Nvidia particularly exposed given how directly its business depends on continued heavy investment in AI computing hardware by its largest customers. Apple, by contrast, has actually reduced its own capital expenditure over each of the past three quarters rather than ramping it up, a markedly more conservative approach that some investors appear to be rewarding as broader anxiety builds over whether the AI spending boom can deliver the returns companies are promising. Nvidia's own chief executive has projected that the five largest cloud computing companies alone will spend close to eight hundred billion dollars on AI infrastructure this year, rising to one-point-three trillion dollars in 2027, numbers that illustrate just how much of the technology sector's near-term fortunes now hinge on that spending continuing uninterrupted. Market watchers say the two companies' rankings could easily flip back and forth again in the weeks ahead, given how closely matched their valuations currently are and how sensitive both remain to shifting sentiment around artificial intelligence spending more broadly.

Turning to national news, the federal government has confirmed it won't appeal a Federal Court decision that overturned an Aboriginal cultural heritage order which had stalled construction of a billion-dollar gold mine in central-west New South Wales. A judge found there had been fundamental procedural errors in the original twenty-twenty-four decision to grant heritage protection over the mine site, a ruling that effectively cleared the way for the project to proceed after it had been held up for roughly two years. The federal Environment Minister says the government will instead focus on redetermining the underlying heritage protection question through a fresh process, noting that at least two new Aboriginal heritage protection applications relating to the same site have already been lodged since the court's decision came down. The minister has been sharply critical of the existing cultural heritage framework more broadly, arguing it currently isn't doing an adequate job of protecting culturally significant sites, and the government has separately flagged broader reforms to modernise the laws governing how such heritage claims are assessed and protected going forward. The mining company behind the project had argued the original heritage declaration made the entire development unviable, while the dispute has reignited broader debate about how Australia balances resource development against the protection of Indigenous cultural heritage.

In Queensland, the state's Land Court has recommended approval for a proposed one-billion-dollar coal mine in the Bowen Basin, rejecting a challenge brought by two conservation groups after a hearing that stretched across seven weeks. The court found in favour of the mining company's plans for the open-cut operation, which is expected to produce around seventeen million tonnes of coal annually once fully operational, though it did impose an additional condition requiring further ecological surveys of threatened species before any construction work can begin. Today's recommendation isn't the final word on the project, with Queensland's state government still needing to formally decide whether the mine proceeds to the next stage of approval. The conservation groups that brought the original challenge say they're now weighing up their options for what comes next, having argued throughout the lengthy hearing that the project posed unacceptable risks to local ecosystems and threatened wildlife populations in the surrounding area. The decision adds to an already busy year for coal project approvals and disputes across Queensland's major coal-producing regions, as the state continues to balance the economic benefits of resource extraction against mounting environmental and community concerns.

And on the Gold Coast, the Suns capped off their AFLW home-and-away season with a dramatic come-from-behind win over the Giants, but the victory wasn't quite enough to secure the club a spot in the competition's top four finals positions. Gold Coast trailed for much of the contest before a late surge, including a crucial goal from Charlie Rowbottom in the final term, lifted them to a nine-point win on the road. Despite claiming their ninth victory of the season, the Suns ultimately missed out on fourth spot and the double chance that comes with it by just two-tenths of a percentage point, with Carlton securing that position instead after their own win the day before. The result means Gold Coast's finals campaign will now begin from a lower seeding than the club might have hoped for after such a promising season, even as today's win provided some solace and a strong send-off for the home-and-away phase of the year. For a club that only secured its first finals berth since 2023 earlier this season, the narrow margin separating fourth and fifth position will likely sting, even as attention now turns to preparing for the finals series itself in the weeks ahead.
