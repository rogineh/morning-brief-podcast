First, technology and AI. The chief executives of six of the world's most powerful AI companies have signed a voluntary safety accord with President Trump at the White House, committing to stronger internal safeguards around the development of frontier artificial intelligence systems. The document, titled the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, was signed by the leaders of OpenAI, Google, Meta, Nvidia, xAI, and Anthropic, and calls on each company to establish internal safety controls, submit those measures to independent audits, and give their own boards greater oversight of safety and security decisions. The accord is entirely voluntary, setting no specific implementation deadlines and no formal consequences if a company falls short of its commitments, which critics say limits its practical impact even as supporters frame it as an important signal that the industry's biggest players are willing to publicly commit to shared safety principles. Analysts say the real test will be whether the companies follow through with genuine independent audits and board-level scrutiny, or whether the accord ends up functioning mainly as a public relations exercise at a moment when AI safety concerns have been mounting across the industry.
