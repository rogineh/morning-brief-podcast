Now to national news here in Australia. Greens senator Larissa Waters has revealed she stepped down as the party's leader this week because of worsening kidney disease, a condition she said had not been publicly known until her health took a sudden downturn. In a statement, Waters said she'd been managing the illness for some time but that doctors had made clear her recovery would need a slower pace than leading a major party allows, adding that her prognosis is good but that she'll remain on medical leave rather than return to the role, while staying on in the Senate. The revelation adds a personal dimension to this week's leadership change, which saw New South Wales senator David Shoebridge elected in her place. Separately, authorities in New South Wales have ramped up their crackdown on illegal electric bikes, with twenty-three non-compliant e-bikes seized under new police powers destroyed and turned into scrap metal at a wrecking facility in Sydney's west this week. Police are now using portable testing units that can measure a bike's power output and speed cut-off on the spot, with any e-bike that doesn't cut its motor at twenty-five kilometres an hour, or that allows throttle-only riding above six kilometres an hour, now liable to be confiscated and destroyed, an approach Queensland adopted even earlier this year with similarly tough penalties of its own.
