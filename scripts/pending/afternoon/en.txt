In a significant moment for the AI industry, the heads of several of the world's biggest technology companies have signed a voluntary accord on artificial intelligence safety. Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Google's Sundar Pichai, and OpenAI's Greg Brockman put their names to a one-page agreement committing their companies to internal testing, outside audits, and board-level reviews of their most powerful AI systems. President Trump described the accord as "morally binding," while Senator Mark Warner said he wants Congress to go further and legislate mandatory testing and incident reporting rather than relying on a voluntary pledge. The announcement came as the Federal Trade Commission confirmed it's investigating OpenAI, Anthropic, and other major AI developers, and that it plans to compel senior executives from those companies to testify as part of its inquiry.

Separately, at its annual developer conference, OpenAI unveiled a new product called "dots," described as always-on AI agents that each get their own cloud computer and the ability to reach into more than four thousand different apps on a user's behalf, available initially to subscribers on its Pro and Business Premium plans. The company also quietly rolled out a new model, GPT-6.1 Sol, which it says delivers results close to its shelved Astra model but at around a fifth of the cost. Competitors weren't far behind, with a rival agent platform called Manus launching its own upgrade, and Meta expanding its Muse AI tools to small businesses with a new enterprise-focused platform.

Turning now to news from across Australia.

In a welcome piece of conservation news, the Australian Wildlife Conservancy has announced the purchase of two hundred hectares on the north coast of King Island, off the Tasmanian coast, to establish what's being called the Boulder Wildlife Sanctuary. Local conservationists have described the purchase as an enormous relief, saying it secures a significant stretch of habitat for native species on the island from future development.

Elsewhere, there's a less cheerful story out of the New South Wales Riverina, where the historic Jerilderie saleyard is set to be decommissioned despite sheep prices sitting at record highs. Murrumbidgee Council had offered the facility's twenty-year lease for just one dollar a year, but failed to find any operator willing to take it on, a reminder of how even strong commodity prices haven't been enough to keep some smaller regional saleyards viable.

Now, the news from here in Queensland.

The Bureau of Meteorology has issued its outlook for the coming cyclone season, warning that a severe cyclone is likely to make landfall in Queensland at some point this season. The bureau is also forecasting warmer than average temperatures across large parts of the state, combined with below average rainfall through until December, a combination authorities say could add extra pressure on bushfire risk in the lead-up to summer.

In governance news, the state's Office of the Information Commissioner says data breaches across Queensland's public sector are on the rise, with officials confirming that some of the incidents were malicious and intentional rather than accidental. The office says it's working with affected agencies to improve how sensitive information is stored and secured, and is urging all public sector bodies to review their data-handling practices.

And finally, here's what's happening on the Gold Coast.

The Suns are facing a looming deadline in the AFL trade period, after Hawthorn lodged an official offer to prised-after forward Ben King worth nine million dollars over seven years. Gold Coast now has until four fifteen on Friday afternoon to decide whether it will match the offer and keep King at the club, or allow him to move to Hawthorn as a free agent. The club's call on King is shaping up as one of the most closely watched decisions of this year's trade period, with list managers across the competition also watching how it might affect other movements still in play elsewhere in the market.

Meanwhile, the City of Gold Coast is reminding residents to start preparing now for the severe weather season ahead, pointing to the Bureau of Meteorology's cyclone outlook as a timely reminder that disasters can strike with little warning. Council officers are encouraging households to check their emergency kits, review their family plans, and make sure they know their local evacuation routes well before any severe weather actually arrives.
