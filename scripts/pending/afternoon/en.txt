Good afternoon, and welcome to your Daily Brief for Wednesday, the 11th of November, 2026. Here's what's happening today in technology and AI, across Australia, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

In technology, Amazon has begun rolling out a text-message version of its Alexa for Shopping assistant, allowing customers to search for products, fill their carts, and place orders simply by exchanging text messages with the artificial intelligence system. The feature started reaching a select group of American customers by invitation earlier this month, with the company planning to extend access to millions more shoppers over the coming weeks as the rollout widens. For grocery shoppers specifically, the assistant can assemble a full weekly order, identify ingredients directly from a photograph of a recipe, and add the relevant products straight into an existing cart without further manual input. Amazon says more than four hundred million customers have used some version of Alexa for Shopping over the past year, and the company reports adoption of its broader agentic shopping features has grown more than eightfold since the start of this year alone. The expansion comes just ahead of the Black Friday and Cyber Monday shopping period, which begins later this month, suggesting the company is positioning its AI shopping tools to play a significantly larger role in this year's peak retail season than in previous years.

Turning to national news, supermarket giant Coles is pushing back against the competition regulator's decision to block one of its proposed acquisitions, taking the matter to the Australian Competition Tribunal in what's being watched closely as an early test of the country's newly strengthened merger rules. The dispute centres on a proposed leasehold acquisition of a supermarket and liquor site in the Western Australian mining town of Kalgoorlie-Boulder, which the competition watchdog moved to block using mandatory merger notification powers that only came into effect earlier this year. It's understood to be the first time those particular powers have actually been used to stop a major supermarket chain from proceeding with a deal. Coles has argued before the tribunal that the regulator's decision risks overriding consumer choice in a regional market that could otherwise benefit from additional competition, while the watchdog maintains its original concerns about market concentration remain well founded. The case comes against a backdrop of continuing scrutiny of the major supermarket chains more broadly, including a separate ongoing court action over allegedly misleading discount promotions that both Coles and Woolworths are contesting.

In Queensland, the state's water authority has released its latest storage figures, showing south-east Queensland's major dams sitting at comfortably healthy levels heading into the warmer months, even as several smaller catchments elsewhere in the state continue to need attention. The most recent snapshot shows Wivenhoe Dam, the region's largest water storage, sitting above eighty per cent capacity, with nearby Somerset Dam recording a similarly solid result, while North Pine Dam, also known as Lake Samsonvale, remains comparatively lower at around half full. The figures come as the state continues working through a five hundred and forty-five million dollar investment program aimed at upgrading and repairing dam infrastructure across Queensland, with work currently focused on North Pine and Lake Macdonald, both of which are being held at reduced operating levels while that work proceeds. Water authorities say the broadly positive picture across the bulk of the south-east's supply network provides a reasonable buffer heading into the state's traditionally higher-risk period for both heatwaves and prolonged dry spells. Officials have nonetheless urged residents not to become complacent about water use, noting that storage levels can shift considerably depending on how the coming wet season actually unfolds.

And on the Gold Coast, hospitality group Hyatt is preparing to open its first Andaz-branded hotel in Australia, with the two-hundred-and-two-room property taking shape within the Star Gold Coast precinct at Broadbeach. Details of the hotel's dining and drinking venues were revealed earlier this month, with a restaurant called Nove set to serve as the property's signature dining destination, drawing on Mediterranean influences and local Australian ingredients built around open-fire cooking techniques. The hotel will also feature a more casual all-day venue combining coffee, matcha, and bakery offerings, alongside a dedicated bar space designed to anchor the property's social scene. The addition of an internationally recognised hotel brand new to the Australian market is being seen locally as a further sign of confidence in the Gold Coast's accommodation sector, which has continued attracting fresh investment despite broader cost pressures affecting the construction and hospitality industries more generally. Tourism figures say a genuinely new brand entering the market, rather than simply another locally-run property, helps lift the city's profile among international travellers who may already be familiar with the Andaz name from other destinations around the world.
