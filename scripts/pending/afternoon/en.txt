In technology, a fresh legal battle over how artificial intelligence companies source their training data has emerged this week, after the New York Times and three Tribune Publishing newspapers filed suit against the browser maker Brave Software in federal court in Manhattan. The lawsuits allege that Brave built a search index covering more than thirty billion webpages using a previously unidentified web crawler, leaving publishers unable to detect or block the activity, and then sold access to that index to a range of artificial intelligence companies. Court filings name several AI firms as recipients of the scraped content, including Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI, and Cohere, though none of those companies have themselves been named as defendants in the case, and the allegations have not been tested in court. One widely cited detail from the filings points to a Brave blog post suggesting Anthropic used the company's search product while training an earlier version of its Claude model, though that claim comes from a source cited within the complaint rather than from any independent verification. The case adds to a growing list of legal disputes between traditional publishers and the AI industry over how training data is gathered, following a separate copyright case filed by News Corporation against Brave earlier this year over similar allegations involving the Wall Street Journal and New York Post.

Turning to national news, Australians are being reminded that Remembrance Day falls this Wednesday, the eleventh of November, marking the moment the guns fell silent on the Western Front in 1918. The Royal Australian Services League's annual Poppy Appeal is running throughout the month, raising funds to support veterans and their families through practical assistance and welfare services. Commemorative services are being planned in cities and towns right across the country, with most observing the traditional minute of silence at eleven o'clock in the morning, the exact time the armistice took effect more than a century ago. Unlike Anzac Day, Remembrance Day is not a public holiday in most states, meaning many workplaces will simply pause briefly for quiet reflection before the working day resumes. Veterans' organisations say the appeal remains a significant source of year-round funding, and they're encouraging Australians to wear a poppy in the lead-up to Wednesday as a visible show of support for current and former service personnel.

In Queensland, authorities are continuing to remind residents that the state's severe weather season is now well and truly underway, with the Bureau of Meteorology's outlook running from October through to April typically bringing a heightened risk of cyclones, flooding, and extreme heat across the state. Forecasters have been describing the coming months as a potential "triple threat" season, with conditions shaped by a strong El Nino pattern and unusually warm global temperatures contributing to the risk profile. South-east Queensland has already experienced a run of severe thunderstorm activity in recent weeks, while northern parts of the state have been grappling with early and intense heatwave conditions well ahead of the traditional peak of summer. Emergency services are using the current relatively calm stretch to encourage households to review their disaster plans and emergency kits, noting that November is traditionally regarded as one of the more volatile months for severe weather developing rapidly along the state's coastline.

And on the Gold Coast, the owner of Dreamworld has unveiled ambitious plans for a major hotel and entertainment precinct at the theme park's Coomera site, in a move aimed at transforming the attraction into a genuine overnight tourism destination rather than a single-day visit. Coast Entertainment Holdings has outlined a hotel proposal valued at roughly two hundred million dollars, alongside a separate entertainment, dining, and retail precinct estimated at around sixty million dollars that would be open to visitors regardless of whether they hold a park ticket. The announcement follows the Queensland government's earlier approval of a masterplan covering more than fifty hectares of land at the Coomera site, after which the company commissioned an independent valuation of the broader Dreamworld business and engaged financial advisers to review its funding and capital options. The company says the project reflects strong recent visitation trends at Dreamworld and its neighbouring water park, with ticket sales reported to be up significantly over the past financial year. Local tourism figures have welcomed the proposal, saying a genuine resort-style offering at the site could help extend the average length of stay for visitors travelling to the Gold Coast specifically for theme park holidays.
