In technology, chipmaker Marvell has agreed to acquire optical components manufacturer Lumentum in an all-stock transaction valuing the combined deal at an implied enterprise figure of around one hundred and fifteen billion dollars, in one of the largest moves yet to consolidate the infrastructure underpinning artificial intelligence data centres. The agreement values Lumentum at close to a twenty-four per cent premium over its share price before the deal became public, reflecting strong investor appetite for companies supplying the optical hardware that allows enormous volumes of data to move quickly between AI computing clusters. Lumentum brings expertise in lasers, optical transceivers, and optical circuit switching to the combination, complementing Marvell's existing electrical and optical interconnect and custom silicon businesses. Once the deal closes, expected in the first half of 2028 subject to shareholder and regulatory approval, existing Marvell shareholders will hold roughly two-thirds of the combined company, with Lumentum shareholders holding the remainder, and Marvell chief executive Matt Murphy will continue leading the business as chairman and chief executive. Company executives have described the tie-up as expanding their combined addressable market substantially, though some analysts have flagged the deal could complicate Marvell's relationships with other customers who compete with Lumentum in certain product categories.

Turning to national news, Qantas has told the market that demand across its network remains resilient heading into the busy summer travel period, even as the airline continues to grapple with persistently elevated fuel costs weighing on its bottom line. The carrier's latest market update, released last week, pointed to steady booking volumes across both domestic and international routes, despite the broader cost pressures being felt by households and businesses alike following this year's run of interest rate rises. In separate but related news, Qantas confirmed plans to launch a new direct service between Sydney and Cape Town, with that route expected to commence in October of 2027, extending the airline's long-haul international network further into southern Africa. The announcement comes as Qantas continues adjusting its broader international timetable, including resuming a seasonal service between Auckland and Adelaide to meet peak summer demand. Analysts say the combination of resilient passenger demand and selective network expansion suggests the airline remains confident in underlying travel appetite, even as it continues managing the same fuel and cost pressures affecting carriers worldwide.

In Queensland, winners from last month's Premier's Export Awards are now preparing to represent the state at the national Australian Export Awards, after a ceremony in Brisbane recognised businesses spanning medical technology, agriculture, and the creative industries. Medical device manufacturer Cook Medical was named Queensland's overall Exporter of the Year, while Brisbane-based avocado grower Green Skin Avocados took out the agribusiness, food and beverages category and Sunshine Coast firm Infravision was recognised among the award's national category winners. Brisbane-based Gameloft and Emesent also picked up category wins spanning the creative, lifestyle and design sector and digital and smart technologies respectively, while a family-scale Wagyu beef supply chain operating out of central Queensland claimed the regional exporter title. Organisers say the breadth of industries represented among this year's winners reflects the diversity of the state's export base beyond its traditional resources and agriculture sectors. The Queensland winners will now compete nationally, with the Australian Export Awards expected to showcase the state's strongest performers alongside exporters from right across the country.

And on the Gold Coast, the Suns are working through a tight squeeze on their player list as they finalise their roster ahead of the AFL draft later this month, after a trade period that brought several significant names to the club. Collingwood premiership veteran Scott Pendlebury moved to the Suns on the opening day of trading, while Hawthorn pair Aidan Schubert and Sam Butler also made the switch to the Gold Coast in a separate deal that saw draft selections change hands between the two clubs. The club has delisted both Jamarra Ugle-Hagan and Elliott Himmelberg ahead of the formal lodgement deadline, but has committed to bringing both players back through the draft itself, which is scheduled for the nineteenth and twentieth of November. Gold Coast currently holds selections including picks eight, eighty-one, eighty-five and one hundred and three heading into draft night, giving list managers some flexibility as they work to finalise a squad that balances the arrival of experienced recruits with continuity from the existing playing group. Fans will have to wait until later in the month to see the club's list fully locked in, with Ugle-Hagan's return in particular expected to be formally confirmed once his redraft is completed on the twentieth.
