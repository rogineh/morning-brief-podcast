Turning now to news from across Australia.

The federal government's follow-up legislation strengthening the under sixteen social media ban is now before a Senate committee, with the bill proposing to double the maximum fines platforms face for non-compliance and expand the powers of the online safety regulator. The original ban, which took effect late last year, requires major platforms to block younger users or face significant penalties, and the government says early compliance data suggests most platforms have made genuine efforts to implement the restrictions, even as questions remain about how consistently the rules are being enforced in practice. Child safety advocates have welcomed the push for tougher penalties, arguing that fines need to be large enough to meaningfully change platform behaviour rather than being treated as a minor cost of doing business. Some technology and civil liberties groups continue to raise concerns about enforcement methods and unintended consequences, including the possibility that determined teenagers simply migrate to platforms outside the scope of the ban rather than reducing their overall social media use. The eSafety Commissioner's office says it's continuing to monitor compliance data closely and will use its existing powers against platforms found to be falling short, while stressing that the expanded powers in the new bill would give it considerably more capacity to act quickly against future non-compliance.

Now, the news from here in Queensland.

The state government has opened a new round of expressions of interest for resource exploration, inviting companies to nominate areas of interest for petroleum, gas, coal, and other mineral projects as part of a broader push to rebuild the state's project pipeline. Resources Minister Dale Last says the move is designed to kickstart new investment and exploration activity after what the government describes as a period of reduced interest under the previous administration's policies. Industry groups have welcomed the release, saying a healthy pipeline of exploration projects today is essential to sustaining Queensland's resources sector over the coming decade, while environmental groups have urged the government to ensure new exploration areas are subject to rigorous assessment, particularly in regions with sensitive ecosystems or existing water allocation pressures. The government says any successful nominations will still need to clear the usual environmental and land access approval processes before exploration can actually begin, and that opening the expressions of interest round is only the first step in what's typically a multi-year process from nomination through to any eventual production decision.

And finally, here's what's happening on the Gold Coast.

A ten million dollar renovation of a historic beachfront building in Burleigh Heads is nearing completion, with the site set to reopen in December as a new restaurant and bar called the Burleigh Pavilion. The project forms part of a broader wave of hospitality investment along the Gold Coast's southern beaches, where operators say strong visitor numbers continue to support new openings even as the industry grapples with the city's notoriously seasonal demand patterns. Local hospitality groups say the Gold Coast's deep swings between peak holiday periods and quieter months remain one of the toughest challenges facing restaurateurs anywhere in the country, but they say ambitious new openings like this one suggest confidence in the market's long-term trajectory remains strong, particularly in well-located beachfront precincts. The developers say they've deliberately retained much of the original building's heritage character rather than demolishing it outright, betting that a point of difference from the city's newer high-rise developments will help the venue stand out once it opens its doors.
