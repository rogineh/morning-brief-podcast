Good afternoon, and welcome to your Daily Brief for Tuesday, the 29th of September, 2026. Here's what's happening today across technology, the nation, Queensland, and the Gold Coast.

First, technology and AI. Chip maker AMD has announced an eight point two billion dollar all-stock deal to acquire World Labs, the AI startup founded by renowned computer scientist Fei-Fei Li, in a move aimed at giving the company a bigger foothold in so-called physical AI, the branch of artificial intelligence focused on helping machines understand and navigate the real world. As part of the deal, expected to close by the end of the year subject to regulatory approval, Li will join AMD as executive vice president and chief scientist, reporting directly to chief executive Lisa Su, in what ranks as the chip maker's second-largest acquisition on record. Separately, President Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson hosted six of the industry's most powerful chief executives at the White House today, including the bosses of Meta, Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Palantir, and Nvidia, for talks aimed at striking a balance between encouraging innovation and putting sensible guardrails around the technology's rapid growth. Speaker Johnson said the government wanted to back innovation while avoiding regulation so heavy it risked leaving American companies behind in the race with China. In a striking coincidence of timing, senior AI researchers from several of the very companies represented at that meeting also issued a joint statement today warning that unchecked AI automation could eventually pose serious risks to humanity if development continues without stronger, mandatory oversight of the most powerful labs.

Now to national news here in Australia. The Reserve Bank has lifted the cash rate by a quarter of a percentage point to four point six per cent, its fourth increase this year and the highest level interest rates have reached since November of 2011. The board's decision was unanimous, with all nine members voting in favour, as policymakers pointed to inflation still running above target, a broadening conflict in the Middle East pushing global energy prices higher than expected, and rapid AI-driven demand pushing up the cost of technology goods worldwide. The bank also flagged ongoing pressure on domestic production capacity, noting many businesses are either raising prices already or actively considering it. For households, the increase means yet another rise in mortgage repayments just as families look ahead to summer spending, with major lenders expected to pass the change on to variable rate borrowers within days. Economists say the size and unanimity of today's decision suggests the central bank is taking the current inflation picture seriously, even if it means further short-term pain for borrowers already stretched by a run of increases this year.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. The Queensland Police Service marked its largest graduation ceremony of the year today, welcoming one hundred and twenty-six new officers into its ranks at a passing-out parade held at the Brisbane Police Academy. The milestone comes just a day after National Police Remembrance Day services were held across the state, giving the new intake a poignant reminder of the responsibility they're taking on as they head out to stations across Queensland in the weeks ahead. Meanwhile, a south-easterly weather change is moving up the coast from New South Wales today, bringing scattered showers to the Gold Coast around lunchtime before pushing through Brisbane into the afternoon and reaching the Sunshine Coast by evening, a timely reminder for anyone with outdoor plans this week to keep an eye on the forecast. Police have also confirmed a woman has been charged following the death of a man at Fernvale, west of Brisbane, at the weekend, with investigations continuing into the circumstances surrounding the incident.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. Anglers from across the country are converging on the Gold Coast this week for the Flathead Classic, Australia's premier catch-and-release fishing tournament, run out of the Gold Coast Sportfishing Club's northern pavilion on Marine Parade. Competing teams of two or three anglers are spending four days chasing flathead using only artificial lures or flies, with every fish measured, photographed, and released alive under the tournament's strict sustainability rules. Organisers say the event has grown into one of the most significant fishing competitions on the Australian calendar, drawing families and serious competitors alike to the Gold Coast's waterways each spring, while showcasing the region's fishing grounds to a national audience. Local tourism operators say the influx of visiting anglers provides a welcome boost to accommodation and hospitality businesses during what's traditionally a quieter stretch between the winter and peak summer seasons.
