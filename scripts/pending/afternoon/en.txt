In technology, OpenAI has announced a new partnership with cybersecurity firm Hacktron aimed at uncovering vulnerabilities in how its systems connect with third-party services and integrations. The arrangement expands Hacktron's access to OpenAI's frontier models through the company's existing red-teaming program, allowing the security firm to probe more deeply for weaknesses than would typically be possible under a standard public bug bounty arrangement. Hacktron first came to OpenAI's attention through the company's regular security bug bounty scheme, having previously been paid for identifying an earlier vulnerability earlier this year, a track record that appears to have factored into the decision to formalise a closer working relationship. The move is being described as the latest in a string of security-focused initiatives OpenAI has rolled out in quick succession recently, as the company works to reassure enterprise customers and regulators that it takes the security of increasingly interconnected AI systems seriously. OpenAI has not disclosed specific financial terms of the arrangement, how long the partnership will run, or precisely how many vulnerabilities Hacktron has identified to date, though the company says findings will continue feeding directly into its broader efforts to harden its systems against exploitation.

Turning to national news, Australia's open banking framework has begun extending to non-bank lenders for the first time, with the largest providers now required to start sharing customer data under the Consumer Data Right from earlier this week. Lenders holding more than ten billion dollars in outstanding loans and leases became the first tranche of non-bank providers required to comply, joining the traditional banks that have participated in the scheme for several years already. A second, broader group of mid-sized lenders will be brought into the framework around the middle of next year, with authorities saying the staged approach is designed to let the sector adjust gradually rather than facing an abrupt, all-at-once compliance deadline. The expansion builds on an earlier requirement, already in place since the middle of this year, for eligible non-bank lenders to publish standard product information such as interest rates, fees, and eligibility criteria through the same data-sharing framework. Regulators say at least three dozen new data holders are expected to join the scheme as the rollout continues, a development consumer groups have broadly welcomed as giving borrowers more practical ability to compare loan products and potentially switch to better deals.

In Queensland, the state government has unveiled a new multi-year freight delivery plan containing around seventy individual actions aimed at improving the productivity, reliability, and safety of how goods move around the state. Among the headline measures is a new rail freight container incentive scheme designed to encourage more freight onto the rail network and off increasingly congested roads, alongside discounted diesel fuel access charges specifically targeted at users of the Mount Isa rail line in the state's north-west. The plan also includes a broader package of reforms to heavy vehicle access arrangements, which industry groups have long argued create unnecessary friction and delay for freight operators moving goods across different parts of the state. Government officials say the plan reflects extensive consultation with transport and logistics operators, mining companies, and regional communities who rely heavily on efficient freight connections to get their goods to market. Industry representatives have broadly welcomed the announcement, while noting that the real test will be how quickly individual measures within the plan are actually implemented on the ground over the coming months and years.

And on the Gold Coast, the Pan Pacific Masters Games are entering their final days, with organisers preparing for Saturday's closing ceremony after more than a week of competition across dozens of sports held throughout the city. Event organisers say participation numbers across individual sports have remained strong right through the Games' second week, building on the momentum established during the opening days of competition. Queensland's tourism sector will be watching closely to see whether this year's event can surpass the economic impact of the last comparable Games, which delivered close to twenty-nine million dollars in economic benefit to the state, itself a substantial increase on the edition held two years earlier. Local hospitality and accommodation operators say the extended, multi-sport nature of the event has helped spread visitor spending more evenly across the fortnight than a single marquee event typically would, with venues across the city continuing to report solid patronage from visiting competitors and their families right up until the final days. Organisers say they'll provide a fuller assessment of this year's turnout and economic contribution once the Games officially wrap up this weekend.
