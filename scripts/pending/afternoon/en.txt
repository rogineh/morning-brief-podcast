First, technology and AI. Shanghai AI Laboratory has quietly released Atria Dawn Preview, a free, seven hundred and forty-four billion parameter agentic AI model built on top of the GLM-5.2 foundation model, with no official blog post or press release accompanying its arrival online earlier this week. The model, which carries a permissive open licence, is designed specifically for long research and engineering workflows: analysing problems, using tools, writing code, running experiments, and recovering when a step fails partway through, backed by a two-hundred-and-fifty-six-thousand-token context window. A technical report with more than a hundred and forty listed authors followed just days later, offering the first full account of how the system was trained, an unusually detailed release for a lab that gave the model itself almost no fanfare. Meanwhile, a broader trend is becoming clear across the industry, with more companies now assigning AI systems narrow, specific jobs rather than general-purpose tasks, from checking security camera footage and helping farmers make sense of their records, to searching satellite imagery and preparing sales quotes, a shift analysts say reflects businesses becoming more comfortable trusting AI with well-defined, lower-risk pieces of work rather than handing over entire processes at once.

Now to national news here in Australia. Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke is set to unveil a long-delayed overhaul of the immigration system tomorrow, after twice postponing the announcement amid disagreement between ministers over competing priorities. Under the changes, international students would effectively be banned from bringing partners and children to Australia on secondary visas, part of a broader push to bring net overseas migration down from around three hundred thousand to two hundred and twenty-five thousand by 2028. The reforms are also expected to restrict student visa applications lodged from within Australia, tighten rules around students switching courses, crack down on asylum seeker work and appeal rights, limit how long backpackers can extend their stay, and stop visitors from applying for family visas altogether. Former immigration officials have already warned the unfinished plans carry real risk if the detail isn't carefully worked through before implementation. Separately, leaders of the Plymouth Brethren Christian Church are set to be hauled before a parliamentary inquiry over allegations the church paid influencers and coordinated volunteers to harass candidates and disrupt polling booths at last year's federal election, claims the church has strongly denied, while itself alleging its own members were the ones targeted by an organised harassment campaign. A parliamentary committee examining the conduct of last year's election has already found in an interim report that democracy faced what it called an assault from third-party groups active during the campaign.

Closer to home, here in Queensland. Brisbane ferry commuters are bracing for another four-hour service stoppage as industrial action by transport workers continues to disrupt the network, with no clear end in sight to the dispute. On a more forward-looking note, Brisbane City Council has committed thirty-eight million dollars toward early planning for future Metro connections reaching the airport, Carseldine, Capalaba, and Springwood, as the city looks to expand its rapid transit network well beyond its current routes. Police have also stepped up enforcement across the city for Queensland Road Safety Week, with a particular focus on speeding, drink and drug driving, and driver fatigue, as authorities look to bring down the state's road toll ahead of the busy spring and summer driving period.

And finally, your Gold Coast update. The Suns have confirmed key forward Ben King will leave the club as a restricted free agent at the end of the season, with the two-time leading goalkicker widely tipped to join Hawthorn once free agency officially opens on the second of October. As a restricted free agent, the Suns retain the right to match any rival offer or accept a compensation pick instead, with King having kicked three hundred and fifteen goals in a hundred and forty-three games since debuting for the club, cementing his status as one of the most prolific key forwards of his generation. And Gold Coast commuters are being warned of extended disruptions on the rail network from the nineteenth of September, when the Logan and Gold Coast Faster Rail project triggers track closures between Boggo Road and Varsity Lakes, with buses replacing trains on that stretch until the eleventh of October.
