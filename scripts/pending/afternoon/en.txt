In technology, Meta has continued rolling out its newer family of AI models, known as Muse, across several of the world's biggest cloud computing platforms, making them available to a much wider pool of developers and businesses beyond Meta's own products. The family includes a handful of different models built for different jobs, ranging from a lightweight, low-cost option designed for high-volume, everyday tasks through to a more capable model aimed at complex reasoning work. The models are now being distributed through major cloud platforms operated by some of Meta's biggest competitors, a strategy that marks a shift away from the company's earlier approach of promoting its Llama models primarily as open-weight downloads for developers to run themselves. Industry analysts say making the models available directly inside widely used cloud platforms removes a lot of the technical friction that previously stopped many businesses from experimenting with Meta's models in the first place. The move comes after a period of internal reorganisation within Meta's AI division, including leadership changes aimed at accelerating the pace of model development after earlier releases were delayed. Commentators say the broader distribution strategy reflects just how crowded the market for enterprise AI tools has become, with every major lab now competing not just on model quality but on how easily businesses can actually plug a given model into their existing systems.

Turning to national news, a new visa condition takes effect today for travellers applying for an Australian tourist visa, an electronic travel authority, or an eVisitor visa, with those applicants now automatically subject to a "no further stay" condition on arrival. The change means visitors arriving under those visa categories will generally no longer be able to apply to extend their stay or switch to a different visa type while still in the country, closing off a pathway that some visitors had previously used to remain in Australia for longer than their visa originally allowed. The update comes in the same month the government confirmed a broader overhaul of the working holiday maker program, which will introduce fresh caps on second and third-year visa extensions once a new registration and invitation process opens at the start of December. Tourism and hospitality groups have given the revised working holiday settings a cautious welcome, saying the final caps were less severe than an earlier proposal, even as some regional operators continue to argue the changes still represent a meaningful cut to the seasonal workforce many of them rely on. The government says the combined changes reflect its broader effort to tighten and simplify the visitor and working holiday visa systems, after years of criticism that the rules had become overly complex and, in some cases, open to misuse. Immigration lawyers say travellers already in Australia on affected visas should check their individual circumstances carefully, since the new condition applies from today for new arrivals rather than retrospectively.

In Queensland, the state's tourism industry gathered earlier this month to celebrate the best of the sector at the annual Queensland Tourism Awards, with gold medals handed out across twenty-nine categories spanning everything from ecotourism and cultural experiences through to major attractions, events, and accommodation. Among the headline winners, Cairns Convention Centre and the Eumundi Markets were both recognised with gold awards in their respective categories, reflecting the geographic spread of tourism excellence being celebrated from the far north down through the hinterland. Industry figures say the awards matter well beyond bragging rights, since gold winners in eligible categories go on to represent Queensland at the national Australian Tourism Awards, to be held in Geelong early next year. Tourism operators say an award like this can make a genuine difference commercially, often translating into a noticeable lift in bookings and public profile in the months that follow a win. The state's tourism body says this year's entries were as strong and competitive as any in recent memory, with judges noting the continued recovery and growth of the sector across both regional and metropolitan parts of the state. Organisers say the awards remain one of the best windows into just how varied Queensland's tourism offering has become, well beyond its more obvious coastal drawcards.

And on the Gold Coast, GC Aqua Park claimed gold in the Major Tourist Attractions category at this year's Queensland Tourism Awards, adding a Hall of Fame recognition on top after securing gold honours in the same category for multiple years running. The inflatable water park, located within the Broadwater Parklands precinct at Southport, has become one of the city's most popular family attractions in recent years, drawing visitors with its floating obstacle course of slides, climbing structures, and bouncing platforms set directly on the Broadwater. Operators say the Hall of Fame recognition reflects sustained, consistent performance over several awards cycles rather than a single standout year, something they say is harder to achieve than a one-off win. Local tourism figures say the win is a timely boost heading into the busy summer holiday period, reinforcing the Gold Coast's reputation for family-friendly attractions at exactly the time of year when visitor numbers typically climb. The park's operators say they have no plans to rest on this year's success, with further enhancements to the attraction already being considered ahead of future seasons. City tourism leaders say wins like this help keep the spotlight on the breadth of things to do across the Gold Coast, beyond its beaches and nightlife.
