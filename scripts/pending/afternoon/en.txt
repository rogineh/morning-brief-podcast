In technology, Alphabet shares tumbled sharply after the company's third-quarter results, wiping out hundreds of billions of dollars in market value in a single trading day despite Google Cloud revenue more than doubling over the year. Cloud revenue jumped from fifteen-point-two billion dollars a year earlier to thirty-point-seven billion dollars this quarter, comfortably beating analyst expectations, and overall company revenue rose twenty-five per cent to a hundred and twenty-seven-point-six billion dollars. But investors focused instead on the company's ballooning capital spending, with Alphabet confirming it still plans to spend between a hundred and ninety-five and two hundred and five billion dollars this year on data centres and other infrastructure to support artificial intelligence, after third-quarter capital spending alone reached fifty-one-point-four billion dollars and pushed free cash flow into negative territory. The result marked the company's first miss on both revenue and profit expectations in nearly four years, with some analysts pointing to softer growth in the core search business as a sign that AI-powered search tools from rivals may be chipping away at Google's long-held dominance. One analyst summed up the market's reaction by noting that Google Cloud had done exactly what it was supposed to, adding that the real concern now lies with everything else around it. The sell-off reflects a broader nervousness spreading across markets about whether the enormous sums tech companies are ploughing into artificial intelligence infrastructure will ever generate returns to match.

Turning to national news, cryptocurrency exchange Binance says it's working to recover from what's being described as the largest crypto theft on record, after an attacker believed to be linked to North Korea drained billions of dollars from the platform's wallets in a matter of minutes. Investigators say the attacker exploited a compromised laptop belonging to one of Binance's own engineers to gain access to internal systems and push through unauthorised withdrawal transactions across ten different blockchains. Binance has since halted deposits and withdrawals while it works with blockchain partners to freeze affected funds, saying roughly three billion dollars worth of the stolen assets have already been frozen on-chain, with that figure expected to rise, and the company has pledged to cover any losses suffered by ordinary account holders. Threat intelligence researchers say the attack dwarfs the previous record crypto theft, a billion-and-a-half-dollar hack of another exchange last year, and fits a long pattern of state-linked hacking groups using sophisticated cyber intrusions to fund other activities. Despite the scale of the theft rattling cryptocurrency markets overnight, Australian share markets largely shrugged off the news today, with the benchmark index managing modest gains even as crypto-linked assets slid.

In Queensland, police say a two-day highway blitz across several major routes in the central part of the state has turned up more than two hundred speeding drivers and dozens of positive roadside drug and alcohol tests. Officers conducted intensive patrols across four highways, catching more than two hundred motorists exceeding the speed limit, while thirty-three drivers returned positive results for either roadside breath testing or drug testing during the same operation. Police say the patrols form part of an ongoing statewide road safety push aimed at reducing serious crashes on regional highways, which continue to account for a disproportionate share of the state's road toll compared with metropolitan roads. Authorities are urging motorists travelling long distances on regional highways to plan rest breaks, stick to posted speed limits, and never get behind the wheel after drinking or using drugs, warning that enforcement operations of this kind will continue in the lead-up to the busier summer holiday driving period.

And on the Gold Coast, tourism and transport authorities are gearing up for a bumper weekend as the city hosts a Rugby League World Cup triple-header at Cbus Super Stadium in Robina, with organisers hoping for a sell-out crowd across three matches. Saturday's schedule features both men's and women's fixtures, including a men's clash between New Zealand and Fiji, alongside two women's matches, with gates opening in the late morning and the final whistle expected in the evening. Ticket prices start from under twenty dollars for adults, and anyone holding a ticket gets free travel on Queensland Rail trains, buses, and trams for several hours either side of the matches, in a bid to ease pressure on roads around the stadium precinct. Local tourism operators say the tournament has already delivered a welcome boost to accommodation bookings and hospitality venues across the city this week, with the triple-header expected to draw visitors from across the country given the rare chance to watch multiple international teams on home soil in one day. Organisers are encouraging fans to arrive early and use public transport, with road closures and parking restrictions expected around the stadium for the duration of the event.
